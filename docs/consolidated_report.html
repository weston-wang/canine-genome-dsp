Every figure below is a model output for a regimen no dog with this disease has ever received. Where a number is assumed rather than measured, this report says so. Every treatment decision belongs with clinicians who can examine the animal.

Ten-year durability is not reached by killing this one tumour harder. It is reached in two moves: clear the tumour that is there, then keep a targeted pill in the background matched to the tumour's own driver fault, killing any new cell that carries it — so the next tumour never gets started.

The plan, in one line: an induction phase (a brain-penetrant oral chemotherapy plus three supporting drugs) that clears the tumour in roughly 4–6 months, then indefinite maintenance on a targeted pill chosen from the tumour's genotype.

The escape-coverage half of this is solid and independently reproduced. The ten-year half is a scientifically grounded hypothesis, not a result: it reduces to a single inequality that fails at the conservative access assumption and holds only at more optimistic, unmeasured values.

The answerHow ten-plus years is actually achieved

Every earlier version of this work chased "kill this tumour so hard it never comes back." That framing can never reach ten years, for a reason unrelated to how good the drug is: this breed is born predisposed to this cancer. Clearing one tumour does nothing about the inherited fault that caused it, so a second one can arise.

The unlock is that the predisposition and the tumour are the same genetic lesion. The cleanest case is deletion of MTAP (with its neighbour CDKN2A) — the most common genetic fault in canine histiocytic sarcoma, found across ~104 cases and shared by the breeds prone to it. It is in the inherited background, so it is in the brain tumour, the lung form, and any future tumour.

The two moves

1 — Induction (months). Clear the tumour that exists now. A brain-penetrant oral chemotherapy (a "mitotic poison," the drug class this cancer is measurably sensitive to) plus three supporting agents. Closes all eleven escape routes at both brain sites; below detectable in about two months, cleared in roughly 4–6. The induction is the same for every dog.

2 — Maintenance (indefinite). A single targeted pill, matched to the genotype, lethal only to cells carrying the driver lesion and sparing normal cells. It kills any lesion-carrying cell at emergence, before it becomes a tumour. Because normal tissue is spared by design, it is the rare cancer drug a dog could plausibly stay on for years.

That second move is what ten years is made of: not "this tumour never recurs" but "the next one never gets going." A cell that has deleted a gene cannot reroute around it — escaping would mean re-growing a locus it threw away, a reversal, not a mutation — which is why a maintenance drug aimed at a genotype (rather than a pathway, which a tumour escapes by rerouting) can plausibly hold for years.

Every genotypeA matched maintenance pill for all cases

MTAP deletion is the cleanest anchor, but it is a minority of tumours; the most common fault is a MAPK driver (SHP2/PTPN11 or KRAS, ~59% of cases). So the maintenance arm is a decision tree, keyed on one NGS panel read once from the tumour tissue. Every genotype gets a matched pill — what varies is the strength of the ten-year hold.

Why the grades differ — stated honestly

Only the MTAP tier is genotype-locked: a cell cannot reroute around a deleted gene. The dependency tiers (PTEN, CDKN2A) are strong but have known resistance routes. The MAPK majority's MEK block is reroutable in an established tumour — it works against a new primary because that starts as a single founding-driver cell before any reroute exists, so it leans on catching recurrences early. So "ten years for all cases" is honest as a clean lock for some genotypes and a surveillance-dependent hold for the majority — not one uniform guarantee.

Credited, not re-discovered

The MAPK branch is not new: the earliest analysis already listed MEK inhibitors as "works, but fails in the brain on access" and named SHP2/KRAS genotyping as a top next step. The genuinely new parts are the maintenance-at-emergence role and the CNS-penetrant candidate (mirdametinib), whose canine brain penetration is still unmeasured.

Where it isThe whole niche — both sites

This is a multi-site disease, and the analysis covers all of it. The same germline MTAP/CDKN2A lesion drives the breed's histiocytic sarcoma wherever it appears, so the target is the whole niche — lung/disseminated and brain — not one compartment. The three source reports treated lung as a co-equal primary site with its own regimen, and so does this one.

A discrete removable lump

Ruled out

Relied on for months in earlier work; one large series says this compartment does not exist in the dog brain. It was a mirage.

The breed's brain tumour is extra-axial — a discrete meningeal-surface mass, the most surgically accessible kind there is. Surgery removes the part where drugs could reach and leaves the parts where they cannot. Extracranial spread of primary CNS HS has never been reported, and dogs of this breed get predominantly confined tumours — so each site is a bounded target.

So "the answer" is not brain-only. The lung/disseminated form is the more tractable site — it closes with systemic drug alone — and the brain is the site that forced the local-delivery and CSF work. The one genuinely open durability gap on the antigen-lost side is a lung finding: the lung/no-antigen cell has no immune component, so it needs ~0.045/day of antigen-independent killing to endure once dosing stops (two internal parameterizations disagree 25× on how badly the unrescued lung cell degrades but agree the fix works). The MEK-inhibitor maintenance is likewise site-split: solid for a lung or disseminated second primary, conditional for a brain one.

The solid halfEscape coverage

An escape is a route by which some cells survive and regrow. Margin = how much faster treatment kills those cells than they regrow; above zero is closed. Figures are the induction regimen at conservative brain access, growth taken at 0.055/day.

All eleven escape routes close at both sites — tightest margin +0.11 (brain tissue) / +0.13 (linings). Routes: pathway restart above the block, target-site mutation, downstream activation, jump to the parallel pathway, lineage independence, antigen loss, survival-signal independence, iron-death (ferroptosis) resistance, self-recycling independence, and dormant survivors (+0.09 / +0.10, the tightest). MGMT repair "cannot apply" — this drug class leaves no damage for it to repair.

What "closed" is actually worth — an honest evidence grade

"Reproduced by the audit" means the arithmetic reproduces, not that the inputs are measured. Every margin is potency × access × duty − growth; almost every potency is a hand-set constant the code labels assumed, and the 0.055/day growth rate is an uncited placeholder — so "all escapes close" is largely definitional. Grading the twelve escapes by what each closure actually rests on (coverage_assessment): 1 measured in canine HS (premise contested), 2 transferred from the wrong disease (PI3K from hemangiosarcoma; HCQ from lymphoma), 2 structural/design arguments, and 7 resting on assumed, never-measured kill rates — including the load-bearing induction agent and the entire ten-year arm. The coverage claim is therefore a structural / hypothesis-level result, not an evidence-backed one — but a falsifiable one, cheapest test first (an MTAP stain). Two target-side assumptions have been removed: ERK2 (100%) and PI3Kα (99.81%) human-to-dog identity are now computed from real sequences in sequence_conservation.

The hypothesis halfThe ten-year durability

The escape coverage above is about the first tumour. Ten-year durability is a separate, weaker claim about the maintenance arm suppressing a second primary. It reduces to a single inequality:

The inequality it reduces to

potency · access − growth > 0

which fails at the module's own conservative brain-access assumption (0.30) and holds only at unmeasured values (≥ ~0.50). Several deciding numbers are assumed, not measured in any dog: the maintenance pill's potency and CNS access; the tumour's growth rate (~0.055/day — it enters the answer twice); the pill's marrow budget (est. 0.35; if the true value exceeds ~0.55 the regimen flips to over-budget); and lisavanbulin's dose-limiting toxicity being CNS rather than marrow.

A caution about the engine itself

Every figure comes from one simulation engine. It carried 1,340+ tests, but until recently not one checked it against a known outcome. Exactly one apples-to-apples external check exists (lomustine, 16 dogs, same endpoint/disease/species, 37.5% relapse-free), and the engine failed it — predicting 0.0% at every potency, because it structurally cannot represent a cured animal. An extinction state was added and it now returns a realistic 42% vs. the observed 37.5% — but only above the potency range the project itself considers plausible and with a favourable second free parameter, i.e. curve-fitting, labelled as such. Read the whole report as hypothesis-generating.

The one testWhat would falsify the headline

MTAP status. A single MTAP immunohistochemistry stain (or MTAP/CDKN2A copy-number assay) on the existing tumour tissue:

MTAP-deletedThe strongest ten-year arm is live.
MTAP-intactThe synthetic-lethal maintenance does nothing and durability collapses to the previous answer.

This is the single most load-bearing fact in the analysis and the cheapest decisive test in it — it needs archived tissue and a matched normal, no live dog. Runner-up falsifier: measuring a PRMT5 inhibitor's CNS penetration in a dog below ~0.37.

DeliveryThe brain, the two-drug / no-antigen problem, and how it closes

For a tumour with no identified targetable antigen, the fallback is two small molecules — an ERK1/2 inhibitor (e.g. temuterkib) at full MTD + a PI3K/AKT inhibitor (e.g. paxalisib), pairing two independent axes. The MAPK rationale rests on three canine HS lines (BD/OD/DH82) that responded in vitro to MEK inhibition (cobimetinib IC50 74–372 nM, PMID 39202410), plus MAPK alteration in ~43–64% of cases across cohorts — not the "twelve canine HS lines" an earlier draft claimed, which do not exist. In the lung this reaches 1.00 at full dose (~0.99 realistic). In the brain it initially read 0.00 — but that came from applying trametinib's poor brain-to-plasma ratio (0.15) to two drugs that are not trametinib. Successive checks narrowed the gap: the molecules work on canine protein (ERK2/MAPK1 100% identical human-to-dog; PI3Kα 99.81%, ATP pocket identical — both now computed from real UniProt sequences and reproducible via sequence_conservation; P-gp/ABCB1, which gates delivery, is only 91% conserved, so canine penetration must be measured); crediting paxalisib at its real penetration drops the ERK requirement to 0.235–0.300; and two structure-based shortcuts were tried and both failed their controls (a firmer basis for "this must be measured").

The closer: local delivery

All four bottleneck coordinates are what happens when a swallowed drug crosses the blood-brain barrier. So stop dosing systemically: convection-enhanced delivery or a drug-eluting implant in the resection cavity (FDA-approved precedent in the Gliadel wafer). Modelled durability is 0.00 at 0.15 systemic penetration and 1.00 at local delivery, holding flat to 10 years. The breed's extra-axial geometry supplies the cavity by construction.

The fluid-borne remainder

The ~52% CSF-positive compartment is not reached by a cavity implant. It maps onto a bounded, CNS-restricted space addressable by a three-part combination: craniospinal radiation (coverage), intrathecal dosing (rate control), and an immune arm (persistence). No durability number is computed for this compartment, and none should be until someone measures whether drug saturates the fluid space. Breed-specific caution: the breed carries the SOD1 degenerative-myelopathy allele at high frequency, so repeated intrathecal dosing is a plausible, unquantified interaction.

The measured realityThe honest headline

Every figure above is a model output; no agent in any regimen here has ever been given to a dog with this disease. The location-matched reality today:

Intracranial, definitive treatment~44-day median (43–44 across sources) — surgery + radiation + chemotherapy, 102-dog series; longest survivor under eight months.
Localized HS, lomustine after debulking568-day median, 243-day disease-free interval, 0.375 relapse-free (n = 16) — the only structurally matched comparator with real durability.

The gap between 44 days and ten years is the real headline, and nothing here narrows it. What "structurally achievable" earns is that the arithmetic closes — the biology permits a durable response — not that one has been achieved. It does not earn a prognosis, because nobody has yet measured how many dogs of this breed carry a non-droppable target.

The auditAm I back where I started?

Four independent review agents each re-ran the code for a slice of the 24 core modules, ignoring all prose and grounding every finding by execution. Verdict: genuinely progressed, not circular. All 439 core tests pass and actively encode the retractions.

What the audit says is genuinely shaky
  • Ten-year durability rests on one drug and one inequality that fails at conservative access; escape-coverage is solid, ten-year durability is a hypothesis stacked on it.
  • Several deciding numbers are unmeasured in dogs — drug potencies, the tumour's growth rate, the maintenance pill's brain access and marrow cost.
  • Some reasoning lives in prose and tests, not the calculator — the live regimen hard-codes potency instead of deriving it from measured IC50s. The conclusions are right, but the wiring is looser than the narrative implies.

What to do firstFeasibility, and the cheapest decisive steps

Cost is roughly $20,000–$45,000 per dog before any investigational-drug cost. The two binding constraints are not biological: drug access (the targeted agents are sponsor-owned, unapproved, with no veterinary precedent — a business-development problem) and enrolment (CNS histiocytic sarcoma is ~2.2% of primary intracranial tumours; it needs a multi-centre consortium).

The cheapest things that would move this — neither is a drug
  • A genotype panel on existing tumour tissue (MTAP/CDKN2A/PTEN copy number; PTPN11/KRAS/RB1 sequence) — assigns the maintenance tier and resolves a separate drug liability at no extra cost. Do this first.
  • The tumour's growth rate from imaging already taken — it enters the answer twice.
  • A vaccine/immune kill rate in a dog, and resiquimod on canine HS cells in vitro (does the immune depot kill this myeloid tumour, or feed it).
  • A brain:plasma ratio for an ERK and a PI3K/AKT inhibitor in a dog — the durability either side of the threshold is already computed.
