Using only treatments that exist today, the B-cell body closes in the model. One escape stays open in the B-cell brain and the T-cell body, and three in the T-cell brain. Nothing here has been tried in a dog.

The plan that works with what exists today (B-cell body)

Anti-CD20 antibody trial · hydroxychloroquine off-label · verdinexor licensed · stem-cell transplant from a perfectly matched donor dog referral centre · oral cytarabine prodrug human drug, dog use unverified. The model clears every escape route in the body by day 74 with a margin of 0.15 per day over the cancer's regrowth. Hundreds of other four-drug combinations of existing treatments also close the body.

It leans on the transplant: a donor transplant needs a perfectly matched littermate (25% chance per sibling), and among 2,328 five-drug sets that close the body, none still works if every drug is half as strong or any one is dropped.

The one escape that stays open, and what closes it

Dormant, pump-armoured progenitor cells. They are measured in dog lymphoma, they do not divide so division-dependent drugs miss them, and they pump out the drugs that do not need division. Every treatment that exists today and reaches the brain either needs the cell to divide, is pumped out, or is defeated by the enzyme loss on the T-cell side. After the transplant window ends, nothing still being given reaches them in the brain. Giving the existing drugs for up to two years does not change this (checked).

The model specifies what is missing: one more treatment that does not need cell division, is not pumped out, reaches the brain, and kills at least 0.06 per day there (0.03 per day in the T-cell body), without overloading the organs. Treatments with human data that meet this, and have to be built for dogs:

  • CAR-T cells for dogs, including CAR-T given into the spinal fluid and CD7 or CD5+CD7 CAR-T for T-cell disease. Human brain-lymphoma data exist.
  • A canine CD3xCD20 T-cell engager (B-cell). Human engagers have strong results (epcoritamab with R-CHOP: 85% complete responses, 80% progression-free at 2 years), but human CD3 antibodies do not bind canine CD3, so a dog version has to be made. Canine CD3 and CD20 antibody parts already exist.

Earlier plans using those treatments close everything in the model: B-cell with 7 treatments, T-cell with 5. They hold at the central human values, and they need the treatments built.

How each class was handled

  • Stem-cell transplant: credited from the dog data (8 of 9 first-remission dogs alive past 4 years, longest about 8 years) and the human T-cell lymphoma trial. It is what closes the B-cell body with existing treatments. It still misses cells that have lost their immune markers (MHC).
  • Vaccines: credited from dog survival data (APAVAC; the telomerase DNA vaccine). They need dividing cells and immune markers, so they add margin but cannot reach the dormant cells. Their long-term tails are flat (3-year survival 10% vs 8%).
  • Inhibitors: credited using human lab and drug-level data (BTK, HDAC, proteasome, XPO1, BCL2 and others). Each covers specific routes. They are pumped out or need division, so none reaches the dormant cells.
  • T-cell engagers and armed T cells: engagers credited from human data and listed above as needing development. Armed T cells have no lymphoma data in any species and get no credit. The eBAT toxin (from the hemangiosarcoma work) is set aside because its targets, EGFR and uPAR, are lower in dog lymphoma than in sarcomas, and a human T-cell line without them was not killed.
  • The spinal pump: no longer needed. Oral cytarabine prodrug reaches the spinal fluid in dogs without a device (1.0 to 3.6 µM).

What the numbers rest on

  • Measured in dogs: anti-CD20 antibody B-cell depletion, hydroxychloroquine tumor levels (about 100 times blood), cytarabine levels in the spinal fluid, and the dormant-cell and drug-pump escapes.
  • Worked out from measurements: verdinexor, radiation, and oral cytarabine, from dog cell sensitivity and drug levels.
  • Calibrated from outcomes: the transplant effect, from dog survival plateaus; vaccines, from dog survival gains over controls.
  • Carried over from human data, reasoning written down: hydroxychloroquine, HDAC inhibitors, BTK inhibitors, CAR-T and engager kill rates, brain access, brain side-effect rates, and 5 to 10 year follow-up.

Conditions, and where each one stands

  • A matched donor dog is available for the B-cell body plan. Status: depends on the dog (25% per littermate).
  • One more treatment is built for the brain and the T-cell body. Status: not met.
  • CAR-T or engager cells expand well in dogs. Status: not shown; dog CAR-T has not yet shown benefit.
  • Human values carry over to dogs (kill rates, brain access, how long cells last). Status: used at the central values; at the low end the B-cell plan with built treatments stops closing.

Outside this check: drug resistance with no traced mechanism (about half of CHOP-treated dogs become resistant, only part explained), eye and testis disease, tumors inside blood vessels, protection by bone-marrow and lymph-node surroundings, and later risks such as second cancers and treatment deaths.
