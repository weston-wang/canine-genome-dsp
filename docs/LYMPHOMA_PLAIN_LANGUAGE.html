Canine multicentric lymphoma · model report

Every escape route we found is covered

A treatment plan for B-cell and for T-cell lymphoma that, in the model, closes all 23 ways the cancer can come back, in the body and in the brain. This is a computer model. Nothing here has been tried in a dog.

"Closed" means that after counting side effects for the whole combination, the treatments kill more cells per day than the cancer regrows, for every escape route, and the model clears every cell inside the time the treatments can safely be given. Both plans still work if every drug is only half as strong as assumed, and if any one treatment is dropped.

The two plans

Green means the treatment exists today. Amber means it has to be built or is still in trials.

B-cell lymphoma

  • Anti-CD20 antibody trial
  • Hydroxychloroquine off-label
  • Panobinostat off-label
  • Tumor vaccine from the dog's own tumor trial
  • Engineered CAR-T cells that last to build
  • CAR-T cells into the spinal fluid to build
  • Continuous spinal-fluid cytarabine pump to build

T-cell lymphoma

  • Hydroxychloroquine off-label
  • Verdinexor licensed
  • CD7 CAR-T cells to build
  • CD5 + CD7 CAR-T cells to build
  • Continuous spinal-fluid cytarabine pump to build

What the numbers rest on

  • Measured in dogs: how fast the anti-CD20 antibody removes B cells, how far hydroxychloroquine concentrates in tumors (about 100 times blood), and the dormant-cell and drug-pump escapes themselves.
  • Worked out from measurements: verdinexor's and radiation's kill rates, from lab sensitivity of dog lymphoma cells combined with drug levels in dogs.
  • Carried over from human data, with the reasoning written down: hydroxychloroquine's and the spinal pump's kill rates, the CAR-T kill rate (0.35 per day, range 0.12 to 1.1), how much CAR-T reaches the brain (half of the body rate), how long cells last in spinal fluid (40% of each dosing interval), brain side-effect rates, and 5 to 10 year follow-up. In people, 92% of patients still event-free at 2 years were alive at 5.
  • Side effects: counted on all treatments together, per organ, so adding a treatment can weaken the others.

The conditions, and where each one stands

The closure holds if all four are met. Each is a yes or no, not a probability.

  • The treatments get built. A CAR-T made for dogs that expands and lasts, CAR-T given into the spinal fluid, and the continuous spinal pump do not exist yet. Status: not met.
  • Dog CAR-T cells expand well. The kill rate in a dog depends on how many cells grow. Dog CAR-T has not yet shown benefit. Status: not yet shown.
  • The central human values hold. At the low end (kill 0.12 per day, cells active 15% of the time) the B-cell plan no longer closes, and the T-cell plan closes but fails the half-strength test. Status: holds at the central values, which are justified transfers from human data.
  • Reaching the brain surface is enough. Canine brain lymphoma sits in the linings and around vessels, which spinal-fluid delivery reaches. Deep tissue access is not measured. Status: holds for the linings and vessel spaces; deep tissue is outside the check.

Outside this check

  • Drug resistance that cannot be traced to a known mechanism (about half of dogs on CHOP become drug-resistant, only part of it explained).
  • Cancer in the eye and testis, and tumors inside blood vessels, which spinal-fluid delivery cannot reach.
  • Later risks: second cancers (21% at 10 years in human CAR-T survivors) and deaths from treatment (18%).
  • Protection from the bone-marrow and lymph-node environment, which the model can only test as an all-or-nothing case.

Treatments considered and set aside

  • Vaccines: the dog's-own-tumor vaccine is in the B-cell plan but is not needed, because the plan closes without it. Telomerase DNA vaccine, CD40-B RNA vaccine and dendritic-cell vaccines did not lengthen time to relapse, or gave no immune response; vaccines in dogs show no long-term benefit (3-year survival 10% vs 8%).
  • Stem-cell transplant: a transplant from a perfectly matched donor gave the longest remissions in dogs (8 of 9 alive over 4 years, longest about 8 years), but it needs a matched donor, carries up to 55% procedure mortality in older series, and cannot be modelled as a kill rate.
  • Others set aside: checkpoint blockers (no responses in 15 dogs), the CD52 antibody (failed its trial), steroid or depot-cytarabine into the spinal fluid (the cancer can disable both), lenalidomide, BTK inhibitors and whole-brain radiation.
