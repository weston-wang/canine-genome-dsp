## 2. UK — the Special Import route. VERIFIED, and it is NOT the cascade limb I expected

### 2.1 The cascade itself does NOT cover a foreign HUMAN medicine
`Veterinary Medicines Regulations 2013` (S.I. 2013/2033), **Schedule 4 paragraph 1(2)** (E+W+S version, as amended to 17.5.2024 by
S.I. 2024/567), read verbatim from legislation.gov.uk:

> "(2) If there is no authorised veterinary medicinal product in the United Kingdom for a condition the veterinary surgeon
> responsible for the animal may, in particular to avoid unacceptable suffering, treat the animal concerned with the following
> ("the cascade"), cascaded in the following order—
> (a) a veterinary medicinal product authorised in the United Kingdom for use with another animal species, or for another
> condition in the same species; or
> (b) if there is no such product that is suitable, either—
>   (i) a human medicinal product **authorised in the United Kingdom**; or
>   (ii) a **veterinary** medicinal product not authorised in the United Kingdom but authorised in another country for use with
>   any animal species ...; or
> (c) if there is no such product that is suitable, a veterinary medicinal product prepared extemporaneously by a pharmacist, a
> veterinary surgeon or a person holding a manufacturing authorisation..."
>
> "(3) In the case of a veterinary medicinal product imported from another country, if the veterinary surgeon has not obtained a
> certificate from the Secretary of State under regulation 25(5) permitting importation, the veterinary surgeon must obtain a
> certificate from the Secretary of State before administration."

URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/2033/schedule/4
(the word "country" at (b)(ii) and (3) replaced "member State" on 31.12.2020 by S.I. 2019/676 reg 3(34)(a)(i) as amended by
S.I. 2020/1461 — i.e. post-Brexit GB law expressly extends the limb to THIRD countries.)

**So limb (b)(ii) is no help for Starasid**: Starasid is a *human* medicine in Japan, and limb (b)(i) requires a human medicine
authorised *in the United Kingdom*. VERIFIED.

### 2.2 The provision that DOES cover it: Schedule 4 paragraph 7, "Treatment in exceptional circumstances"
Verbatim (E+W+S):

> "**7.**—(1) Where the health situation so requires, and where there is no suitable veterinary medicinal product available either
> as an authorised product or under the cascade, a veterinary surgeon may treat an animal with **a medicinal product authorised in
> another country**; but a veterinary surgeon who has not obtained a certificate from the Secretary of State under regulation 25(5)
> permitting importation must obtain a certificate from the Secretary of State before treating the animal.
> (2) The certificate may be granted subject to any condition the Secretary of State thinks fit."

Note the words: **"a medicinal product"**, not "a veterinary medicinal product". This is the limb that reaches a Japanese human
product. It sits *below* the whole cascade ("no suitable product ... either as an authorised product or under the cascade"), so the
vet must first exhaust UK-authorised vet drugs, UK-authorised human drugs (which includes **generic cytarabine injection** — see
§6) and third-country vet products. VERIFIED from the legislation; the "below the cascade" ordering is the statute's own wording.

### 2.3 Import authority
`VMR 2013 reg 25` (E+W+S), verbatim:
> "(1) No person may import or be concerned in the importation of an unauthorised veterinary medicinal product except in accordance
> with this regulation.
> (5) A veterinary surgeon may import an unauthorised veterinary medicinal product that is authorised in another country if it is
> for the purpose of administration by that veterinary surgeon or under that veterinary surgeon's responsibility under the cascade
> **or administration in exceptional circumstances in accordance with Schedule 4**; the import must be in accordance with the
> appropriate certificate granted by the Secretary of State, and the product may be imported by the veterinary surgeon personally
> or by using a wholesale dealer or pharmacist as an agent."

URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/2033/regulation/25
So: the vet is the importer (or uses a wholesaler/pharmacist as agent), and the Sch 4 para 7 certificate is the import authority.
VERIFIED.

### 2.4 Scheme mechanics — VMD guidance
Current VMD cascade guidance (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-cascade-prescribing-unauthorised-medicines) states the GB cascade in
four steps and adds the express exception: **"In exceptional circumstances, a human medicine may be imported from outside of the
UK"** with a Special Import Certificate. It also states the mandatory conditions: the vet must make a clinical assessment, the
animal must be under their care, **"You must obtain the owner's consent"**, records kept **5 years**, and a human medicine may not
be prescribed "simply because it is cheaper" than an authorised alternative. VERIFIED (gov.uk, primary).

Detailed mechanics come from **VMGN No. 5 "Import Certificate Schemes"** — which carries the banner
**"Document Withdrawn as out of date"** (last updated October 2013, pre-Brexit) on the gov.uk asset store, so treat the numbers as
indicative of practice, not current law:
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6825cb47940c028bcf34e031/VMGN_05_Import_Certificate_Schemes.pdf
- Two certificate types then existed. The one that matches Starasid is the **Special Treatment Certificate (STC)**: para 22 —
  "If a veterinary surgeon considers that there is not a suitable veterinary or human medicinal product authorised in the UK or a
  suitable VMP authorised in another EU MS to treat a condition, a veterinary surgeon may apply for an STC to import a suitable
  authorised product from outside the EU." Para 23 — "If the veterinary surgeon identifies a human medicinal product as being the
  only suitable alternative, this will require an STC, **regardless of whether it is from Europe or a third country**."
- **Who applies:** para 16 — "You may only apply for an SIC or STC if you are a veterinary surgeon registered with the Royal
  College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS)." The holder is "normally ... the individual veterinary surgeon who is caring for the
  animal concerned". Products "do not need to pass through a wholesale dealer; veterinary surgeons may act as their own importers"
  (para 17).
- **Evidence required** (para 25): "(a) the clinical details/history of the animal; (b) reference to any alternative products
  authorised in the UK (or EU) and a justification as to why these products cannot be used; (c) previous relevant treatments;
  (d) the results of any diagnostic tests which have been performed." Para 26: "A separate justification should be included on each
  application. It is not sufficient to refer to previous applications."
- **First-time product** (para 36): because Starasid has (probably) never been imported, the VMD additionally requires
  "(a) copy of the Summary of Product Characteristics (SPC) or datasheet **in English**" and "(b) copies of the labels".
  Para 37: VMD may request data direct from the manufacturer. INFERRED that Starasid is a "new" product to the VMD — not checked.
- **One animal per application** (para 40): "An STC application must be supplied for each individual animal."
- **Chronic/long-term use is expressly contemplated** (para 42): "Generally, it is not permitted to hold stocks in veterinary
  practices of any imported human medicines or of veterinary medicines imported from countries outside of the EU. ... **For longer
  term use in a particular animal, an individual STC application naming that animal should be submitted and the quantity applied
  for should correspond to the dosage required by that animal.**"
- **Validity** (paras 14, 32): "the import certificate will remain valid until the quantity specified has been imported and used,
  or the expiry date stated on the certificate, whichever occurs first. The period of validity for the import certificate **does
  not have to extend to the whole course of treatment**." "The certificate will be valid for **no more than one year** and for
  SIC/STC the total amount required and proposed dosage schedule should reflect this." Para 30: repeats are possible online
  ("e.g. if you are permitted repeats at 6 monthly intervals") but **"For an STC ... all initial applications for a particular
  animal must be assessed."**
- **Timescales** (para 52): new product 15 working days (urgent 5); previously imported 10 working days (urgent 2).
- **Fees** (para 53): online SICs free, online repeat STCs free, hard-copy STC £30, hard-copy repeat STC £30.
- **No class restriction on cytotoxics was found.** The only product-class cautions in VMGN 5 are: import certificates are "less
  suited" to food-producing species and to **vaccines** from outside the EU (para 11), and controlled drugs additionally need a Home
  Office import licence (para 48). Cytarabine ocfosfate is not a controlled drug. VERIFIED as an absence in this document; I did
  not find a separate VMD cytotoxic-handling rule (not searched exhaustively).
- Advertising/promotion of specially imported products is unlawful (VMD blog, 13 Sep 2023).

Scheme now runs through a new digital service: applications only via the new service since **15 July 2024**
(https://www.gov.uk/government/news/further-changes-to-our-special-imports-digital-service; portal www.vmdconnect.uk). SECONDARY/
primary-news.

### 2.5 UK bottom line (so far)
There IS a documented lawful route in Great Britain: **VMR 2013 Sch 4 para 7 + reg 25(5)**, operated as a VMD Special Import
Certificate, applied for by the RCVS-registered vet treating the named dog, one application per animal, certificate up to one year,
renewable, and the guidance expressly addresses "longer term use in a particular animal". The gate is that the vet must show no
suitable UK-authorised product — and a UK marketing authorisation for **cytarabine injection** exists, so the justification has to
be about the *route* (oral, at home, daily) rather than the molecule. See §6.

## 1. JAPAN — the product is BEING WITHDRAWN. This is the headline finding and it undercuts every import route

### 1.1 What is/was approved
- **Starasid Capsules 50** (スタラシドカプセル50), generic name **cytarabine ocfosfate hydrate** (シタラビン オクホスファート水和物),
  `Cytarabine Ocfosfate Hydrate`, ATC **L01BC01**, Japanese therapeutic class 4224 (antineoplastic), YJ code 4224001M1020.
  MAH / 製造販売元: **日本化薬株式会社 (Nippon Kayaku Co., Ltd.)**, Marunouchi 2-1-1, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo.
  Price ¥289.5 per capsule. Regulatory class 劇薬, 処方箋医薬品 (powerful drug; prescription-only).
  Package insert **revised June 2026 (2nd edition of the new format)**; record version 2026/09/16.
  Source: KEGG MEDICUS reproduction of the JAPIC/PMDA package insert,
  https://www.kegg.jp/medicus-bin/japic_med?japic_code=00072101 — **SECONDARY** (faithful reproduction of the PMDA-filed 添付文書;
  I could not drive PMDA's own `PmdaSearch/iyakuSearch` POST form from here).
- **Indications (4. 効能または効果), verbatim:** "○成人 急性非リンパ性白血病（強力な化学療法が対象となる症例にはその療法を優先する。）
  ○骨髄異形成症候群（Myelodysplastic Syndrome）" — i.e. adult acute NON-lymphocytic leukaemia, and MDS. **Not lymphoma.**
- **Dose (6. 用法及び用量), verbatim:** ANLL — "シタラビン オクホスファートとして、1日100〜300mgを2〜3週間連続経口投与し、2〜3週間休薬する。
  これを繰り返す。… 本剤の投与時期は食後とし、1日1〜3回に分けて服用する。"  MDS — same but "1日100〜200mg".
  So the label is **intermittent: 2-3 weeks on, 2-3 weeks off, after meals, 1-3 divided doses**. Maximum approved daily dose 300 mg.
- **Label warning directly relevant to multi-year dosing (8.4), verbatim:** "また、使用が長期間にわたると副作用が強くあらわれ、遷延性に推移する
  ことがあるので、投与は慎重に行うこと。" ("with long-term use adverse reactions appear more strongly and may become protracted, so
  administer with caution"). Serious ADRs listed: myelosuppression and **interstitial pneumonia**. Anorexia 35.0%.
- Approval date **2 October 1992** for leukaemia and MDS — **SECONDARY** (AdisInsight / Inxight via web search summary; not confirmed on a
  PMDA page).

### 1.2 Current marketing status: the 100 mg capsule is GONE and the line is being terminated
**VERIFIED on primary MHLW documents:**
1. MHLW / Chuikyo (中央社会保険医療協議会) paper 総－１ dated **7.10.29 (29 October 2025)**, "薬価基準から削除する品目について"
   (Items to be deleted from the drug pricing standard): **item no. 203 = 「スタラシドカプセル１００」, 「１００ｍｇ１カプセル」,
   manufacturer 「日本化薬」.**

   希望する場合" (the MAH has requested **cessation of supply** and deletion from the pricing standard because medical demand has ceased, etc.),
   agreed by the relevant academic societies, and that "当該506 品目については、11 月頃に経過措置に移行予定であり、経過措置期間は
   **令和８年３月末**までとなる" (the 506 items move to transitional status around November 2025 and the transitional period runs to
   **end of March 2026**).
   URL: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/10808000/001588123.pdf
2. The MHLW transitional-measure abolition list, "令和８年３月３１日限りで廃止となる経過措置医薬品（内服薬）", current as of
   令和８年２月１９日 (19 Feb 2026), lists drug code **614220012 「スタラシドカプセル１００ １００ｍｇ」, カプセル, ¥389.1**.
   Retrieved from a pharmacy-trade reproduction: http://www.mediclerk.co.jp/fax/20260304haishi.pdf — **SECONDARY** for the copy,
   but it corroborates the primary Chuikyo document exactly.

**So: as of today (2026-10-06) the 100 mg Starasid capsule is off the Japanese pricing standard and no longer supplied.**
The **50 mg capsule survives** — its package insert was revised June 2026 and section 22 (包装) now lists only
"＜スタラシドカプセル50＞ 40カプセル［10カプセル（PTP）×4］". **INFERRED** from the June-2026 insert carrying only the 50 mg pack.
I did **not** find a Chuikyo deletion entry for the 50 mg capsule, so on the record it is still marketed.

**Consequence (INFERRED, but the inference is tight):** a MAH that has told MHLW medical demand has ceased and has withdrawn the larger
strength is exiting the product. Any import route that depends on a continuous 1-3 year supply of this product from a single Japanese
manufacturer that is discontinuing it is **a supply risk, not a legal one**, and the supply risk is already materialising. At 50 mg per
capsule a 100-200 mg/day canine dose is 2-4 capsules/day, i.e. 730-1,460 capsules/year, from a 40-capsule pack.

### 1.3 Japanese VETERINARY status
No Japanese veterinary approval of cytarabine ocfosfate was found. Japanese veterinary medicines are approved by **MAFF** through the
National Veterinary Assay Laboratory (動物医薬品検査所, NVAL), not PMDA (https://www.maff.go.jp/nval/). Searching MAFF/NVAL for a canine
cytarabine or cytarabine-ocfosfate product returned only the generic approval-procedure guidance, no product.
**Status: NOT FOUND (a negative search, not a verified absence)** — I did not query the NVAL approved-product database directly.
Under Japan's Pharmaceutical Affairs Act a human drug used in an animal would itself be an off-label/veterinary-use question I did not reach.

### 1.4 The dog PK paper
PMID 37670479 (J Vet Intern Med) used oral cytarabine ocfosfate in **4 healthy dogs for 7 daily doses** — already in the repo record
(`docs/LYMPHOMA_UNIVERSE.md` §G, H.3). It is the only canine exposure data and it is 7 days, not years. Already covered; not new.

## 4a. US — INAD / investigational exemption, 21 CFR Part 511. VERIFIED verbatim

Full text read from the official CFR XML:
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title21-vol6/xml/CFR-2024-title21-vol6-sec511-1.xml
(statutory hook: FD&C Act §512(j); the regulation is "New animal drugs for investigational use exempt from section 512(a)").

### What it permits
§511.1(b): "A shipment or other delivery of a new animal drug ... intended for **clinical investigational use in animals** shall be
exempt from section 512(a) and (m) of the act if all the following conditions are met". Conditions, verbatim in the operative parts:
- **(b)(1) label:** "Caution. Contains a new animal drug for use only in investigational animals in clinical trials. Not for use in
  humans. Edible products of investigational animals are not to be used for food unless authorization has been granted by the U.S.
  Food and Drug Administration or by the U.S. Department of Agriculture." (The edible-product machinery at (b)(5) is irrelevant to a dog.)
- **(b)(4) before shipping:** "Prior to shipment of the new animal drug for clinical tests in animals, the sponsor of the
  investigation shall submit in triplicate to FDA a 'Notice of Claimed Investigational Exemption for a New Animal Drug' including a
  signed statement containing the following information: (i) The identity of the new animal drug. (ii) All labeling and other
  pertinent information to be supplied to the investigators... (iii) The name and address of each clinical investigator. (iv) The
  approximate number of animals to be treated (or if not available, the amount of new animal drug to be shipped)."
  (The extra (b)(4)(v) items — withdrawal time, max daily dose, start/end dates — are required only "If the new animal drug is given
  to **food-producing** animals", so not for a dog.)
- **(b)(7) investigator qualification and records**, 2-year retention; **(b)(8)(ii)** sponsor must provide current monitoring and report
  safety findings.
- **(b)(2)/(b)(3):** due diligence that it will "actually be used for tests in animals", and shipment records for 2 years.
- **(b)(10):** a NEPA claim for categorical exclusion (§25.30/§25.33) or an environmental assessment.
- Submissions go to "Center for Veterinary Medicine, 7500 Standish Pl., Rockville, MD 20855" (§511.1(e)).

### The two provisions that decide the pet-dog question
1. **§511.1(b)(8)(iii): "Shall not unduly prolong distribution of the new animal drug for investigational use."**
2. **§511.1(d) Termination of exemption:** the exemption ends if "(2) The continuance of the investigation is unsafe or otherwise
   contrary to the public interest **or the drug is being or has been used for purposes other than bona fide scientific
   investigation**".
Together these mean the Part 511 exemption is for a **bona fide investigation**, not for treating one named pet indefinitely.
A client-owned pet dog CAN lawfully receive the drug — but only as an enrolled subject of a real clinical investigation with a
protocol, a sponsor, a qualified investigator and monitoring. **VERIFIED from the regulation's own text**; the "not for one named pet
indefinitely" reading is **INFERRED** from (b)(8)(iii) + (d)(2), which is as far as the text goes.

### Importation under Part 511 — §511.1(b)(9), verbatim
> "(9) If the shipment or other delivery of the new animal drug is **imported or offered for importation into the United States** for
> clinical investigational use in animals, it shall also meet the following conditions:
> (i) The importer of all such shipments or deliveries is an agent of the foreign exporter residing in the United States or the
> ultimate consignee, which person has, prior to such shipments and deliveries, informed FDA of his intention to import the new animal
> drug as sponsor in compliance with the conditions prescribed in this subdivision; or
> (ii) The new animal drug is shipped directly to a **scientific institution with adequate facilities and qualified personnel to
> conduct laboratory or clinical investigations** and is intended solely for use in such institutions and which institution has
> submitted a statement as sponsor of the investigation."

**This is the route a veterinary teaching hospital would use**: limb (ii) lets Nippon Kayaku ship Starasid directly to the institution,
which is itself the sponsor. VERIFIED. No separate "foreign approval" test applies — Part 511 does not care that the drug is approved
in Japan; it is simply an unapproved new animal drug in the US either way.

### Is there a CVM compassionate/emergency-use mechanism for an individual animal?
**No such mechanism was found in Part 511, and Part 511 contains no analogue of the human expanded-access rules (21 CFR 312.300-320).**
There is also **no "expanded access" or "single-animal IND" regulation in 21 CFR Parts 511-516** that I could locate.
Marked **NOT FOUND**, not "does not exist" — I have not exhausted CVM's Program Policy & Procedures Manual (e.g. PPM 1243.2100,
1243.4000, 1243.4065) which I did not read in full.
