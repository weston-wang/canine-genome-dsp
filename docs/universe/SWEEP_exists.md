Success criteria (user's words, graded against exactly these): "make sure every mechanism and every escape is closed by either real data or rigorous model"; "looking for 10+ years of durability"; "assuming early detection"; "I'm okay with no specific data but if scientifically sound"; latest: "I needed scientifically sound, not totally theoretical."

## 1. Implanted intrathecal pumps / reservoirs / ventricular access in dogs

VERIFIED
- PMID 8164885 (Sabbe 1993, Neurotoxicology 14:397-410, no DOI in PubMed). "Male beagle dogs were prepared with chronic lumbar intrathecal catheters connected to subcutaneously implanted infusion pumps. Three groups of dogs received 28 days of infusion of saline (vehicle: 1 ml/24 hrs; N = 10), 200 micrograms/ml/24 hrs baclofen (N = 10) or 2000 micrograms/ml/24 hrs baclofen (N = 10)." "...a mild fibrotic reaction to the catheter, but there were no changes distinguishable from vehicle infused animals..." Research (laboratory beagle) model, 28 days. Grade: MEASURED for catheter/pump tolerability over 28 d, drug = baclofen.
- PMID 31124198 (Hildebrand 2019, Neuromodulation 22:790-798, doi 10.1111/ner.12963). "Adult dogs (hound cross, n = 32) were implanted with intrathecal catheters connected to SynchroMed II infusion pumps." Morphine 12 mg/day at 25 mg/mL as x1/x2/x4/x8 boluses vs continuous 20 uL/h. "animals receiving morphine displayed spinally compressing durally derived masses ... rank order of median cross-sectional mass area: Saline 0.7 mm; x2 1.8; x4 2.7; x1 2.7; x8 4.2; Continuous 8.1 mm, with statistical difference from saline being seen with continuous (p < 0.0001) and x8 (p < 0.05)." "Using multiple bolus dosing, IMs were reduced as compared to continuous infusion". IMPORTANT for the model: the SynchroMed II + intrathecal catheter in dogs is a real, published, MEASURED platform (Medtronic-sponsored lab study), BUT the dog model showed that CONTINUOUS infusion of a concentrated drug (25 mg/mL morphine) gave the largest catheter-tip masses; this is a morphine/opioid-specific granuloma literature (morphine is the known granuloma-prone drug); cytarabine at the concentrations needed is a different drug. Not transferable as "cytarabine is safe", but it is the right model and shows bolus-programmed dosing (which SynchroMed does) lowers risk.
- Commercial veterinary use of SynchroMed-type pumps: NOT FOUND in PubMed (no clinical canine series of implanted programmable intrathecal pumps found; search "intrathecal pump dog baclofen OR morphine implanted" returned 2532 mostly human/unrelated hits, top 15 not clinical canine pump series; "Ommaya reservoir dogs" returned only 504981, a 1979 Sci Am overview with "Dogs" as MeSH, not an Ommaya study). Searches for dog + subcutaneous intrathecal port/chronic catheter + PK returned 0.

VP shunt (ventricular access in client-owned dogs; the nearest real device for a ventricular catheter)
- PMID 30747447 (Gradner 2019, J Vet Intern Med 33:403-412, doi 10.1111/jvim.15422), systematic review: "Complications in dogs (n = 60) were shunt obstruction (6/60; 10%), pain (4/60; 5.5%), shunt infection (3/60; 4.1%), disconnection (3/60; 4.1%), excessive shunting (2/60; 2.7%), and kinking (1/60; 1.6%)... Complications were most likely during the first 6 months after shunt placement."
- PMID 37675951 (Farke 2023, J Vet Intern Med 37:2269-2277, doi 10.1111/jvim.16861): 75 dogs and 7 cats; "Overshunting had a prevalence of 18%"; hemispheric collapse/subdural hemorrhage in 15 animals.
- PMID 30888275 (Gillespie 2019, JAVMA 254:835-842, doi 10.2460/javma.254.7.835): 28 surgical dogs; "4 (15%) had known postoperative complications"; median follow-up 15.5 months surgical.
- PMID 37370401 (Keadwut 2023, Animals 13:1890, doi 10.3390/ani13121890): 8 dogs, "Three of them had shunt infections within 4 to 6 weeks after surgery" (all with low-pressure valve overshunting, n=4).
- PMID 38519448 (Wyatt 2024, Vet Surg 53:1430, doi 10.1111/vsu.14090): case, "Long-term follow up at 40 months after the second surgical procedure revealed a normal neurological examination" (shunt in situ >=18 months then second shunt). Longevity evidence is case-level only.
- PMID 39027907 (Schmidt & Farke 2024, Front Vet Sci 11:1435982, doi 10.3389/fvets.2024.1435982) review: "shunt failure remains a major problem, and often leads to multiple hospital admissions."
- PMID 36382395 (Beckmann 2022, J Vet Intern Med 37:204-215, doi 10.1111/jvim.16583): intraventricular tumours, VPS+RT median survival 1103 d vs RT alone 162 d (n=12 animals total). Shows ventricular catheters are tolerated alongside radiotherapy in client-owned dogs.
- PMID 41321578 (Jeon 2025, Front Vet Sci 12:1665844, doi 10.3389/fvets.2025.1665844): patient-specific 3D-printed guide vs neuronavigation for ventricular catheter placement in beagles: ventricular catheter placement accuracy is a solved engineering task in dogs.
NOT FOUND: any canine Ommaya/subcutaneous reservoir used for drug delivery; any dog implanted-pump infection rate; longevity beyond ~40 months case-level.

## 2. Cytarabine in pumps; human intrathecal chemo via reservoirs

VERIFIED (stability)
- PMID 6214944 (Keller & Ensminger 1982, Am J Hosp Pharm 39:1321-3; no DOI). "pumps, filled with cytarabine, dichloromethotrexate, and vinblastine, in a 37 degree C water bath with mild agitation ... Cytarabine and dichloromethotrexate showed no appreciable drug decomposition in the control vials or the pump over the 15-day and 28-day study periods, respectively." (A totally implanted subcutaneous drug delivery system; abstract does not name SynchroMed and does not state concentration.) Grade: MEASURED at 37 C for 15 days, device not SynchroMed.
- PMID 1598939 (Rochard 1992, Am J Hosp Pharm 49:619-23; no DOI). EVA portable reservoirs, cytarabine 25 or 1.25 mg/mL: "Cytarabine was stable for 28 days at 4 and 22 degrees C and for 7 days at 35 degrees C." "Cytarabine and doxorubicin hydrochloride showed decreasing stability at longer storage times and higher temperatures." So at body temperature the planning refill interval is 7 d (conservative) to 15 d.
- PMID 10282686 (Vyas 1987, Hosp Pharm 22:685-7): cytarabine minimum stability 8 days at room temperature in PVC bags.
NOT FOUND: a stability study of cytarabine in a SynchroMed II reservoir specifically; stability in CSF-like (pH 7.4 bicarbonate) at 37 C beyond the above. (Not yet searched: manufacturer labelling; Medtronic list of approved intrathecal drugs.)


VERIFIED (CSF kinetics of cytarabine: why bolus fails and what the dose must be)
- PMID 6896013 (Riccardi 1982, Cancer Res 42:1736-9, rhesus macaque; no DOI): "ara-C given alone was cleared from CSF with alpha and beta half-lives of 27.5 +/- 6.7 and 115.6 +/- 0.4 (S.D.) min, respectively" ; "mean calculated clearance rates for ara-C alone ... 7.5 ... ml/hr". So an intrathecal bolus is gone in ~2 h: bolus schedules cannot hold a cell-cycle-specific drug in the CSF; this is the quantitative reason the model asked for continuous delivery. Species: macaque (TRANSFER to dog).
- PMID 1742843 (Scott-Moncrieff 1991, Cancer Chemother Pharmacol 29:13-8, doi 10.1007/BF00686329) DOG, measured: "During a 12-h infusion of ara-C (50 mg m-2 h-1), the plasma steady-state concentration was 14.1 +/- 4.2 microM, the CSF steady-state concentration was 8.3 +/- 1.1 microM, and the CSF: plasma ratio was 0.62 +/- 0.14." After 600 mg/m2 bolus: "The peak concentration of ara-C in the CSF was 29 +/- 11 microM ... The CSF elimination half-life was 113 +/- 26 min." Healthy male dogs (n=10), "No clinically significant toxicity was observed over a 21-day period". THIS IS THE KEY EXISTING SUBSTITUTE FOR THE PUMP in the CSF (not the parenchyma): IV cytarabine CRI already gives CSF ~8 uM in dogs. MEASURED, dog, normal BBB.
- PMID 37670479 (Zwueste 2023, J Vet Intern Med 37:2429-2442, doi 10.1111/jvim.16842) DOG, measured: oral cytarabine ocfosfate (lipophilic prodrug) 200 mg/m2 PO q24h x7 in 4 healthy dogs: "Maximum serum concentration (Cmax) for Ara-C was 456.1-724.0 ng/mL (1.88-2.98 uM) and terminal half-life was 23.3 to 29.4 hours. Cerebrospinal fluid: serum Ara-C ratios ranged from 0.54 to 1.2." "Incorporation of Ara-CTP ... remained >25% of peak concentration at 13 days." An existing sustained-exposure cytarabine in dogs that reaches CSF; but serum 1.9-3.0 uM is ~3-4x lower than the IV CRI steady state (14 uM). Availability (veterinary compounding / Japan) NOT verified in PubMed.
- PMID 27641693 (Early 2016, J Vet Pharmacol Ther 40:411-4, doi 10.1111/jvp.12360): dogs with MUE, CRI 25 mg/m2/h for 8 h: "mean peak concentration (Cmax) and AUC ... 1.70 +/- 0.66 ug/mL and 11.39 +/- 3.37 h.ug/mL" (CSF not measured). 
- PMID 27240905 (Lowrie 2016, Vet J 213:1-5, doi 10.1016/j.tvjl.2016.03.026): MUO dogs, 100 mg/m2 CRI over 24 h vs SC bolus: "Survival at 3 months was 22/39 (44%) with SC delivery versus 37/41 (90%) with CRI." Outcome evidence that CRI beats bolus for a CNS-target in dogs (non-neoplastic disease though; TRANSFER of "CRI > bolus" principle).
- PMID 31769013 (Guillen 2019, Aust Vet J 98:69-78, doi 10.1111/avj.12895): 26 dogs with high-grade NHL with BM or CNS involvement, single cytarabine CRI within CEOP: "Gastrointestinal toxicity occurred in 17 dogs (65.3%), with 5 (19.2%) experiencing grade III or IV toxicity. Neutropenia occurred in nine dogs (34.6%)... Three dogs (11.5%) had thrombocytopenia".
- PMID 10677637 (Groothuis 2000, Brain Res 856:281-90, doi 10.1016/s0006-8993(99)02089-2), rat (one dog infused by CED): "After IT and IVT administration, tissue levels were high at the brain and ventricular surfaces, but declined exponentially into brain... These studies suggest that delivery of AraC to brain parenchyma by the IV, IT or IVT routes will be subtherapeutic." CAVEAT: an intrathecal/ventricular cytarabine pump treats leptomeningeal/CSF-space disease, NOT parenchymal disease; for parenchyma the evidence says CED or systemic high-dose.

Human reservoir/IT experience (verified)
- PMID 33101460 (Seidel 2020, Ther Adv Neurol Disord 13, doi 10.1177/1756286420951087): 43 elderly PCNSL pts, Ommaya + ICV methotrexate/prednisolone/AraC: "No Ommaya reservoir infection was observed. Complications of ICV treatment were pericatheter leucencephalopathy in two patients and surgical scar dehiscence with cerebrospinal fluid leak in one patient." PFS 16 mo.
- PMID 33324883 (Seidel 2019, Neurol Res Pract 1:17, doi 10.1186/s42466-019-0024-2): "Ommaya reservoir infection occurred in 3/33 patients (9%)"; 98 PCNSL pts, median PFS 11.4 mo.
- PMID 38678195 (Masrour 2024, BMC Neurol 24:143, doi 10.1186/s12883-024-03647-7), HUMAN intrathecal baclofen pumps meta-analysis: "In 501 ITB implantations, 203 medical complications were reported, including ... 33 infections (16.26%), eight meningitis (3.94%), and 16 cerebrospinal fluid leaks (7.88%) ... 75 catheter and pump complications". (Percentages are of complications, not of implants: 33/501 = 6.6% infections per implant by arithmetic from the same sentence.)
- PMID 32929856 (Goel 2020, Neuromodulation 24:126-134, doi 10.1111/ner.13263): 11,041 admissions for chronic-pain spinal device infections 2005-2014; "meningitis, abscess ... 4.93%, 5.08%".
- Liposomal (sustained-release) cytarabine, human: PMID 31637444 (Le Rhun 2020, Neuro Oncol 22:524-538, doi 10.1093/neuonc/noz201) DEPOSEIN randomized, 73 pts breast LM: "median LM-PFS was 2.2 months ... control versus 3.8 months ... experimental group (hazard ratio 0.61 ...)"; OS 4.0 vs 7.3 mo, not significant. PMID 21229289 (Brion 2011, Invest New Drugs 30:1697-702, doi 10.1007/s10637-011-9632-6): 20 pts with leukaemic or lymphomatous meningitis, "9 were alive and in complete remission at the end of the study. Median survival from the time of the first injection was 22.7 months". PMID 26827696 (Mack 2016 review): "Liposomal cytarabine shows advantages in terms of longer injection intervals, a sufficient distribution in the entire subarachnoid space after lumbar administration". WITHDRAWAL of DepoCyt: NOT FOUND in PubMed (search "DepoCyt withdrawn market discontinued" = 0 hits); to be checked outside PubMed.
- PMID 29997626 (Schultze-Florey 2018, Front Immunol 9:1475, doi 10.3389/fimmu.2018.01475) human CNS PTLD: "Complete remission was achieved by repeated intravenous and intrathecal anti-CD20 antibody rituximab administration combined with intrathecal chemotherapy (methotrexate, cytarabine, prednisone) ... Continuous complete remission was confirmed 27 months after initial diagnosis."
NOT FOUND: any report of continuous (multi-day) intrathecal cytarabine via implanted programmable pump in humans (PubMed "continuous intrathecal cytarabine infusion" returned 21 hits, none an IT pump report; top 10 were IV/ALL/Burkitt protocols). Existing human practice is intermittent bolus via Ommaya/lumbar puncture (twice weekly induction), or liposomal q2 weeks.

## 3. Intraventricular / intrathecal rituximab and anti-CD20 antibodies

HUMAN (verified)
- PMID 17312328 (Rubenstein 2007, J Clin Oncol 25:1350-6, doi 10.1200/JCO.2006.09.7311): Phase I, 10 pts, "nine injections of rituximab (10 mg, 25 mg, or 50 mg dose levels) through an Ommaya reservoir over 5 weeks"; "The maximum tolerated dose was determined to be 25 mg and rapid craniospinal axis distribution was demonstrated. Cytologic responses were detected in six patients; four patients exhibited complete response."
- PMID 24190981 (Kadoch 2014, Clin Cancer Res 20:1029-41, doi 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-13-0474): "Complement C3 and C5b-9 were reproducibly activated in CSF after intraventricular rituximab"; three-compartment PK model built (numbers not in abstract; full text not read).
- PMID 24054402 (Birnbaum 2013, J Clin Neurosci 21:356-8, doi 10.1016/j.jocn.2013.03.033): one pt, lymphomatous meningitis "controlled for more than 6 years with intraventricular rituximab. Apart from two infections of the Ommaya reservoir, no severe side effects".
- PMID 12241117 (Pels 2002, J Neurooncol 59:213-6, doi 10.1023/a:1019999830455): IV + IVT rituximab (10, 40, 25, 25 mg); "total clearing of lymphoma cells in the cerebrospinal fluid"; "Reversible side effects such as nausea, chills and hypotension were observed only immediately after intraventricular administration of 40 mg".
- PMID 11697503 (Harjunpaa 2001, Leuk Lymphoma 42:731-8, doi 10.3109/10428190109099335): IV rituximab: "In CSF the rituximab concentration ... remained below 0.55 microg/ml (compared to a Cmax of 400 microg/ml in peripheral blood)... an intact blood-brain barrier restricts the entry of rituximab into the CNS."
- PMID 29703403 (Szczepanek 2018, Neurol Neurochir Pol 52:410-4, doi 10.1016/j.pjnns.2018.01.007): intraventricular rituximab + methotrexate in SCNSL case.
CANINE
- PMID 38662527 (McLinden 2024, J Vet Intern Med 38:1666-74, doi 10.1111/jvim.17080): 42 client-owned dogs, anti-CD20 1E4-cIgGB + doxorubicin: "median fraction of baseline at 7 days = 0.04 ... at 21 days = 0.01" CD21+ B cells; "at Day 196, only 6/17 dogs (35%) remaining on the study had CD21+ counts >0.5 of baseline"; "well tolerated with only 1 dog exhibiting a hypersensitivity event".
- PMID 25764941 (Rue 2015, Vet Immunol Immunopathol 164:148-59, doi 10.1016/j.vetimm.2015.02.004): "The in vivo half-life of 1E4-cIgGB in a healthy dog was ~14 days"; "1E4-cIgGB and 1E4-cIgGC significantly deplete B-cell levels in healthy beagle dogs".
- Intrathecal/intraventricular use of any canine anti-CD20 antibody: NOT FOUND in PubMed.
- Blontress licensing status: NOT in PubMed; see web check below.

## 2b. DepoCyt (liposomal cytarabine) in dogs, and its status (PubMed + web)
- PMID 32442344 (Vazquez Fuster 2020, J Vet Intern Med 34:1563-9, doi 10.1111/jvim.15809): 5 healthy beagles, DepoCyt 50 mg/m2 SC: "Subcutaneous LC administration resulted in a maximum plasma concentration of 26.3 to 59.78 ng/mL ... plasma bioavailability (%F) of 19.6% to 31.3%"; "is apparently safe and well tolerated, but does not result in prolonged cytotoxic plasma concentrations." The paper states DepoCyt "lasts longer in plasma and CSF compared with free CA" and "The use of LC has not been evaluated in dogs" (before this). So SC route in dogs is a dead end; INTRATHECAL liposomal cytarabine in dogs: NOT FOUND.
- Web (WebSearch result snippets, not primary regulatory documents; genengnews.com and biopharmadive.com "Pacira halts production of Depocyt"): "Pacira discontinued production of DepoCyt (U.S. and Canada) and DepoCyte (E.U.) in June 2017 due to persistent technical issues specific to the DepoCyt(e) manufacturing process"; "DepoCyte was withdrawn from the EMA ... on July 10, 2018 on request from the manufacturer for commercial reasons." Conclusion: the sustained-release IT cytarabine product that exists in human data is NOT obtainable; (my search for a current replacement was not completed; see end).

## 4. Cell therapies that exist in dogs today

AUTOLOGOUS EX VIVO EXPANDED T CELLS (adoptive T-cell transfer, no gene engineering)
- PMID 34950726 (Gareau 2021, Front Vet Sci 8:787373, doi 10.3389/fvets.2021.787373): NC State, 10 dogs with B-cell LSA: CHOP + autologous peripheral-blood stem-cell transplant (autoPBHSCT) + ACT; "CD3+ CD8+ and CD3+CD4+ T-cells were expanded from the peripheral blood at a commercial laboratory. Two to four ACT infusions were given to each dog, with a total of 23 infusions given. Infusions were administered with no complications or adverse events. The median cell dose for all infusions was 5.62 x 10^8 cells/kg"; "4/10 (40%) of dogs were cured of their disease (defined as disease-free for >=2 years post-autoPBHSCT)"; "did not lead to a statistically significant increase in median disease-free interval and overall survival when compared to dogs who received CHOP chemotherapy and autoPHSCT alone." => EXISTS in client-owned dogs (academic, with commercial expansion lab); no demonstrated benefit of the T cells over transplant alone.
- PMID 38695516 (Benedict 2024, Vet Pathol 61:765-70, doi 10.1177/03009858241249114): "94 dogs with multicentric lymphoma received a hematopoietic cell transplant following total body irradiation at North Carolina State University ... during a 10-year period. Seven of these 94 dogs (7%) died prior to discharge." So TBI + HCT is a service that has been delivered to ~94 client-owned dogs at one centre (OUTCOME/availability: referral centre).
- PMID 3887690 (Transplantation 1985, 'Marrow transplant studies in dogs with malignant lymphoma', doi 10.1097/00007890-198505000-00008; authors and pages not recorded): 95 dogs with lymphoma in chemo remission: "Among 38 dogs treated with 8.4 Gy delivered at 4 cGy/min, 9 (24%) became long-term disease-free survivors. Ten of the 38 (26%) died of transplant-related complications and the actuarial relapse rate was approximately 65%." Higher-dose TBI (13.5 Gy) "increased incidence of transplant-related deaths (55% vs 26%) and did not reduce the actuarial relapse rate".
ALLOGENEIC INVARIANT NKT
- PMID 37852175 (Rotolo 2023, Cell Rep Med 4:101241, doi 10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101241): "Optimal allo-iNKTs transferred into MHC-mismatched dogs remain detectable for at least 78 days"; dog is "an established large-animal model" (healthy-dog experiment, trial-stage; no lymphoma efficacy).
CANINE CAR-T (trial only; none commercial)
- PMID 32329214 (Vet Comp Oncol 2020, 'Optimization of canine CD20 CAR T cell manufacturing and in vitro cytotoxic activity against B-cell lymphoma', doi 10.1111/vco.12602; authors not recorded): "there is no clinically available canine CAR-T cell therapy" (2020 statement).
- PMID 32002286 (Panjwani 2020, Oncoimmunology, doi 10.1080/2162402X.2019.1676615): 5 dogs, CD20 CAR T (mouse scFv, canine 4-1BB/CD3z): "Circulating CAR T cells were detectable post-infusion, however, induction of canine anti-mouse antibodies (CAMA) was associated with CAR T cell loss. Specific selection pressure on CD20+ tumors was observed following CAR T cell therapy, culminating in antigen escape and emergence of CD20- disease. Patient survival times correlated with product expansion."
- PMID 41376156 (Goodman 2025, Mol Ther 34:2276-92, doi 10.1016/j.ymthe.2025.12.010): "In a first-in-canine trial, anti-CD20 CART with canine 4-1BB-CD3z (cBBz) domains induced CD20-negative lymphoma outgrowth but did not persist or deplete B cells." Human BBz-signalling canine CARs "demonstrate superior therapeutic function" (in vitro killing and a canine B-cell leukemia mouse xenograft).
- PMID 35898541 (Atherton 2022, Front Vet Sci 9:824982, doi 10.3389/fvets.2022.824982): one dog, autologous CAR-T, CRS 3 days after infusion (IL-6, MCP-1, IFN-g, IL-10 up).
- PMID 42480604 (Peng 2026, Mol Cancer Ther, doi 10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-26-0365): "We developed canine TCARs directed against CD19 and CD20 ... TCAR-engineered canine T cells effectively and specifically eliminate cells expressing CD19 and/or CD20" (in vitro; not yet in dogs per abstract).
- PMID 39314237 (Yoshimoto 2024, iScience 27:110863, doi 10.1016/j.isci.2024.110863): PD-1/CD28 switch receptor augments canine CAR-T in vitro; "paves the way for studies in canine BCL patients".
- PMID 41847725 (Li 2026, Vet Comp Oncol 24:370-81, doi 10.1111/vco.70061): rabbit anti-canine CD19 mAb + CD19 CAR sequence "successfully transduced into Jurkat cells" (groundwork only).
- PMID 38573683 (Rotolo & Atherton 2024, Clin Cancer Res 30:2359-69, doi 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-23-3690): review: "issues related to autologous CAR T-cell manufacturing, limited CAR T-cell persistence, and tumor antigen escape, mirrored challenges observed in human CAR T trials."
- Solid-tumour canine CAR-T (B7-H3, PMID 35405743, 38554158; HER2 CAR-TIL, PMID 36765608) exist in pet dogs/xenografts: not lymphoma.
- Canine CD19 CAR-T in a dog with lymphoma: NOT FOUND (only construct/antibody work, PMID 41847725; TCAR in vitro, PMID 42480604). FMC63 (human anti-CD19 scFv) cross-reactivity with canine CD19: NOT FOUND (0 PubMed hits).
- Canine NK adoptive transfer clinical in lymphoma: NOT FOUND. Only phenotyping (PMID 27933061: NCR1/NKp46 canine NK subsets, "canine CD3-/NKp46+ cells kill canine tumor cell lines without prior sensitization"; PMID 37672843 canine lymphoma NK ligands) and NK exosomes in mice (PMID 34527741). Allogeneic gamma-delta: not searched.
- T-cell antigens: CD7/CD5 CAR-T in dogs: NOT FOUND (see section 7 for human CD7).

## 5. CNS-penetrant licensed / off-label drugs in dogs and canine CNS lymphoma outcomes

Canine CNS lymphoma outcomes (all verified)
- PMID 30022475 (LaRue 2018, J Small Anim Pract 59:547-52, doi 10.1111/jsap.12868): 18 dogs, 7 institutions: "A total of 15 of 18 dogs received specific treatment other than prednisone ... the median survival time was 171 days (range 1 to 1942 days)." "Dogs receiving any type of treatment for central nervous system lymphoma lived longer than ... historical reports." Mixed modalities; no regimen-level separation.
- PMID 37756055 (Mizutani 2023, Vet Sci 10:533, doi 10.3390/vetsci10090533): 2 dogs, nimustine (ACNU, IV) + prednisolone: "complete or nearly complete remission ... long-term survival (>2583 days and 1218 days), but with problematic adverse effects"; "dose of nimustine ... 25-30 mg/m2 every 3-4 weeks for a total of four times". Nimustine "mainly used to treat human intracranial neoplasia because of its high permeability across the blood-brain barrier". n=2, case level; nimustine is licensed for humans in Japan (not US/EU); availability to a US dog: NOT established.
- PMID 37732143 (Lyseight 2023, doi 10.3389/fvets.2023.1209935): one dog; cisternal intrathecal cytarabine + methotrexate with dexamethasone gave "a short-lasting CNS clinical remission (3 weeks)"; euthanised 9 weeks after diagnosis. This is the only canine IT cytarabine report found and it shows both feasibility (cisterna magna access) and bolus duration (3.5 weeks, consistent with CSF half-life ~2 h and no sustained exposure).
- PMID 22210944 (Seo 2011, Can Vet J 52:778-83): three dogs with CNS relapse during chemotherapy; abstract reports diagnosis, risk factors and "treatment options are discussed" only: the abstract does NOT report a cytarabine CRI result. (The task brief listed this PMID as a cytarabine CRI source; the abstract does not support that.)
- PMID 36899719 (Neuropathology 2023, Animals 13:862, doi 10.3390/ani13050862): 45 canine nervous-system lymphomas; forebrain most frequent location in dogs (descriptive).
- PMID 33769594 (J Small Anim Pract 2021): CNS lymphoma with choroid-plexus involvement, 5 dogs and 1 cat, 4 of 5 CSF samples diagnostic (descriptive; relevant to ventricular/CSF access: choroid plexus is a lymphoma site).
- PMID 37265807 (Can Vet J 2023): spermatic-cord B-cell lymphoma relapsing to brain at 5 months (already in the record as the testis-to-brain case).
Drugs
- Cytarabine: see section 2 (IV CRI 50 mg/m2/h x12 h gives CSF 8.3 +/- 1.1 uM, CSF:plasma 0.62; PMID 1742843). Toxicity PMID 31769013 above. Oral ocfosfate PK PMID 37670479.
- Lomustine (CCNU; the task brief states it is licensed for dogs; I did not verify the licence in PubMed or on the web): PMID 38890811 (Catalucci 2024, Vet Comp Oncol 22:422-8, doi 10.1111/vco.12990) first-line lomustine+prednisolone, n=30: "overall response rate (RR) was 87% ... median TTP, MDFI and MST were 42, 63 and 90 days". PMID 41914636 (Machado 2026, Vet Comp Oncol 24:382-92, doi 10.1111/vco.70062) 70 dogs with mediastinal lymphoma (95.6% T-cell), lomustine- or anthracycline-based: "Median progression free survival (PFS) was 132 days ... median overall survival time (OST) was 223 days"; "Differences in outcome between anthracycline- or lomustine-based chemotherapy protocols were not statistically significant." ## 6. Radiation in dogs with lymphoma (all verified)
- PMID 42525883 (Colosi 2026, J Vet Intern Med 40(4), doi 10.1093/jvimsj/aalag144): L-CHOP + low-dose-rate (6 Gy; 8-14 cGy/min) sequential half-body RT without maintenance, 75 dogs vs 115 controls: "Median PFS and OS for the L-CHOP-LDR-HBRT group were 532 days (95% CI, 389-782) and 752 days (95% CI, 526-963) ... controls (296 days ... and 457 days ...)". "grade 3 and 4 toxicities occurring in 4.5% of all administered treatments" (AE data for 30 dogs); "A delay in irradiating the caudal half negatively affected PFS and OS."

## 12. Bottom line for the model (strength shown, no odds)
1. The "to build" pump is the weakest of the four as an invention claim and the strongest as an engineering one: every component has a real precedent (device in dogs, ventricular/cisternal access in dogs, drug CSF kinetics in dogs, drug stability at 37 C), and the nearest drug-delivery stand-in that exists today without any device is IV cytarabine CRI (CSF 8.3 uM, measured in dogs) or oral cytarabine ocfosfate. What is not in the record is: cytarabine in a programmable pump in a dog, chronic IT cytarabine safety in dogs, and any parenchymal reach (the rat data say IT/IVT cytarabine is subtherapeutic in brain tissue).
2. Item (a) and (b) share one dependency: a canine CAR-T that persists. The only measured canine persistence data are negative or short (weeks); the fixes are published as in vitro/xenograft results. In humans, systemic CAR-T already reaches the CSF, so (b) may not need a separate route if (a) is solved; the intraventricular human data show a 32% per-infusion neuro-inflammation rate.
3. Item (d) has no dog substitute with a kill number; the existing dog agents for T-cell disease have short PFS (64-292 d in the series above), so this remains the least-supported item and the one closest to invention.
4. Corrections to earlier notes: Blontress is not purchasable (license voided 2017, web source); DepoCyt is gone (2017/2018, web source); PMID 22210944 does not report a cytarabine CRI; PMID 31124198's dog pump data concern morphine granulomas, not cytarabine safety.
5. Items I could not verify with a primary source are labelled WEB-SOURCED or UNVERIFIED above; the model should not grade them above ASSUMED until checked.
