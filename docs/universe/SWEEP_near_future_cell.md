### 2.2 Large-animal and DOG evidence for in-vivo CAR

- **DOG (NEW, important):** VivoVec UB-VV100 (anti-CD3 scFv-displaying cocal-pseudotyped lentivirus), J Immunother Cancer 2023, PMID 36918221 (doi 10.1136/jitc-2022-006292; PMC10016276 full text read). Healthy beagles got a GFP-payload version: 4e8 TU ultrasound-guided bilateral inguinal **lymph-node** injection, or 4e9 TU intraperitoneal. By ISH, ">70% to >99%" of transduction events in node samples were canine CD45+ immune cells (T cells CD3+ and macrophages CD68+); canine T cells were transduced in inguinal/iliac nodes and spleen at 1 week; "no tissue pathology". Caveat from the authors: the anti-HUMAN CD3 scFv does not cross-react with canine CD3, so transduction in dogs was by the cocal envelope (untargeted), not T-cell-targeted. So: a lentiviral in-vivo route demonstrably transduces canine lymphoid T cells in situ; a canine-T-cell-targeting binder (anti-canine CD3 or CD8) has to replace the human anti-CD3. No CAR payload, no efficacy tested in dogs. Readiness for dogs: D (no CAR in-dog), human platform C.
- NHP: INT2104 (CD7 binder + CAR20) in cynomolgus macaque and mouse: CAR-T (CD4, CD8) and CAR-NK generated and CD20+ B cells depleted after single IV dose (PMID 40581818, doi 10.1016/j.ymthe.2025.06.036; abstract read). Humans later dosed (INVISE).
- Canine anti-CD3 binders that could serve as the targeting moiety exist: mouse anti-canine CD3 mAb used for T-cell expansion (PMID 12679562, 2003), rabbit anti-canine CD3e mAb HORCF-CD3.1 that "effectively induced T cell stimulation in vitro" (PMID 41427134, doi 10.3389/fvets.2025.1612069), anti-canine CD3 + CD28 nano-beads (PMID 33679741). Epitope mapping for an engager/targeting arm: NOT FOUND.

### 2.3 What human data give for expansion kinetics and the dose that gave CR (to convert to a per-day kill and a dose requirement)

Already in record (SWEEP_cart_kill): Kimmel 2021 kmax 0.34/0.7/1.15 per day, C50 2e9 cells for ~90 kg, doubling time 0.78 d, peak d10-14; Kalos UPN03 1.4e7 CART19 with ~1e12 CLL cells cleared, expansion >1000x.
NEW, verified in abstract:
- **Ultralow-dose IL-10-armoured CD19 CAR-T (META 10-19), R/R DLBCL, JAMA Oncol 2026, PMID 42490071 (doi 10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2490):** Flu/Cy then **2e3, 5e3 or 2e4 CAR-T per kg**; 13 treated; ORR 92.3%, **CR 84.6% (11/13)**; CRS 12/13 (1 grade 3), ICANS 2 (grade 1, 2); "median peak expansion 660.7 (30.7-10,562) cells/uL"; at data cutoff (median follow-up 15.6 mo) 5 in maintained CR, 7 relapsed/progressed (2 CD19-negative). Derivation (mine, arithmetic, flagged DERIVED): at 2e4/kg infused into ~70 mL blood/kg the starting concentration is ~0.3 cells/uL, so a peak of 660 cells/uL is a ~2,000-fold expansion; at 2e3/kg the fold is ~20,000. A human CR therefore needs expansion to ~1e1-1e3 cells/uL, not a large infused dose. Durability at 15.6 mo: 5/13 = 38% ongoing (follow-up short).
- Consequence for the model (inference, not measured): the canine doses already given (0.28-6.6e5 CAR/kg) are 14-330x HIGHER than 2e3-2e4/kg, yet no canine expansion/persistence occurred (CAR gone by day 14-28; CAMA from day 18). So the dog barrier is persistence/expansion (xeno scFv immunity; canine 4-1BB signalling), not dose. The model's statement that 0.12 /day "needs 6.5e7 cells" should be read as needing an EXPANDING product, consistent with SWEEP_cart_kill's own conclusion.
- Non-engineered effector comparator, human: CD19 CAR-NK, 1e5/1e6/1e7 cells/kg, 11 pts, ORR 73%, 7 CR (4 lymphoma, 3 CLL), no CRS/neurotoxicity/GVHD, persisted at low levels >=12 mo (NEJM 2020, PMID 32023374, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa1910607). CD19 BBz CAR-NK repeated dosing, 8 pts LBCL: ORR 62.5% at d30, CR 50%, median PFS 9.5 mo (Nat Cancer 2025, PMID 40251398, doi 10.1038/s43018-025-00940-3). iPSC FT596 CD19 CAR-NK Phase 1 (Lancet 2025, PMID 39798981, doi 10.1016/S0140-6736(24)02462-0): abstract truncated in my read; results not recorded here.

## 4. CD3 BISPECIFIC ENGAGERS

### 4.1 Canine-specific or cross-reactive CD3 constructs
- No canine CD3 x CD20 / CD19 engager, BiTE or caninized bispecific found (PubMed `canine/dog x CD3 x bispecific/engager/BiTE`: 4 records, none a canine engager; web search: same). Record agrees (SWEEP_bispecific 8, 11).
- NEW building-block facts: (a) rabbit anti-canine CD3e mAb HORCF-CD3.1 (Front Vet Sci 2025, PMID 41427134, doi 10.3389/fvets.2025.1612069): "effectively induced T cell stimulation in vitro as an anti-CD3 activator"; the authors present it as a component for bispecifics; epitope not mapped. (b) mouse anti-canine CD3 mAb with IL-2 for expansion (PMID 12679562) and anti-canine CD3/CD28 nano-beads (PMID 33679741): agonist anti-canine CD3 exists. (c) Canine CD28 antibody safety signal in dogs, PMID 25988188 (Transplant Direct 2015, doi 10.1097/TXD.0000000000000516): infusing mouse anti-canine CD28 mAb 1C6 IV into 4 dogs produced, within 1 h, leukopenia and a steroid-refractory cytokine storm (MCP-1, IP-10, IL-10, IL-6, TNF-a; fever, alveolar hemorrhage in one, GI hemorrhage in one), while the Fab fragment (2 dogs) was well tolerated. Reading: dogs mount a human-like CRS to a T-cell-agonist antibody; so a canine CD3 engager is expected to produce CRS (needs step-up dosing and a monovalent/Fab-like or effector-silent format); also evidence the mechanism class has real in-dog biology (not E). NEW.
- Human CD3 arms do not bind canine CD3 (43% ectodomain identity; record, SWEEP_bispecific 9). Unchanged.
- Canine CD20 binder side already exists and is in dogs (4E1-7-B_f, 1E4-cIgGB; record).

### 4.2 Human CNS data for CD20 x CD3 engagers (all already in record except as noted)

NEW (small): case reports/short series of glofitamab in R/R PCNSL, 2 pts both CR without CRS/ICANS (Hematol Oncol 2026, PMID 42319957, doi 10.1002/hon.70211); 1 pt CR (Leuk Lymphoma 2025, PMID 40174220); epcoritamab in CNS-involved DLBCL, 1 case (PMID 41867647); neurotoxicity of bispecifics "resembles CAR-T with a lower rate of ICANS" (review, PMID 42757517). Engager infections of the CNS: fatal HHV-6 encephalitis after mosunetuzumab (PMID 40625203) and PML after epcoritamab (PMID 39552980): case reports; relevant to the toxicity budget (opportunistic CNS infection), not quantified.
- Intrathecal engager administration in any species: NOT FOUND again (PubMed: epcoritamab/mosunetuzumab/glofitamab/blinatumomab x intrathecal/intraventricular/CSF: only intrathecal CHEMOTHERAPY alongside blinatumomab). A CSF:plasma figure other than glofitamab 0.44% max: NOT FOUND.
- Engager CNS effect despite 0.44% antibody penetration is explained by T-cell trafficking (CAR-T and engager efficacy are both effector-cell limited), consistent with the record's use of an OUTCOME-derived multiplier 0.2/0.5/0.85 rather than a concentration ratio.

### 4.3 Escape evidence for the engager row (NEW)
- Mosunetuzumab: in 32 paired progression biopsies, **CD20 loss in 11/32 (34%)**; baseline <10% CD20+ tumour cells in 16/293 (5.5%); CD20 maintained in 29/30 (97%) on-treatment biopsies; loss by reduced transcription or truncating mutation (Blood 2024, PMID 38048694, doi 10.1182/blood.2023022348). Together with the record's 59% after glofitamab (38720530), CD20 loss is the dominant measured engager escape (34-59% of relapses in biopsied series). No B2M/HLA-I loss as an engager-resistance mechanism was found in the clinical literature searched (see 6.3).
- Also: CD20 re-emergence after CD20-negative relapse (case, PMID 42131932); retained epcoritamab activity despite CD20-negative routine assay one half-life after mosunetuzumab (PMID 42816411, abstract not read). Implication for the model: an engager row should carry the CD20-loss escape as uncovered unless paired with a CD19-directed arm; dog CD20 loss after CD20 CAR-T also seen (32002286).

## 5. BISPECIFIC-ANTIBODY-ARMED ACTIVATED T CELLS (BATs, eBAT-like) IN LYMPHOMA/LEUKAEMIA

- No lymphoma or leukaemia BAT trial in any species found (PubMed: Lum LG[au] x bispecific/armed returned 30 records; CD20Bi/CD19Bi x armed ATC x lymphoma returned two preclinical papers). Confirms the record (SWEEP_bispecific 7).
- Preclinical, lymphoid/myeloid: CD20Bi-armed ATC kill CD20+ lines and "bypass complement-mediated rituximab resistance", not blocked by 8000-fold excess soluble rituximab (Exp Hematol 2005, PMID 15781336, doi 10.1016/j.exphem.2005.01.007; 51Cr assay; E:T 6.25-50; patient leukapheresis products usable); ipilimumab during expansion raises CD20Bi-armed ATC kill of Daudi by 27.5% (J Transl Med 2014, PMID 25008236); CD123/CD33GO BATs vs AML in NSG mice, survival prolonged (Front Immunol 2022, PMID 36389764, doi 10.3389/fimmu.2022.899468); BiAb-armed CD19 CAR-T (PMID 32449004). All preclinical. NEW citations (record had only the myeloma CD20 BAT trial 26827660 and solid-tumour trials).
- Human clinical-stage evidence for the MECHANISM (arming) exists in other tumours (record: EGFR, HER2, GD2 BATs, n=7-32; stable disease/OS 13-31 mo; no cure-level kill; CD20 BATs in myeloma n=12, CMPC reduction up to 58%).
- Dogs: no arming step found. The relevant dog precedent is UNARMED T cells: commercial autologous CD3/CD28-activated T cells + CHOP (Texas A&M/CAVU "PET 2.0", WEB) and 5.62e6 cells/kg with no adverse events in 10 dogs (PMID 34950726, record).
- eBAT (the EGF/uPA angiotoxin) is a different object, already excluded in the record for lymphoma on target-expression grounds. No change.
- Readiness: D for lymphoma (preclinical CD20-BAT; mechanism clinical-stage in solid tumours = C for the arming concept). Model number: none derivable (kill rate ASSUMED if used), consistent with the record's "not credited".

## 6. ALLOGENEIC CANINE HCT AND MHC/HLA LOSS

### 6.1 Canine allo HCT: availability and outcomes beyond PMID 35789057
Already in record (SWEEP_hct_model.md): the 15-dog allo series and its donor logistics (25% per sibling, no registry, centres, NCSU paused, cost), AATT human GvL, DLI, haplo lethal GVHD, RIC, 34950726 (auto + T cells, 4/10 disease-free >=2 y), 38695516, 42525883.
NEW:
- DLA diversity/availability (Cells 2023, PMID 36899945, doi 10.3390/cells12050809; 829 dogs, 59 breeds, Japan): 52 DLA-88/12/88L/DRB1 haplotypes found as homozygotes; **homozygosity rate 23.8% (198/829)**; haplotype diversity varies widely between breeds but is "relatively conserved within most breeds"; the authors estimate that homozygotes/heterozygotes carrying these haplotypes would benefit from 88-12/88L-DRB1-matched transplantation. Reading: within-breed unrelated DLA-identical donors are plausible for conserved-haplotype breeds, which would relax the "25% per sibling" limit; no registry exists (record). Japanese population; US breed data not found.
- DLA typing by sequencing predicts rejection: matched skin grafts survived to 21 d vs <=14 d for haplo/mismatched; MLR stimulation index higher in mismatched (P<0.001) (J Vet Med Sci 2020, PMID 32624549). DLA-88 NGS genotyping reveals many new alleles (PMID 36227705; not read).
- Non-myeloablative alternative: 211At-anti-CD45 (CD45-B10) conditioning + DLA-HAPLOIDENTICAL PBSC in 17 healthy dogs: all had donor chimerism by day 28 (27-100%), engraftment 100% but durability variable, no GVHD, 12/17 survived >30 d (J Nucl Med 2024, PMID 39025648, doi 10.2967/jnumed.124.267540). Research stage (D).
- Single-case outcome: DLA-matched sibling CD34+ allo-HCT after induction in a dog with acute large granular lymphocytic leukaemia: full donor chimerism within 2 weeks, stable >=2 years, alive and well (JAVMA 2015, PMID 25875671, doi 10.2460/javma.246.9.994). n=1, T/NK-lineage.
- Canine graft-versus-malignancy evidence beyond lymphoma outcome: UTY minor-H antigen CTLs from female dogs recognise and lyse DLA-identical male marrow/cells in MHC-I-restricted manner (Scand J Immunol 2013, PMID 23126655, doi 10.1111/sji.12011); in record only as the SMCY/SRY vaccine abstract (25965411). 
- Outcome data for allogeneic canine HCT in lymphoma beyond 35789057: none found (PubMed `dogs x transplant x lymphoma 2021-2026`: 7 records, all already in record or irrelevant).
- Tier: allo DLA-identical HCT stays A/B-limited (exists-today, centre- and donor-limited; no registry).

### 6.2 Human: MHC/HLA loss after allo-HSCT (frequency)
All frequencies below are for LEUKAEMIA after partially-HLA-MISMATCHED (haploidentical/related) transplants, where a mismatched haplotype can be deleted; none is a DLA/HLA-identical lymphoma series.
- ATG-based T-replete haplo-HSCT, 788 pts, 180 relapses, 106 screened: **HLA loss in 54 (50.9%)**; later than non-HLA-loss relapse (lymphoid median 323 vs 151 d; myeloid 321 vs 223 d); risk: chronic GVHD (OR 27.7) and MRD before relapse in lymphoid disease; preemptive DLI did not delay relapse in HLA-loss patients (JAMA Netw Open 2022, PMID 35385089, doi 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.6114). Record held 33% of relapses (23/69) after partially matched related HSCT (25371177) and 5/17 after haplo + donor T cells (19641204); this adds a 51% figure.
- HLA class II loss in 2 children with ALL after HLA-DR/DQ-mismatched related HSCT (PMID 26544669, doi 10.1002/pbc.25819); haplo-HSCT in adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma: allele-specific flow cytometry identifies HLA-loss relapse (Haematologica 2026, PMID 42750474; abstract not available, so no frequency).
- **HLA-matched setting:** in CLL after allo-HSCT (10 pts), "no selective advantage for HLA loss was observed, even when present in pretransplant subpopulations"; late relapses show neoantigen depletion and epigenetic change instead (Sci Transl Med 2020, PMID 32938797, doi 10.1126/scitranslmed.abb7661). This is the closest datum to a DLA-identical canine lymphoma: HLA loss is a mismatch-driven escape, not what selection acts on under full match in CLL (n=10; indirect). Record already held "for DLA-identical donors mismatched-haplotype loss does not apply".
- Frequency of MHC loss after allo in LYMPHOMA: still NOT FOUND.

### 6.3 Human: MHC/HLA loss after engager or CAR-T in lymphoma
- PubMed queries combining B2M/HLA/MHC-I loss with CAR-T or CD3 bispecifics in lymphoma/DLBCL returned no clinical relapse series (4 irrelevant records: preclinical immunogenicity, neuroblastoma review, allogeneic CAR design). Engager relapse mechanism in the clinical biopsies found is CD20 loss (34% mosunetuzumab, PMID 38048694; 59% glofitamab, PMID 38720530 in record), not MHC-I loss. CAR-T: record already holds antigen-loss share 28-63% and "MHC-independent"; HLA abnormalities 62% DLBCL / 77% PCNSL at diagnosis yet CR 50-57% (28507804 + outcomes) as indirect support.
- B2M loss predicts PD-1 inhibitor resistance in DLBCL (cell-line and IHC, PMID 42046553); not relevant to engager/CAR kill, relevant to vaccines (MHC-I-dependent).
- Summary: MHC/HLA loss is a measured escape for MHC-dependent effectors (allo-GvL after MISMATCHED transplant 33-51% of relapses; vaccines by mechanism), and is NOT reported as a relapse mechanism after CAR-T or CD3 engagers in lymphoma in anything I found. Strength: not found in PubMed titles/abstracts; per-patient B2M/HLA status after engagers is not reported.

## 7. NOTES ON THE RECORD (consistency checks raised by this sweep)
1. PMID 41376156 contains no new dog-trial data (see 1.1). If the model text cites it for dog doses/efficacy, that should read as "refers to the Penn first-in-canine trial (32002286)".
2. SWEEP_cart_kill says Flu/Cy was tolerated in dogs (35405743, 2 purpose-bred dogs); the 2024 CCR review states Flu/Cy is impractical in dog CAR-T trials because of cytopenias and transfusion needs. Penn is testing bendamustine (no results found).
3. Allogeneic/xenogeneic dog CAR-T trials exist (Penn, Minnesota); results unpublished. They would answer "can an off-the-shelf product persist in a dog" (iNKT 78 d in MHC-mismatched dogs, 37852175, is the only published datum).
4. In-vivo CAR-T needs no ex-vivo dose: human CRs occurred with expansion from 2e3-2e4/kg to ~660 cells/uL (42490071); dog doses given were higher and failed, so the model's CAR-T potency gate should be framed as an expansion/persistence gate, not a dose gate.
