# REGIONAL (CSF-DELIVERED) AGENTS FOR THE CANINE CNS LYMPHOMA SANCTUARY -- FINAL REPORT

Source: PubMed only (MCP), every PMID below was fetched this session (metadata; two full texts: PMC3944388, PMC5925833). "[abs]" = taken from the abstract only; I did not read the full text of those. Where the abstract did not contain a number I say so. Verified-citation working notes are in the appendix at the bottom of this file.

## 0. Bar I am grading against (quoted, CLAUDE.md rule 2)
"make sure every mechanism and every escape is closed by either real data or rigorous model, potency, toxicity etc all need to be considered"; "looking for 10+ years of durability"; "assuming early detection"; "I'm okay with no specific data but if scientifically sound" (transfer allowed if justified in writing and graded TRANSFER; no-basis numbers stay ASSUMED); "I'm not asking if it's been demonstrated, I know it's not." So "not demonstrated in dogs" is NOT reported as a finding below. Grades: MEASURED (measured in the target disease/species), DERIVED (arithmetic on verified numbers), TRANSFER (another species/disease/class, basis written), OUTCOME (clinical outcome only, no mechanism-level number), ASSUMED.

## 1. Bottom line (strength actually shown)
1. The access problem is the part that regional delivery genuinely changes, and it is changed by a large factor with human data behind it: IV rituximab reaches CSF at ~0.1% of serum (PMID 12393404, 11697503; project's 0.002 is consistent), while an intraventricular dose is delivered into the CSF itself (25 mg into a fitted 62.2 mL CSF compartment = ~400 ug/mL, DERIVED) and produced rapid depletion of CSF lymphoma cells "within hours" with complement activation measured in CSF (PMID 24190981, full text). For the CSF-facing compartment, access goes from ~0.002 to ~1.0 (TRANSFER, human CSF PK -> dog; needs dose scaling by CSF volume, dog CSF volume NOT FOUND).
2. Two of the five levers satisfy the user's criteria on mechanism (non-division-gated, not a pump substrate): antibody (CDC/ADCC) and effector cells. The other two (cytarabine depot, glucocorticoid) are either division-gated plus dCK-dependent (ara-C) or lose to receptor loss (GC), so they cannot close the persister escape however well delivered. A third class found incidentally, intraventricular 131I-antibody (compartmental radioimmunotherapy), also satisfies the criteria and has the best human regional-delivery durability signal I found (neuroblastoma, not lymphoma).
3. What is NOT shown anywhere: (a) any kill rate against a quiescent, pump-high lymphoid progenitor from any of these agents (every number is bulk-cell response; persister kill stays ASSUMED/TRANSFER-by-mechanism); (b) any 10-year durability for CSF-directed therapy (longest human IT-containing data: 4.8 y median OS in one retrospective cohort, 3-y PFS 63% in a 19-patient regimen that also used strong systemic chemo; typical CSF response durations are months); (c) any dog data for IVT/IT antibody, IVT/IT CAR-T/effector cells, Ommaya in dogs, or IT therapy of canine lymphoma with outcome. Dog data that DO exist: IT cytarabine +/- MTX bolus safety in 112 dogs; IT cell suspensions (MSC) safe in dogs; chronic lumbar port patency in lab dogs; IT large proteins reach canine brain parenchyma; CSF ara-C PK after IV in dogs.
4. Parenchyma is the weak point for every CSF-delivered agent except (possibly) antibodies and cells; see section 8. Canine DLBCL pattern is meningeal + perivascular + periventricular (PMID 27511313), i.e. CSF-facing, which favours the regional route; intravascular lymphoma (PMID 36329600) is not CSF-reachable.
5. Single binding constraints for the model if regional delivery is adopted: (i) antigen status of the persister (CD20 loss documented in dogs after CD20 CAR-T, PMID 32002286 [abs]); (ii) exposure duty (IVT antibody sits in CSF ~1 day per dose; CAR-T persists ~1 week in CSF at best in humans, ~24 h in NHP); (iii) parenchymal access (unmeasured); (iv) chronic-access hazards (infection, pericatheter injury) over a decade (no decade-scale data exists).

## 2. Modality 1: intraventricular / intrathecal anti-CD20 antibody (rituximab; canine antibodies)
Verified human data
- Phase 1, intraventricular rituximab monotherapy via Ommaya, n=10, doses 10/25/50 mg, 9 injections over 5 wk (PMID 17312328, JCO 2007) [abs]: MTD 25 mg; "rapid craniospinal axis distribution was demonstrated"; cytologic responses 6, complete 4 (of 10); intraocular improvement 2; parenchymal NHL resolution 1; Pim-2 and FoxP1 RNA high in refractory meningeal lymphoma.
- Phase 1 rituximab + MTX, n=14, 10 or 25 mg twice weekly x4 wk (PMID 23197589, Blood 2013) [abs]: >150 doses, no serious toxicity; cytologic CR 75%; overall CR in CSF and/or parenchyma 43%.
- Children, n=25, 163 IT/IVT doses, median 25 mg (PMID 26872652) [abs]: CNS remission 72% (18/25) with or without other CNS therapy; gr1-2 neuropathy 20% (5), allergy 2, headache 2, gr3 neuropathy 1, seizure 2.
- HOVON 80 (PMID 27530779) [abs]: secondary CNS lymphoma n=36 (24 with parenchymal disease): ORR 53%, CR 22%, 1-y PFS 19%, 1-y OS 25% with IT rituximab inside R-DHAP/HD-MTX then ASCT.
- Rituximab + liposomal ara-C via Ommaya, n=14 (PMID 18820836) [abs]: 10 PR, 4 PD, no CR; median response duration 4.0 months (1-6); median survival ~5 months.
- R-IDARAM + IT rituximab/MTX/dex/ara-C, n=19 PCNSL (PMID 27029056) [abs]: CR 89%, 3-y PFS 63.2%, 3-y OS 84.2%, median follow-up 39 mo (systemic regimen confounds).
- Case-level: MCL meningeal CR sustained 23 months after IT rituximab + ASCT (PMID 18766006); HBV reactivation after IT rituximab monotherapy (PMID 21150178).
Mechanism / PK numbers (PMID 24190981, full text)
- CSF C3a des-Arg baseline mean 28.56 ng/ml, peak mean 74.6 ng/ml at 1-2 h, back to baseline by 24 h; C5b-9 formed; B-lymphoma cell depletion in CSF within hours (flow at 1 h). So complement IS active in lymphoma CSF. Monocytes (CD14+) in CSF make C3; high baseline C3a predicted shorter OS (paradox, authors' hypothesis: C3b impairs NK/ADCC).
- 3-compartment popPK (n=7): CSF V1 62.2 mL, serum V2 9,250 mL; CSF->serum 3.57/day, CSF->brain tissue 2.61/day, Q 208 mL/day, CLd(brain) 189 mL/day, V3 320 mL, serum t1/2 21.1 d. Brain penetration is inferred from biphasic CSF decay; authors: "additional studies are required to directly quantify brain tissue penetration". LLOQ 0.25 ug/mL CSF.
- Monkey: terminal CSF t1/2 4.96 h, no neuro toxicity (PMID 12393404). NHP IT human anti-Nogo-A: CSF t1/2 6.4 h, serum t1/2 13.7 d, parenchymal penetration with target binding, no toxicity at 75-150 mg x repeat (PMID 39572304) [abs].
- Rat Raji CNS lymphoma: IVT rituximab ALONE gave no survival benefit (n=13); rituximab + human serum did (n=12, P=0.049) (PMID 20820968) [abs] => complement supply can be limiting in a non-inflamed CNS.
Dog antibody context (systemic only): canine anti-CD20 1E4-cIgGB + doxorubicin, 42 dogs, B cells to 0.04 of baseline at d7 (PMID 38662527); afucosylated 4E1-7-B_f + CHOP, 13 dogs, CR 13/13, median PFS 340 d, 2-y survival 38.9% (PMID 41742528); 4E1-7-B_f is a type II (ADCC-strong) antibody, rituximab type I (CDC-dominant) (PMID 40577406) [abs].
Numbers for the model
- (i) CNS access: CSF-facing compartment 1.0 per dose (TRANSFER). Time-covered: derived CSF exit rate 6.18/day => initial exit t1/2 2.7 h (ignores brain->CSF return flux, not retrieved); mixing 25 mg into 62.2 mL gives ~402 ug/mL falling to ~0.83 ug/mL at 24 h (DERIVED; at/above the 0.25 ug/mL LLOQ). So ~1 day of coverage per dose; twice-weekly dosing = duty ~2/7 = 0.29. Systemic comparator 0.14% (0.55/400 ug/mL; PMID 11697503). Parenchymal access: no measured value (see sec. 8). Spill-over: ~all of an IVT dose exits to serum (25 mg/9,250 mL = ~2.7 ug/mL DERIVED), so IVT dosing also gives systemic anti-CD20 exposure.
- (ii) Per-day kill (transfer logic): bulk lymphoma cells in CSF disappear within hours at antibody peak, so bulk kill during exposure is >> 1/day (inference from "within hours", cytology detection limit unknown; OUTCOME-grade). At duty 0.29 the time-averaged kill needed to reach the 0.10/day bar is 0.35/day during exposure (0.10/0.286, DERIVED). Weaknesses: persister-specific kill never measured; assumes persister is CD20+ and complement-competent; CDC/ADCC are not cell-cycle gated and not pump substrates (mechanistic argument, TRANSFER from systemic antibody biology, not demonstrated for quiescent progenitors). Pim-2/FoxP1-high cells resist (PMID 17312328). Rat model shows complement can be limiting.
- (iii) Persistence window: CSF ~1 day/dose; maintenance requires repeated dosing; 5-week induction is the longest tested schedule found. No pump/infusion data for mAb (NOT FOUND). Canine antibodies keep systemic B cells depleted >200 d (systemic only).
- (iv) Toxicity axis CNS_LOCAL: neuropathy gr1-2 20% and gr3 4% (1/25), seizure 8% (2/25) in children (PMID 26872652); no serious toxicity in >150 adult doses (PMID 23197589). Immune-mediated/systemic axis: HBV reactivation case; anti-drug antibodies (dogs given human proteins IT make anti-drug antibodies that blunt efficacy, PMID 22402327 [abs]; canine anti-mouse antibodies killed CD20 CAR-T, PMID 32002286) => repeated IT dosing in dogs needs a canine/caninised antibody.
- (v) Availability: rituximab human, licensed IV, IT use is off-label; canine anti-CD20 antibodies are veterinary-trial stage and have only been given IV; IT/IVT canine antibody: buildable (dose by puncture or reservoir), nothing published.
- (vi) Escapes: covers pump, quiescence (by mechanism), dCK, MHC loss (CDC/ADCC are MHC-independent). Does NOT cover CD20 loss (documented in canine DLBCL after CD20 CAR-T, PMID 32002286 [abs]), CD20-low (dose-density dependent, no number retrieved: NOT FOUND for CDC threshold), complement-inhibitor-high microenvironment.
- (vii) Grade: access TRANSFER (human CSF PK); bulk kill OUTCOME; persister kill ASSUMED; durability not shown.

## 3. Modality 2: CSF-delivered CAR-T / effector cells
Human (all solid CNS tumours except one T-ALL case) [abs unless noted]
- Intrathecal bivalent EGFR/IL13Ra2 CAR-T, n=6 rGBM (PMID 38480922): CAR-T abundant in CSF in all 6; early neurotoxicity "most consistent with ICANS" in all, managed with high-dose dexamethasone + anakinra; 1 DLT; tumour enhancement fell in all 6, none met ORR. (Cell dose exponent not rendered in the abstract text returned, so I do not quote it.)
- Intraventricular B7-H3 CAR-T in DIPG, 21 pts (PMID 41798119): tumour-inflammation-associated neurotoxicity in 16/21 (76%) pts and 49/152 infusions (32%), mostly gr1-2, one gr3; one CSF diversion; resolution <24 h median.
- IL13Ra2 CAR-T intratumoral/intraventricular/dual, 65 pts (PMID 38454126): no DLT; grade >=3 toxicity: one encephalopathy, one ataxia; SD or better 50% (29/58), 2 PR, 1 CR; max feasible dose 200 x 10^6 per cycle.
- Systematic review of 8 paediatric trials, 63 infused (PMID 42295570): no treatment-related mortality; disease control 65%; "Objective responses were rare".
- Intrathecal allogeneic B7-H3 CAR gamma-delta T, n=3 leptomeningeal metastases, 3 x 10^7 cells (PMID 42207176): one gr3 ICANS; CAR cells persisted in CSF "for at least one week"; cytology cleared in the 1 patient positive at baseline.
- Intrathecal TCR-/HLA-I-edited allogeneic IL13Ra2 CAR-T, n=5 glioma (PMID 41495049): no gr>=3 AEs; 1 CR + 3 PR.
- Intrathecal CD7 CAR-NK, ONE patient with isolated CNS relapse of T-ALL (PMID 36941630): marrow remained in CR >9 months; fever, headache, vomiting and "a spinal cord transection with incontinence".
- B-cell lymphoma/leukaemia with CSF-delivered CAR-T: NOT FOUND.
- IV CAR-T in CNS lymphoma (shows how much CNS access IV CAR-T already has): meta-analysis 8 studies/63 pts: ORR 69%, CR 51%, progression after remission 38% (95% CI 21-55), gr>=3 neurotoxicity 12% (3-24) (PMID 35796863); CD19 CAR-T PCNSL 3/5 CR (PMID 34492703); CAR19+CAR22 cocktail: 2 CR, 3 PR, 4/5 progressed, in 2 the target was still present and CAR-T had re-expanded in CSF (PMID 32903866); CD19/CD20 CAR-T in 7 CNSL: CR 4, PR 3, CAR-T in CSF in 4, median CR duration 22.4 mo, CD20 loss in 1 relapse (PMID 35152726); two fatal ICANS after CAR-T consolidation in PCNSL (PMID 42035265).
NHP/dog
- NHP with ventricular and lumbar reservoirs (PMID 29712574, full text): intraventricular autologous HER2-CAR T into target-bearing CSF; T cells "detected in the CSF of one animal 24 h after treatment"; IL-6/IL-2 rose in CSF; nothing in blood/plasma. Mouse: a log fewer CAR-T needed regionally than IV.
- Dogs: CD20 CAR-T first-in-species 5 dogs, antigen escape and CD20-negative relapse, canine anti-mouse antibodies associated with CAR-T loss (PMID 32002286) [abs]; canine CAR with canine 4-1BB-CD3z "did not persist or deplete B cells" (PMID 41376156) [abs]; canine tandem CD19/CD20 CAR kills CD19+ and/or CD20+ targets in vitro only (PMID 42480604) [abs]. CAR-T or any T cell delivered into dog CSF: NOT FOUND (closest: IT autologous/allogeneic MSC in healthy dogs, no abnormality on neuro score/CSF/MRI at d1 and d5, PMID 34427495; MSC via cisterna magna in 8 MUO dogs, no major adverse effects up to 2 y, PMID 26415563).
Tandem/multi-antigen evidence (human, IV): TanCAR7 n=87, CR 70%, 40% still in remission at 63.4 mo, 5-y OS 60.1% (PMID 41804087); zamto-cel n=12, 5/12 CR without relapse to 5 y (PMID 41512222); prizlon-cel n=48, CR 85%, ICANS 6.3% none >=gr3 (PMID 39813680); CD19/20 CAR-T n=32, ICANS 9% (all gr3) (PMID 42144261); AUTO3 CD19/22: "Neither dual-targeting CAR-T nor pembrolizumab prevented relapse in a significant proportion of patients" (PMID 36821767). Reference single-antigen: ZUMA-1 5-y: responses ongoing 31%, OS 42.6%, "Protracted B-cell aplasia was not required for durable responses" (PMID 36821768... see note below).
Numbers for the model
- (i) Access: 1.0 at the CSF-facing surface at administration (TRANSFER; delivered into CSF). Parenchymal infiltration is clinically shown only in gliomas (responses) and by IV CAR-T in PCNSL. Existing project value 0.5 for systemic CAR-T CNS access is ASSUMED; the lymphoma data above (IV CAR-T detected in CSF 4/7; PCNSL CR 3/5 and meta-analysis CR 51%) support non-trivial IV access (OUTCOME), but no number converts to a fraction. Mouse/NHP: regional route needs ~10x fewer cells (TRANSFER).
- (ii) Kill: no per-day rate in any CSF CAR-T paper. Logic: CAR-T cytolysis is MHC-independent, not pump-limited, not cycle-gated, so the persister criteria are met if the antigen is present and the cell is reached; strength of transfer is weak (solid tumours, different antigen, no kill kinetics). Evidence that CNS CAR-T is not enough alone: 4/5 progressed after CAR19/22 in CNS lymphoma although CAR-T re-expanded in CSF with antigen present (exhaustion/TME); pooled 38% progression after remission.
- (iii) Persistence window: CSF ~24 h (NHP, 1/4 animals) to >=1 week (human IT gdT); systemic CAR-T in responding lymphoma >500 d (PMID 42144261); canine CAR-T persistence short (anti-mouse antibodies; cBBz non-persistent). Weekly repeat intraventricular dosing was done in children (PMID 37961215, preprint, not peer reviewed). A one-time dose is a hit-and-clear strategy; ZUMA-1 shows hit-and-clear can give durable systemic remissions without lifelong persistence (systemic, not CNS).
- (iv) Toxicity axes: CNS_LOCAL neurotoxicity is the dominant one: 100% ICANS-like in the 6-patient IT bivalent trial (steroid + anakinra needed), TIAN 76% of patients/32% of infusions in DIPG (mostly gr1-2), IT gdT gr3 ICANS 1/3, IT edited allo CAR-T none >=gr3 (n=5); IV CAR-T in CNS lymphoma gr>=3 neurotox 12% pooled, fatal ICANS 2 pts. Immune-mediated: CRS reported (e.g. 53% with 12% gr>=3 for IV CD19/20 CAR-T). Interaction: IT dexamethasone/MTX/hydrocortisone controls ICANS (PMIDs 41479887: 12/13 remission, median 1 d; 37897136; 32407473; 39300248 [abs not available]) but "IDM significantly reduced CAR-T cells ... in cerebrospinal fluid" (41479887), i.e. a steroid or chemo arm co-delivered intrathecally would antagonise a CAR-T arm; timing matters (IT chemo on day 1 of blinatumomab: OR 15.6 for neurotoxicity, PMID 41550943).
- (v) Availability: canine CD20 CAR-T = trial/academic (Penn); tandem canine CD19/CD20 = in vitro; human CAR-T licensed IV only. Intrathecal = buildable. Allogeneic off-the-shelf options exist in human IT trials (gdT, edited T, NK) which matter because autologous dog CAR-T had manufacturing/persistence limits.
- (vi) Escapes: covers pump, quiescence (by mechanism), dCK, MHC loss. Tandem CD19/CD20 narrows CD20-loss and CD19-loss escape but relapse persists (AUTO3; TanCAR7 40% in remission at 5 y); antigen-low escape needs high-sensitivity design (PMID 34878825, 32193224 [titles/abstract only]). Does NOT cover exhaustion/TME, antigen-null, and is the highest neurotoxicity-risk option.
- (vii) Grade: access TRANSFER; kill ASSUMED (no kinetics); persistence TRANSFER (NHP + human solid tumour); toxicity MEASURED for human solid-tumour CNS (indirect for lymphoma).

## 4. Modality 3: intrathecal glucocorticoid
- Human IT glucocorticoid single-agent efficacy in lymphomatous meningitis: NOT FOUND. IT hydrocortisone appears only as the "H" in triple IT (MTX/ara-C/hydrocortisone) and as ICANS therapy (PMID 37897136, 5 children; PMID 41479887). Triple-IT literature found gives toxicity not GC-attributable efficacy: acute neurotoxicity 5.8% (3/51) and 18.4% (7/38) with 20 and 17 triple-IT doses (PMID 11070476); neurocognition comparable to 18 Gy CRT (PMID 17971588); triple IT is the default in Spanish NHL practice, 166 of 187 prophylaxis cases (PMID 18928733).
- Dog biology (the decisive evidence): "Prednisone resistance develops rapidly and essentially universally when dogs with lymphoma are treated with corticosteroids"; clinical resistance after median 68 d (range 7-348), with NR3C1a mRNA down in all 6 high-grade cases (PMID 31943234 [abs], n=7); high serum cortisol associated with fewer GCR+ cells and worse PFS/OS (PMID 38471970, n=30); canine lymphoid lines dex-resistant with low GR (PMID 20362310, in vitro); RCT of 81 dogs: prednisolone added nothing to a multidrug protocol (PMID 23746872). One P-gp-high doxorubicin-resistant canine line stayed prednisolone-sensitive (PMID 24975508), so GC is not pump-limited.
- (i) Access: CSF 1.0 by IT (TRANSFER); parenchymal access of small molecules after IT is negligible in the PBPK model (PMID 38289997 [abs]) and limited in dog IT etoposide (PMID 1517797 [abs]); lipophilic steroid CSF/plasma ratio after IV was NOT FOUND, so I cannot say IT adds access over IV.
- (ii) Kill: not division-gated and not pump-limited (mechanism + PMID 24975508), but receptor-loss escape is documented in dogs. Project bracket 0.0098-0.144/day (status doc) is unchanged by IT delivery unless the limit was exposure; PMID 31943234 says the limit is receptor down-regulation. TRANSFER only.
- (iv) Toxicity: low; IT dexamethasone/hydrocortisone REDUCES DepoCyt arachnoiditis/neurotoxicity (PMID 25749825, 28665999). Best role is protective co-drug (and ICANS rescue), not closer.
- (v) Availability: licensed generic steroids; IT GC in dogs for lymphoma: NOT FOUND; IT ara-C +/- MTX in dogs: 112 dogs/8 cats, one seizure (PMID 25041580 [abs]).
- (vi) Escapes: antigen-independent, MHC-independent, pump-independent, quiescence-independent (by mechanism). Does NOT cover GC-receptor loss/NR3C1 down-regulation (documented in dogs), BCL2-type apoptosis block.
- (vii) Grade: ASSUMED/TRANSFER, resistance MEASURED in dogs.

## 5. Modality 4: sustained-release cytarabine (DepoCyt / NS-101) and intrathecal chemotherapy
- RCT, lymphomatous meningitis n=28, DepoCyt 50 mg q2wk vs free ara-C 50 mg 2x/wk: response 71% vs 15% (P=.006); neuro progression 78.5 vs 42 d; survival 99.5 vs 63 d (P>.05) (PMID 10506606 [abs]).
- DepoCyt keeps cytotoxic free ara-C in CSF "for at least 2 weeks" (PMID 17112293 [abs]); NS-101 phase 1 via Ommaya: effective concentration for 2 weeks, MTD 50 mg (PMID 18030013 [abs]); rat: free ara-C above 0.4 uM for 2 weeks after 0.8 mg (PMID 28654460 [abs]).
- Lymphoma series: Polish n=120, cohort C (CNS disease) neurologic response 76% (40% CR), median OS 4.8 y; no CNS relapse in 95 high-risk/pleocytosis pts at 3 y (PMID 25716885 [abs]); Balearic n=58: CSF clearance 77% (n=32 treated), median OS 6 mo, prophylaxis relapse 11% (3/26) at 55 mo, headache 33%, neurologic deficits 20% (PMID 28665999 [abs]); ALL RCT n=145: grade 3-4 neurotoxicity 24% (50 mg q14d) vs 3% triple IT, reduced by IT dexamethasone (PMID 25749825 [abs]).
- Withdrawal: DepoCyt "was discontinued in 2017 due to persistent manufacturing issues" (PMID 39799998 [abs], a review statement; I did not verify against a regulator).
- Dog/other large animal: NO liposomal-ara-C PK/tox data found. Dog IV ara-C data: 12-h infusion at 50 mg/m2/h gives plasma Css 14.1 +/- 4.2 uM, CSF Css 8.3 +/- 1.1 uM, CSF:plasma 0.62 +/- 0.14; CSF t1/2 165 +/- 28 min (PMID 1742843 [abs], healthy dogs). Dog IT ara-C +/- MTX bolus safety: PMID 25041580. Free IT ara-C CSF half-life: NOT FOUND (the 1973 paper PMID 4201653 has no abstract).
- (i) Access: CSF 1.0. Important for the model: IV ara-C CRI in dogs already gives CSF concentrations ~20x the 0.4 uM cytotoxic threshold (8.3 vs 0.4 uM, DERIVED comparison across different studies) and CSF:plasma 0.62 (MEASURED, healthy dogs); so access of ara-C to CSF was probably never the constraint, and parenchymal levels were not measured. Check the model's CNS access value for IV cytarabine against 0.62.
- (ii) Kill: ara-C is S-phase specific and needs dCK; kill = f_awake x rate (the project's own dormancy logic). Persister (quiescent, dCK-low) is not addressed by longer exposure. Bulk responses are real (71% vs 15%; CSF clearance 77%).
- (iii) Window: ~14 d per injection; q14d dosing.
- (iv) Toxicity: chemical arachnoiditis/headache, neurotoxicity gr3-4 24% at 50 mg q14d (ALL), mitigated by dex.
- (v) Availability: DepoCyt discontinued; buildable only as multivesicular liposome (proprietary technology per PMID 17112293) or as continuous IT infusion of free ara-C via pump (pump chronic data in dogs: see sec. 7).
- (vi) Escapes: covers pump (not an efflux-limited agent in CSF), antigen loss, MHC loss. Does NOT cover quiescence, dCK loss, GC-independent... i.e. it cannot close the user's stated surviving escape.
- (vii) Grade: access MEASURED (human/rat/dog IV), kill on persister ASSUMED-low.

## 6. Other CSF-deliverable agents found (non-gated, non-pump candidates)
- Intraventricular 131I/124I-antibody compartmental radioimmunotherapy (cRIT): 38 pts phase 1 omburtamab, CSF:blood absorbed dose ~25:1 (1.01 vs 0.04 mGy/MBq), median PFS 7.5 y in neuroblastoma vs historical (PMID 36371226 [abs]); 21 recurrent CNS neuroblastoma pts: 17 alive free of CNS disease at median 33 mo (PMID 19890606 [abs]); device safety 6/151 (4%) complications, no infection (PMID 24777835 [abs]); 15% abnormal CSF flow on screening (PMID 32005772 [abs]); mast-cell chemical meningitis case (PMID 33533945); anti-drug antibodies speed blood clearance. Satisfies non-gated/non-pump, tolerates antigen-low through crossfire, but no lymphoma/anti-CD20 cRIT found (NOT FOUND), needs radiation-safety handling in dogs (no source), myelosuppression/thyroid.
- Intrathecal bispecific T-cell engager in lymphoma: NOT FOUND (IV glofitamab: CSF/plasma up to 0.44%, ORR 75% in 16 PCNSL pts, 2 patients gr>=3 neurotoxicity, PMID 42035265; IT chemo day 1 + blinatumomab neurotoxicity OR 15.6, PMID 41550943).
- IT trastuzumab/others, oncolytic virus (Zika IT in 3 dogs with CNS tumours, PMID 32220305): not lymphoma-relevant.

## 7. Feasibility of chronic CSF access (dogs first, then human)
Dogs
- Chronic lumbar catheter with vascular access port in conscious lab dogs: 67% patent >30 days after first surgery, 86% after repair/replacement (PMID 24694254 [abs]).
- 32 dogs with IT catheters + SynchroMed II pumps (PMID 31124198 [abs]) and 52 dogs with two pump brands (PMID 22239738 [abs]): catheter-tip granulomas occurred only with morphine, "zero incidence in baclofen or saline", i.e. a pump itself is not granulogenic; drug-specific. 14-day IT infusion in beagles is a routine tox design (PMID 31117921).
- Bolus IT cytarabine +/- MTX safe in 112 dogs/8 cats (PMID 25041580); CSF collection major complication 11/7,545 (0.15%) with high mortality once it happens (PMID 40526210 [abs]).
- NOT FOUND: any Ommaya/ventricular reservoir in a dog (0 hits), any dog catheter infection rate, any dog data past ~2 y of repeated IT access.
Human (longest device experience)
- 98 children, 5,472 instillations, 199,956 device-days (median 1,336 d), 1 infection (1%) (PMID 25017328 [abs]) => 5.0e-6 per device-day = ~0.18%/device-year, ~1.8%/10 y (DERIVED, one event, wide CI); per access 1.8e-4.
- PCNSL, 94 pts, 1,247 installations: misplacement 9.6%, symptomatic pericatheter leucoencephalopathy 4/94 + CSF leak 1 (5.3% symptomatic non-infectious), asymptomatic white-matter lesions 8.5%, infection 6.4%, none lethal (PMID 33316084 [abs]).
- Meta-analysis 43 studies, 1,995 insertions: overall complications 6.4% (image-guided) vs 14.1%; early infection 0.7% vs 4.5%; mortality 0.6% vs 1.4% (PMID 30447448 [abs]).
- H-port vs Ommaya in leptomeningeal metastasis: infection 14/152 vs 12/89; CSF leak 3/152 vs 13/89 (PMID 38061763 [abs]). Historical infection 10.25% of 322 patients (1.71% of 1,932 treatments) vs 0/63 patients with prophylactic 10 mg intraventricular vancomycin each dose (PMID 40719464 [abs]). Paediatric worse-case: 7 of 20 children infected, 17/31 reservoirs explanted (PMID 26479760 [abs]).
- So the per-access infection hazard spans ~1e-4 to ~2e-2 across series (100x), depending on aseptic discipline; decade-scale data do not exist.

## 8. Parenchymal vs leptomeningeal disease: what CSF delivery reaches
What reaches what (evidence in hand)
- Leptomeninges/ventricular surface/CSF: everything delivered into CSF (cells, antibody, small molecules) at ~full concentration; shown by cytologic clearance (rituximab, ara-C, DepoCyt) in humans.
- Perivascular (Virchow-Robin) spaces and periventricular brain: plausible for antibody; not measured for lymphoma.
- Deep parenchyma: small molecules negligible by PBPK (PMID 38289997 [abs]) and limited diffusion for dog IT etoposide with high concentrations near the injection site and neurotoxicity at higher dose (PMID 1517797); antibodies "may achieve therapeutic concentrations in the deep brain parenchyma" by PBPK (same paper) and macaque IT anti-Nogo-A penetrated parenchyma and bound target (PMID 39572304); recombinant I2S (large enzyme) distributed "widely" in dog and NHP brain after ICV or lumbar IT (PMID 22279584 [abs]). Counter-evidence: mouse IVT IgG stays periventricular, Mab "localized around the administration site" (PMID 41558020 [abs]); human IgG in rat CSF localised selectively at injury sites (PMID 29665816 [abs]); rat IVT 3F8 reached 86% of brain deposits but <1% of lumbosacral spinal deposits even with continuous infusion, and needed neutrophils/complement (PMID 10404068 [abs]); dog ICV I2S gave little spinal cord exposure, lumbar IT did (PMID 22279584) => intraventricular-only dosing can under-cover spinal meninges; lumbar plus ventricular, or both, is needed.
- Human lymphoma clinical signal on parenchyma: responses in "non-bulky lesions (< 2 cm) within brain parenchyma" (PMID 24190981 intro, full text), one parenchymal resolution in 10 pts (PMID 17312328); HOVON 80 (24/36 parenchymal) CR 22%, 1-y PFS 19% (PMID 27530779) -- not durable, but confounded by systemic therapy and bulk disease.
- Cells: IV CAR-T already enters parenchyma in PCNSL (CR of enhancing lesions 3/5, PMID 34492703); IT/ICV CAR-T gave parenchymal glioma regressions (PMID 38454126, 41495049) -- trafficking works clinically, durable regression rarely.
Canine anatomy of the disease (what the sanctuary actually looks like)
- 37 necropsied dogs with neural lymphoma: DLBCL involves meningeal, perivascular and periventricular compartments; CSF cell count >64/uL tracks marked meningeal + periventricular infiltration (PMID 27511313 [abs]).
- MRI, 12 dogs/cats: extraparenchymal 7, intraparenchymal 3, mixed 2; "abnormal meninges around the lesion" 10/12 (PMID 22093168 [abs]); choroid plexus involvement in 5 dogs (PMID 33769594).
- Primary leptomeningeal lymphomas with sparing of parenchyma: PMID 10667407, 6699229 (case reports).
- Intravascular CNS lymphoma in dogs (PMID 36329600): cells inside meningeal and parenchymal vessels: NOT reachable by CSF delivery; needs a systemic agent.
Interpretation for the model (labelled as reasoning)
- Leptomeningeal/periventricular/perivascular compartment: access ~1.0 for antibody/cells/small molecules; "deep parenchymal" fraction: no measured access for any agent; antibody and cells are the only classes with any support for entering it. Early detection (small, meningeal-first disease) favours the regional route, but the persister's anatomical niche in dogs has not been mapped (NOT FOUND). Recommend carrying two CNS compartments in the model (CSF-facing, deep parenchyma) with parenchymal access for IVT antibody as an ASSUMED bracket (e.g. 0.05-0.3) and the systemic IV route kept as the second route to the parenchyma.

## A. Intrathecal / intraventricular rituximab

- PMID 12393404 (Rubenstein, Blood 2002, DOI 10.1182/blood-2002-06-1636) [abstract]: IV rituximab CSF level "approximately 0.1% of serum levels"; IT rituximab in cynomolgus monkeys: no significant acute/delayed neuro toxicity; CSF clearance biphasic, "terminal half-life of 4.96 hours".

- PMID 24190981 (Kadoch, Clin Cancer Res 2013, DOI 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-13-0474, PMC3944388) [full text]: Ommaya sampling; rituximab 10, 25, 50 mg doses. Authors state brain penetration is *inferred from biphasic CSF decay*, "additional studies are required to directly quantify brain tissue penetration". LLOQ 0.25 ug/mL CSF.
   Trial 1 (n=10) responses "within the intraocular compartment and in non-bulky lesions (<2 cm) within brain parenchyma" (from the introduction text, full text).
- PMID 26872652 (Ceppi, Am J Hematol 2016, DOI 10.1002/ajh.24329) [abstract]: 25 children, 163 IT/IVT rituximab doses, median 6 doses/patient, median dose 25 mg; AEs gr1-2 neuropathy 20%, allergy 2, headache 2; gr III neuropathy 1, seizure 2; "Eighteen patients (72%) ... achieved a CNS remission" (with or without other CNS therapy).

## Self-derived arithmetic (labelled DERIVED; uses only numbers verified above)
- From PMID 24190981 popPK (n=7): V1(CSF)=62.2 mL; k(CSF->serum)=3.57/day; k(CSF->brain)=2.61/day. Sum of CSF exit rates = 6.18/day -> initial CSF exit half-life ln2/6.18 = 0.112 d = 2.7 h (ignoring brain->CSF return flux k31, which was not given in the text I retrieved; so true terminal CSF decay is slower). Monkey measured terminal CSF half-life 4.96 h (PMID 12393404); NHP anti-Nogo-A CSF half-life 6.4 h (PMID 39572304).
- Instantaneous CSF concentration if 25 mg is mixed into 62.2 mL: 25 mg / 62.2 mL = ~0.40 mg/mL (402 ug/mL). With 6.18/d exit: after 24 h ~ 402*exp(-6.18) ~ 0.83 ug/mL (+ back flux) -> roughly at/above the 0.25 ug/mL assay LLOQ at 24 h but ~3 orders of magnitude lower than at injection. => per-dose antibody coverage in CSF is ~1 day; 2x/week dosing leaves gaps; 5 weeks of dosing is what was tested. - PMID 28665999 (Balearic Lymphoma Group, PLoS One 2017, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0179595) [abstract]: ITLC n=58 (prophylaxis n=26, treatment n=32): toxicity 33% headache, 20% neurological deficits, 11% nausea, 9% dizziness; prophylaxis cohort median f/u 55 mo: 3 CNS relapses (11%); treatment cohort: CSF complete clearance 77%, median OS 6 months; deaths from lymphoma progression 19/24 (79%); "A high number of ITLC injections was associated with better rates of CSF clearance, clinical responses, PFS and lower relapses"; tolerability better with concomitant dexamethasone.
- PMID 25749825 (Haematologica 2015, DOI 10.3324/haematol.2014.123273) [abstract] RCT, ALL, n=145: ITLC 50 mg (6/8 injections) vs IT triple therapy (MTX/ara-C/prednisone x12): grade 3-4 neurotoxicity 17/71 (24%) vs 2/74 (3%) (P=0.0002); 4 patients (6%) stopped ITLC for toxicity; "Neurotoxicity worsened with liposomal cytarabine every 14 days (T-cell disease), and was improved by the adjunct of intrathecal dexamethasone"; meningeal relapses 2 vs 4.
- PMID 28654460 (J Pediatr Hematol Oncol 2018, DOI 10.1097/MPH.0000000000000888) [abstract] RAT: liposomal ara-C 0.8 mg IT once: "Mean concentrations of free cytarabine in cerebrospinal fluid remained above the cytotoxic threshold of 0.4 uM for 2 weeks after dosing"; fewer cognitive deficits than IT MTX.
- Mechanism caveat (my reasoning, flagged as such): ara-C is S-phase-specific and needs dCK activation -> a division-gated agent; a 2-week >=0.4 uM window helps only cells that enter S phase in that window and have dCK. Does not address quiescent persisters or dCK loss.
