**Reading:** the canine standard of care for an intracranial target is **40 Gy in 10 x 4 Gy** (hypofractionated definitive intent, the Zurich protocol, now with its own canine dose-volume constraints) or **50 Gy in 20 x 2.5 Gy** for pituitary; stereotactic 1-5 fractions (15-35 Gy) is the other common option. **Acute / early-delayed neurologic toxicity runs 5-10% and is transient and steroid-responsive. Severe LATE toxicity is NOT reported in any of these canine series** -- the largest (110 dogs, median f/u 498 d) saw none, the RCT (57 dogs) saw none, the 46-dog series saw none. The only canine late events retrieved are 2 cases of post-RT hypoadrenocorticism after a 50 Gy pituitary course and 1 presumptive radiation-induced trigeminal nerve sheath tumour at 1,724 days (41588441).

### Q1.2 So where is 23.4 Gy in 13 x 1.8 Gy on this scale?
- 23.4 Gy / 13 fx: BED(alpha/beta=2, late CNS) = 23.4 x (1 + 1.8/2) = **44.5 Gy2**; EQD2 = 22.2 Gy.
- 40 Gy / 10 x 4 Gy (canine standard): BED2 = 40 x (1+4/2) = **120 Gy2**; EQD2 = 60 Gy.
- 50 Gy / 20 x 2.5 Gy: BED2 = 50 x (1+2.5/2) = **112.5 Gy2**; EQD2 = 56.3 Gy.
=> **23.4 Gy in 13 fractions is about 37-40% of the late-effect BED of what dogs routinely receive.** It is CONSERVATIVE, not aggressive, in late-CNS terms -- even allowing that the model's course is whole-brain rather than focal, since the canine constraint that matters (Brain-GTV V32Gy <= 13 cm^3, whole-brain gEUD <= 30 Gy; PMID 41420297) is not approached: a 23.4 Gy whole-brain course puts gEUD at 23.4 Gy and V32Gy at zero.
- Caveat to state: every canine series above is a FOCAL/conformal field, so the whole-brain VOLUME at 23.4 Gy is larger than anything in the canine record; what the canine data constrain is the high-dose volume, and 23.4 Gy whole-brain satisfies both published canine constraints by construction. Whole-brain-specific canine late data: see Q1.4.

### Q1.4 Canine spinal cord tolerance (for the craniospinal variant)  [NEW]
- **PMID 26366252 (Jin 2015, J Radiat Oncol, doi 10.1007/s13566-015-0212-9, full text in PMC4559090):** pooled fit of radiation-induced myelopathy (RIM) data from 5 rat, 3 large-animal (monkey, **dog**, pig) and 18 patient studies. alpha/beta for cord = **3.9 Gy** and essentially identical across species (3.9 large-animal, 3.7 human, 4.1 rat) -- so a dog-to-human cord transfer is justified by this paper directly. BED50 (50% RIM) = 111 Gy => D50 73.4 Gy at 2 Gy/fx; **BED5 (5% RIM) = 83.9 Gy => D5 = 55.4 Gy at 2 Gy/fx, or 16.2 Gy single fraction**.
- Applying it to the model's course: 23.4 Gy in 13 x 1.8 Gy gives cord BED(alpha/beta 3.9) = 23.4 x (1 + 1.8/3.9) = **34.2 Gy3.9**, i.e. **41% of BED5 and 31% of BED50**. Myelopathy risk at this dose is **well under 5%** -- the craniospinal variant of the model's course is inside canine/large-animal cord tolerance with a wide margin. Grade: DERIVED from a cross-species fit that explicitly includes canine data.
- Other canine cord data: PMIDs 11403709 and 11229724 fit NTCP / critical-volume models to **two sets of canine spinal cord irradiation data** at two fractionations including partial-length irradiation (dose-volume effects); PMID 7302125 (Zook 1981 Radiat Res) fractionated photons/neutrons on the canine cervical cord with paralysis as endpoint (abstract not available in PubMed -- UNVERIFIED numbers). So canine cord tolerance is one of the better-characterised canine late endpoints, which is why the alpha/beta fit above is possible.
- For contrast on canine LATE effects where dose/fraction was pushed: PMID 11954816 (pelvic RT, 16 dogs) -- late colitis in 9/16 (56%), severe in 5, with 3 GI perforations; **every severe late effect occurred at 3.0-3.3 Gy/fraction, none at 2.7-2.9 Gy/fraction**. Not a CNS endpoint, but it is the canine datapoint showing that fraction SIZE, not total dose, drives canine late injury -- and 1.8 Gy/fraction is below every fraction size in that series.

## Q2. The combination risk: whole-brain radiation PLUS repeated intrathecal methotrexate

### Q2.1 PMID 39269476 VERIFIED, and the record's wording needs one correction  [verification; one NEW nuance]
Verified word for word (Masuda 2024, Ann Hematol 104:1095-1104, doi 10.1007/s00277-024-05989-1): 126 adults with CNS lymphoma, high-dose methotrexate +/- intrathecal MTX and **response-adapted** WBRT; median observation 38.7 months. Treatment-related leukoencephalopathy (tLE) in 33 patients, **cumulative incidence 29.2% (95% CI 20.6-38.2%) at two years**; **IT MTX the only significant risk factor on multivariable analysis, HR 4.50, P<0.001**; number of IT MTX doses associated with increased incidence AND severity.
**Three things the record does not currently say, and should:**
1. **"most of them asymptomatic"** -- the 29.2% is a RADIOLOGICAL endpoint (white-matter change on MRI), not a clinical deficit. The model's budget is a clinical-toxicity budget; 29% radiological tLE is not 29% neurological morbidity.
2. **Median time to development was 3.0 months**, not years. That makes it an early-delayed, not a true late, endpoint in this series -- which also means a 38.7-month observation window did capture it.
3. **The radiation was NOT the identified driver.** WBRT was response-adapted (dose varied, not everyone received it) and did not emerge as a risk factor; the intrathecal methotrexate did. So this paper argues against the IT MTX arm of the model's brain closure, not against the radiation arm. Section J.3/K.3 currently attributes the 29% to "whole-brain radiation and high-dose methotrexate"; the paper's own conclusion is "confirm the neurotoxicity of IT MTX".

### Q2.5 What sequence or dose is defensible for a 10-year goal in a dog
From the above, stated as a derivation, not a measurement:
1. **Keep 23.4 Gy / 13 x 1.8 Gy and do not escalate.** It is 37-40% of the late BED dogs routinely tolerate (Q1.2), it is the human dose selected to avoid leukoencephalopathy (Q2.2), and canine dose escalation has twice failed to buy anything (TBI 8.4 vs 13.5 Gy, PMIDs 3887690/3901841; the 11% intracranial SIB, PMID 35775129).
2. **Sequence radiation FIRST and concentrate the intrathecal methotrexate AFTER it, not concurrently.** The one identified driver of leukoencephalopathy is the number of IT MTX doses (HR 4.50, dose-dependent), and tLE appeared at a median of 3.0 months -- i.e. during the overlap window. Concurrent delivery is the configuration the human series ran and is the one that produced 29%.
3. **Cap the IT MTX dose count and space it.** The model's 0.12/day mean kill corresponds to dosing every 2-3 weeks for 12-14 months = 20-30 doses, against 10-19 in routine paediatric practice and a human PCNSL median of 46 punctures. Both numbers bracket the plan, so it is not outside human experience, but the dose count is the quantity the HR 4.50 scales with -- so the defensible version is the LONGEST interval that still clears (the model's own sensitivity: at 0.06/day it needs verdinexor brain access >=0.3 and ~800 days).
4. **Craniospinal rather than whole-brain is affordable on cord grounds** (Q1.4: 34.2 Gy3.9 vs BED5 83.9 Gy3.9), and the 1984 canine precedent was craniospinal.
5. **The genuinely unaddressed late endpoint for a 10-year canine goal is the one with a canine datapoint: a radiation-induced second tumour.** PMID 41588441 reports a presumptive radiation-induced trigeminal nerve sheath tumour at **1,724 days (4.7 years)** after a 50 Gy canine pituitary course. A 10-year horizon is long enough for this to matter in a way that a 2-year horizon is not, and no canine series has follow-up long enough to give an incidence. This is a REAL residual, and it is NOT the same thing as leukoencephalopathy.

### Q1.5 Delivery burden: centres and anaesthesia  [NEW]
- Every canine radiation fraction needs **general anaesthesia**. 13 fractions = 13 anaesthetics.
- Per-event canine anaesthetic mortality: **0.1-0.2% in healthy dogs and 0.5-2% in sick dogs** (PMID 18658000, Brodbelt 2008 Vet J 182:152-61, review; verified) and **0.11% death with 2.1% complications in 8,087 dogs** (PMID 9657167, Dyson 1998, prospective, 66 practices; verified). Compounded over 13 fractions: **1.3% cumulative mortality at the healthy-dog rate, 6.3% at the 0.5% sick-dog rate** (DERIVED, 1-(1-p)^13). ASA class 3-5 raises complication odds 2.5x in dogs (PMID 9657167) and a lymphoma patient on a transplant program is not ASA 1.
  **This is a cost the model does not carry anywhere, and it is of the same order as the toxicities it does carry.** It is also an argument for fewer, larger fractions (Q3) independent of radiobiology.
- Centre count: not established from a citable source. Web search returns facility marketing describing linear-accelerator centres as "one of the few in the country" / "a handful in the Northwest"; no registry number was retrieved. **Mark UNVERIFIED: number of veterinary radiation-oncology centres not sourced.** What IS clear from the series above is that every modern canine brain-RT cohort comes from a single academic institution (Zurich 110 + 105 + 57 dogs; CSU; UC Davis; Hokkaido), i.e. the capability is concentrated in teaching hospitals and a small number of referral centres, and a 13-fraction daily course requires the owner to be within commuting distance of one for 2.5 weeks.

## Q3. Could a LOWER radiation dose substitute?

### Q3.1 The human low-dose evidence, verified
- **PMID 20970029 VERIFIED** (Ganem 2010 IJROBP 78:975-82, review; already in record as a bare citation). Exact content: **4 Gy in two fractions of 2 Gy within 3 days**; first report 1994 in chemoresistant indolent NHL; "**at least eight series** ... showing a **55% complete response rate** in irradiated sites, with a **median duration of 15-42 months**". Also: conventional curative dose for indolent NHL is 30-35 Gy, but older series found "a significant number of 'nodular' lymphomas were controlled with a dose of **<22 Gy for >3 years**"; and low-dose TOTAL BODY irradiation at **1.5-2 Gy** was "particularly demonstrative". Mechanism: apoptosis plus a macrophage/dendritic-cell-mediated immune response.
- **PMID 12459371 NEW** (Johannsson 2002 IJROBP 54:1466-70, prospective phase 2): 22 patients, disseminated indolent NHL (n=15) or CLL (n=7), **2 Gy x 2 within 3 days**, 31 sites. **Overall response 82% per patient (55% CR), 87% per site (65% CR); median duration of response 22 months; "None of the patients had significant side effects"**. CLL subset: 71% ORR but only 29% CR -- i.e. the more quiescent/resistant lineage responded less completely, which matches the canine CLL1390 line being the resistant one.
- **PMID 40940942 NEW** (Grujic 2025 Cancers 17:2845, systematic review, 10 studies / **591 patients**, ocular adnexal lymphoma, **4 Gy in 2 fractions**): ORR **88-100%**, CR **50-95%**, local control 63-100%, 2-y PFS >85% in most studies; **no grade >=3 toxicity**; acute toxicity up to 42% (grade 1 dry eye/conjunctivitis); late toxicity 16-33%, cataracts rarely needing surgery.
- **Low-dose radiation in CNS lymphoma specifically: NOT FOUND.** No series of 2 x 2 Gy or 4/8 Gy single-fraction whole-brain radiotherapy for CNS lymphoma was retrieved. The lowest whole-brain dose with prospective CNS-lymphoma data is 23.4 Gy (Q2.2). So a low-dose WBRT substitute has **no clinical precedent in the compartment at issue** (rule 14: the proxy searched was "whole-brain / CNS low-dose lymphoma radiotherapy"; the measurement does not exist).
- **Canine low-dose datapoint, NEW: PMID 7262204** (Cowall 1981 Exp Hematol 9:581-7). Low-dose TOTAL-BODY irradiation, **150 rad = 1.5 Gy in 10 fractions over 5 weeks**, in 5 dogs with lymphoma: **3 of 5 achieved remissions of limited duration**. No change in hemogram or marrow cellularity, but GM-CFC/M-CFC progenitors fell significantly (P<0.001) and endotoxin-challenged marrow response was "dramatically blunted" 2 weeks after. Verified. => a 1.5 Gy total dose produces measurable antitumour effect in canine lymphoma in vivo, at the price of a latent marrow reserve deficit.

**Answers:**
- **2 x 2 Gy cannot substitute.** It delivers **0.17-0.67 in-vivo e-folds, i.e. 4-6x less than the 23.4 Gy course**. In the model's brain closures the radiation is already worth only 0.9-3.7 e-folds and dropping it entirely pushes the intrathecal-methotrexate window from 399-420 d to ~1,283-1,346 d (section J.2/K.2); a 0.2-0.7 e-fold course is close to no radiation at all. The late-toxicity saving is real (BED2 8 vs 44.5 Gy2) but it is buying off a toxicity that the human 23.4 Gy data say is already not occurring (Q2.2).
- **A single 8 Gy fraction is the interesting alternative, and the record has not considered it.** It gives **1.37-3.40 in-vivo e-folds -- 52% MORE than 23.4 Gy at the resistant line (1.37 vs 0.90), 96% at the median (1.60 vs 1.67 -- essentially equal), 73% (2.15 vs 2.94) at OSW** -- at a slightly LOWER late-CNS BED (40 vs 44.5 Gy2), **one anaesthetic instead of thirteen**, and no 2.5-week daily-commute requirement. The LQ quadratic term is what does it. Caveats to state plainly: (a) single-fraction 8 Gy whole-brain has **no CNS-lymphoma efficacy data**, (b) the LQ model at 8 Gy/fraction is extrapolating well beyond the SF2/SF5 fit range and over-predicts single-fraction kill, (c) large single fractions raise acute oedema risk (steroid cover needed), and (d) canine stereotactic practice already uses 15-18 Gy single fractions to FOCAL brain targets (PMIDs 37423611, 37604645), not whole-brain. Grade: **DERIVED / HYPOTHESIS WORTH TESTING IN THE MODEL, not a substitute that evidence supports.**
- **Omitting radiation entirely and relying on intrathecal methotrexate alone for ~3.5 years:** the human and paediatric evidence (Q2.3) points this way, not the other -- >10,000 children have had cranial RT omitted with intrathecal therapy holding CNS relapse at 1.5-3.9%, including a T-lineage-specific arm where extended triple intrathecal therapy matched cranial RT (PMID 19694717). **But** the model's own clock says methotrexate alone needs **1,283-1,346 days (3.5-3.7 years) of dosing**, i.e. ~60-90 doses at every 2-3 weeks, against the 10-19 of paediatric practice and a human PCNSL median of 46 punctures, and the HR 4.50 for leukoencephalopathy scales with dose number. So **neither arm is free**: radiation shortens the methotrexate exposure 3-fold (the quantity with a measured, dose-dependent neurotoxicity hazard) at the cost of a course whose own neurotoxicity was prospectively measured as absent. **On the evidence retrieved, keeping the 23.4 Gy course and SHORTENING the methotrexate is the better-supported trade, which is what the model already does.** That is a conclusion this sweep SUPPORTS rather than overturns.

## Q4. Radiosensitivity of PRIMARY canine lymphoma cells, and specifically primary canine T-CELL lymphoma
### Q4.1 Plainly: there is NO primary-canine-cell clonogenic radiosensitivity assay, T-cell or otherwise
Searched: canine lymphoma radiosensitivity in vitro (3 hits total in PubMed), canine lymphoma clonogenic / apoptosis / ex-vivo, primary canine lymphoma G2 / gamma-H2AX / comet, canine T-cell lymphoma cell line clonogenic. **No clonogenic, apoptosis or ex-vivo radiosensitivity assay on PRIMARY (patient-derived, non-immortalised) canine lymphoma cells exists in the retrievable literature, and none on primary canine T-cell lymphoma.** The record's statement (section I.4: "the thing that would decide it is a measurement ... primary canine T-cell lymphoma radiosensitivity (clonogenic or apoptosis, cycling vs resting, from fine-needle samples)") **stands and is confirmed as still open after this sweep.** It remains the single named missing measurement.

### Q4.2 The closest things that DO exist, in order of proximity
1. **PMID 17532464 (Kisseberth 2007, Leuk Res 31:1709-20) -- THE FINDING OF THIS SWEEP.** The canine **OSW** cell line was "established from the malignant pleural effusion of a dog with **PERIPHERAL T-CELL LYMPHOMA**", with an **oligoclonal T-cell receptor gamma (TCRgamma) gene rearrangement** (CD3-, TCRab-, TCRgd-, CD4-, CD5-, CD8-, Thy1-, CD21- by flow; cytoplasmic CD3- but CD20+; a null-phenotype PTCL). Verified in abstract.
   **=> OSW, already in the model's RT_SURVIVAL table at SF2 0.61 / SF5 0.15, IS a canine T-CELL LYMPHOMA line.** The record has carried a canine T-cell lymphoma radiosensitivity measurement all along without labelling it as one.
   - LQ fit (repo's own `lq_alpha_beta`): alpha 0.1590, beta 0.0441. **23.4 Gy in 13 x 1.8 Gy = 5.58 e-folds in vitro, 2.94 e-folds in vivo after DMF 1.9.**
   - That is **76% MORE than the "median line" 1771 (1.67 in vivo) that the model's headline T-cell-brain window (399 d, section J.2) is computed on, and 3.3x the "most resistant line" CLL1390 (0.90)**. The model's T-cell-brain radiation input is therefore **CONSERVATIVE relative to the only canine T-cell lymphoma line in the record**, not optimistic.
   - Lineage of the other three (so the asymmetry is explicit): **CLBL-1 = canine diffuse large B-CELL lymphoma** (CD79a+, CD3/CD4/CD5/CD8/Thy-1 negative, monoclonal IGH; PMIDs 22761949, 30592723, verified); **17-71 ("1771") = B-lineage** (CD79a+, CD3-, TCR-; secondary citation via web summary of PMID 22236332 -- **MARK UNVERIFIED, the Seiser abstract does not state lineage**); **CLL-1390 = a chronic lymphocytic leukaemia line, lineage not stated in any abstract retrieved -- UNVERIFIED.** Maeda 2016's own Table 1 labels all four only as "Lymphoid" with no immunophenotype (full text checked at PMC4892608).
   - **Consequence for the model, stated as a code change (rule 11):** `lymphoma_sustained.RT_EFOLDS_IN_VITRO` labels the three lines "most resistant / median / most sensitive" with no lineage. For the T-CELL brain the lineage-matched line is OSW, and using it raises the radiation from 1.67 to 2.94 e-folds in vivo. `minimal_it_mtx_window(..., rt_efolds=2.94)` would give the lineage-matched T-cell window; the published 399 d is the B/CLL-mixed median. This is a sensitivity the record has not run.
2. **PMID 23935952 (Thamm 2013 PLoS One 8:e69192) -- primary canine cells, but the host's normal lymphocytes, not the tumour.** G2 chromosomal radiosensitivity assay on stimulated lymphocytes from **21 golden retrievers with lymphoma vs 20 healthy golden retrievers vs 20 healthy mixed-breed dogs**, after 1.0 Gy ionising radiation or bleomycin. **Dogs with lymphoma showed ELEVATED sensitivity to chromosome damage at multiple doses and time points; GR with lymphoma 21.2x more likely to be classified "sensitive" than healthy GR (OR 21.2, 95% CI 2.3-195.8)**. Verified. Endpoint is chromatid gaps/breaks per 1,000 chromosomes, NOT survival, and the cells are germline, not malignant -- so it is **not** a tumour radiosensitivity measurement. It does say the direction: lymphoma-bearing dogs' cells repair radiation damage WORSE, i.e. are more radiosensitive. Grade: SUPPORTING, INDIRECT.
3. **PMID 7262204 (Cowall 1981)** -- in-vivo canine lymphoma response to 1.5 Gy total-body in 10 fractions, 3/5 remissions (Q3.1). An outcome, not a radiosensitivity parameter.
4. **Already in record: PMID 40088118** -- canine half-body irradiation, **T-cell PFI 292 d vs B-cell 2,127 d** (SWEEP_exists / SWEEP_hct_model). This is the one canine OUTCOME datapoint that argues T-cell disease does worse after radiation; note it is a 7-fold difference in durability, which the OSW in-vitro value (T MORE sensitive than the B/CLL median) does not explain -- so the T-cell disadvantage in dogs is most likely NOT intrinsic radioresistance but systemic/escape biology. Worth stating because it is a conflict between two record items that was not previously noticed.
5. **Human T-lineage, already in record: PMID 1429095 (Uckun 1992)** -- 42 primary T-lineage ALL/NHL samples: **mean SF2 0.36 +/- 0.04, alpha 0.558; CD3+ subset (n=28) SF2 0.441, D0 189.6 cGy; CD3- subset (n=14) SF2 0.189, D0 108.7 cGy**; 14/42 had SF2 >=0.50 with alpha <=0.2. Verified. This is the closest HUMAN T-lineage primary-cell value and the right fallback. Note OSW's SF2 of 0.61 is WORSE (more resistant) than the human primary T-lineage mean of 0.36-0.44 -- a cell line adapted to culture is expected to be, so the human primary value is the more flattering one and **rule 14 says use the lineage-and-species-matched measurement (OSW, SF2 0.61) over the more flattering human one.**

## FINISH -- the five answers asked for
1. **Canine standard whole-brain/intracranial dose and fractionation, and its late-toxicity profile.** **40 Gy in 10 daily 4 Gy fractions** is the definitive-intent canine standard (the "Zurich protocol"; 110 dogs PMID 42304866, 105 dogs PMID 41420297, 57-dog RCT PMID 35775129); **50 Gy in 20 x 2.5 Gy** for pituitary (PMID 41588441); stereotactic 15-35 Gy in 1-5 fractions is the common alternative. Fields are FOCAL/conformal, not whole-brain. Acute or early-delayed neurologic toxicity **5-10%, transient, corticosteroid-responsive**; severe LATE toxicity **not reported** in 110-dog, 105-dog, 57-dog or 46-dog series (median follow-up 498 d in the largest). The only canine late events found: 2 cases of hypoadrenocorticism at 67 and 232 d after a 50 Gy pituitary course, and **one presumptive radiation-induced trigeminal nerve sheath tumour at 1,724 days**. Canine dose-volume constraints now exist: Brain-GTV V32Gy <= 13 cm^3, whole-brain gEUD <= 30 Gy. Survival with RT is 432-1,174 d median depending on tumour, quality of life reported good to excellent.
2. **Is 23.4 Gy / 13 fx conservative, standard or aggressive for a dog?** **CONSERVATIVE.** Late-effect BED(alpha/beta 2) = 44.5 Gy2 against 120 Gy2 for 10 x 4 Gy and 112.5 Gy2 for 20 x 2.5 Gy -- **37-40% of the late dose dogs routinely receive**; fraction size 1.8 Gy is below every fraction size in canine practice and below the 3.0 Gy/fx threshold above which canine severe late effects appeared in the pelvic series; both published canine brain dose-volume constraints are satisfied by construction. Cord BED = 34.2 Gy3.9 against a 5%-myelopathy BED5 of 83.9 Gy3.9, so the craniospinal variant is also inside tolerance. The only canine thing it is NOT is routine: no canine series has used a whole-brain field at 1.8 Gy/fx, and the one canine craniospinal report is 4 dogs from 1984 with no dose given.
3. **Combination risk with intrathecal methotrexate, with numbers.** PMID 39269476 verified exactly: 126 CNSL patients, tLE cumulative incidence **29.2% (95% CI 20.6-38.2%) at 2 years**, **IT MTX the only independent risk factor, HR 4.50, P<0.001, dose-number-dependent**; median time to onset 3.0 months; **most cases asymptomatic**; WBRT was response-adapted and was NOT a risk factor. Against that: in the two prospective cohorts that used exactly 23.4 Gy / 13 fx (n=52 PMID 24101038 and n=29 PMID 35772168), **no patient developed Fazekas 4-5 white-matter change and neuropsychological scores were stable or improved out to 36 months**, and 23.4 Gy is the dose recommended specifically to lower neurotoxicity (PMID 32725379). Paediatric ALL went the other way and removed cranial RT entirely: >10,000 children across St Jude Total 15/16, EORTC 58951 and CCCG-ALL-2015 with CNS relapse **1.5-3.9%** on intrathecal therapy alone, including a T-ALL arm where extended triple intrathecal therapy matched cranial RT (PMID 19694717), and **T-cell phenotype is the only independent CNS-relapse risk factor (HR 5.15)**. Canine precedent for the combination: **one 1984 report, 4 dogs, intrathecal cytarabine + craniospinal cobalt irradiation, "marked improvement", no dose and no late follow-up (PMID 6547126)**. Defensible sequence for a 10-year canine goal: radiation first, 23.4 Gy / 13 x 1.8 Gy, no escalation; concentrate the methotrexate AFTER rather than concurrently (the 3.0-month onset window is the overlap); cap and space the methotrexate doses since the hazard scales with dose number; accept 13 anaesthetics as a real 1.3-6.3% cumulative mortality cost; and treat the **radiation-induced second tumour at ~4.7 years as the genuinely unquantified late endpoint** for a 10-year horizon.
4. **Could a low-dose schedule substitute?** **2 x 2 Gy: no.** It is a real human regimen (4 Gy / 2 fx: 55% CR in >=8 indolent-NHL series PMID 20970029; 82% ORR / 55% CR / 22-month median duration in a prospective phase 2 PMID 12459371; 88-100% ORR, no grade >=3 toxicity in 591 ocular-adnexal patients PMID 40940942), but in the model's units it delivers only **0.17-0.67 in-vivo e-folds against 0.90-3.71 for the 23.4 Gy course** -- a 4-6x loss, close to removing the radiation, which triples the methotrexate requirement. There is also **no low-dose whole-brain CNS-lymphoma series at all**. The schedule that deserves testing instead is **a single 8 Gy fraction: 1.37-3.40 in-vivo e-folds (equal to or MORE than 23.4 Gy for the resistant and median lines) at a slightly LOWER late-CNS BED (40 vs 44.5 Gy2) and one anaesthetic instead of thirteen** -- derived from the LQ fit, extrapolated beyond its fit range, with no CNS-lymphoma efficacy data: a hypothesis, not a supported substitute. **Omitting radiation entirely for ~3.5 years of intrathecal methotrexate** is what paediatric oncology actually did and is defensible on relapse grounds, but it trades a course with prospectively measured absence of neurotoxicity for 60-90 doses of the one agent with a measured dose-dependent leukoencephalopathy hazard -- so keeping the 23.4 Gy course to shorten the methotrexate, which is what the model already does, is the better-supported trade.
5. **State of primary canine T-cell radiosensitivity data.** **No clonogenic, apoptosis or ex-vivo radiosensitivity assay on primary canine lymphoma cells exists, T-cell or otherwise** -- that gap is confirmed, not closed. **But the record already contains a canine T-CELL LYMPHOMA line measurement and did not know it: OSW (SF2 0.61 / SF5 0.15, Maeda PMID 27257868) was established from a dog with peripheral T-cell lymphoma with an oligoclonal TCRgamma rearrangement (PMID 17532464).** Using it, 23.4 Gy gives **2.94 in-vivo e-folds** versus the 1.67 the model's headline T-cell-brain window is computed on -- so the model's T-cell radiation input is conservative and the lineage-matched window is shorter than 399 days. The closest human primary T-lineage values remain PMID 1429095 (SF2 0.36-0.44, D0 190 cGy), which are MORE sensitive than OSW; rule 14 favours the species-and-lineage-matched OSW number even though it is the less flattering one. Residual genuinely open: primary (non-immortalised) canine T-cell lymphoma radiosensitivity, cycling vs resting, from fine-needle samples.

## Addendum: one more reason craniospinal must use 1.8-2 Gy fractions, not the canine 4 Gy standard
All BEDs recomputed and checked: cord BED(alpha/beta 3.9) is **34.2 Gy3.9 for 23.4 Gy / 13 x 1.8 Gy**, but **81.0 Gy3.9 for 40 Gy / 10 x 4 Gy** and **82.1 Gy3.9 for 50 Gy / 20 x 2.5 Gy** -- both essentially AT the 5%-myelopathy BED5 of 83.9 Gy3.9 (PMID 26366252). Dogs tolerate those schedules because the field is a focal brain volume and the cord is largely out of it. **If the course is CRANIOSPINAL, the canine 4 Gy/fraction standard cannot be used: 1.8 Gy/fraction is required, which is exactly the schedule the model specifies.** So the model's unusual-for-a-dog fractionation is the correct choice for the field it uses, and this is a point in its favour rather than a deviation needing excuse.
