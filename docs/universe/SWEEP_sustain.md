## 1. Human cytarabine ocfosfate (Starasid, YNK01, SPAC)
All [NEW] unless marked (none of these PMIDs is in the record; grep of docs/ for the PMIDs/"Starasid" found only 37670479, 31769013, 1742843).
- LABEL SCHEDULE is INTERMITTENT, not continuous [NEW; web, not PubMed; PMDA-derived text via WebSearch, not opened as a label]: Starasid, AML 100-300 mg/day in 1-3 doses; MDS 100-200 mg/day "for 2 to 3 weeks every day, then discontinue 2-3 weeks and repeat". (100-200 mg/day = ~60-120 mg/m2 for a 1.7 m2 adult.) (unverified: not the official insert.)
- PMID 9766508 (Braess 1998, Leukemia 12:1618; doi 10.1038/sj.leu.2401152) HUMAN, MEASURED: 14-day cycles, 43 pts (AML 23, low-grade NHL 20). MTD 600 mg/m2 (grade 3-4 diarrhoea DLT). Metabolic bioavailability 15.8%; AraC t1/2 22.6 h. "YNK01 at 100-150 mg/m2 gave AraC plasma levels comparable to LDAC 20 mg/m2; 450-600 mg/m2 gave standard-dose (100 mg/m2) levels." Linear in dose. => human 14-day cycles; NHL 2 PR + 6 SD of 20.
- PMID 11960335 (Maloisel 2002, Leukemia 16:573; doi 10.1038/sj.leu.2402433) HUMAN, n=98 CML: 14 d/4 wk at 600 mg/day + IFN: "Most patients experienced side-effects and all permanently stopped YNK01" (initial dose "probably too high").
- PMID 15550587 (Mollee 2004, Ann Oncol 15:1810) HUMAN n=40, 600 mg/day x10 d monthly x1 y: toxicity led to discontinuation of one or both drugs in 60%.
- PMID 12646944 (Rosti 2003, Leukemia 17:554) HUMAN n=86, 600 mg/day 14 d/month + IFN: 25% discontinued within 1 y; 13.17 adverse events (mostly grade 1-2) per patient-year; "not because of severity but frequency, duration and repetition of the side effects".
- PMID 11736933 (del Canizo 2001, Br J Haematol 115:541) HUMAN, DAILY CONTINUOUS oral YNK01 + IFN, n=10 pilot: GI main side effect, macrocytic anaemia 3 pts. Duration of continuous dosing not in abstract (unverified).
- PMID 10867132 (Kuhr 2000, Leuk Res 24:583) HUMAN, CONTINUOUS 300 mg/day (escalate to 900) + IFN, advanced CML, 'pilot' (n small); one CCR at 18 mo after starting => continuous dosing >=18 months existed in >=1 patient (abstract; unverified for tolerability).
- PMID 25652695 (Arahata 2015, J Hematol Oncol 8:5; full text read) HUMAN case: 72-y woman, maintenance SPAC Nov 2011 to Oct 2013, "12 courses", "no significant toxicity", "continue the maintenance therapy for two years", molecular remission persisting off therapy. (Courses = intermittent.)
- PMID 24080768 / 18228114 (Horikoshi 2013, 2008) HUMAN: SPAC + etoposide oral induction in elderly AML/MDS "well tolerated"; plasma AraC accumulated with number of days of SPAC (18228114: "accumulation of ara-C with increasing numbers of days") => accumulation on repeat daily dosing is documented.
- PMID 9797816 / 9151927 (Suto 1997/98) HUMAN HCC, 200-300 mg/day x2 wk q4wk "as long as possible"; PR duration ~4 mo, NC ~3 mo; main AEs anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, GI; no grade >3 in 23 pts. (Longest course not given.)
- CONCLUSION Q1: NO human source documents continuous daily ocfosfate for years. Every long human schedule is 10-14 days on per 28 days at 100-600 mg/day, and at 600 mg/day (~350 mg/m2) 25-60% stop within a year for cumulative GI/marrow toxicity. Longest continuous: >=18 mo in a single advanced-CML patient (abstract-level). The dog PK dose 200 mg/m2/day is ~1.7-3.3x the human label MDS daily dose and is given every day.
  Intermittent vs continuous and the kill rate: Ara-CTP retained >25% of peak at 13 d in dog leukocyte DNA after 7 doses (37670479) is the only quantitative basis for bridging an off-week; 14-on/14-off therefore halves the dosed fraction (0.5 duty) and the model's kill_per_day should be scaled by duty (0.5-0.7 given the retention) -> 1431 d continuous becomes ~2000-2900 calendar days. ASSUMED scaling; no source measures kill under intermittent ocfosfate.

## 2. Dogs: cytarabine data
- [REC] 37670479: 7 daily doses only (n=4 healthy), CBC/flow follow-up to 21 d; "Peripheral blood lymphocyte concentrations remained within the reference range, proliferation after stimulation decreased at 6 d". No repeated-cycle or chronic data; no toxicity table in abstract.
- PMID 15084010 (Ide 2003, Aust Vet J 81:47; doi 10.1111/j.1751-0813.2003.tb11423.x) DOG, case: Shih Tzu with MDS treated with cytarabine ocfosfate in Tokyo and "appeared to recover" (duration/dose not in abstract; unverified) => ocfosfate has been given clinically to a dog in Japan; supports availability in Japan, not chronic safety. [NEW]
- PMID 41742567 (Majors 2026, JVIM 40(1); doi 10.1093/jvimsj/aalaf082; PMC12883064) DOG, n=43 MUO: cytarabine 600 mg/m2 EVERY 3-6 WEEKS for >=6 months or until death, minimum follow-up 24 months; 23/25 survived >24 mo (median 1027 d, range 770-1277); "No serious adverse events were noted". LONGEST canine cytarabine exposure found: >2.8 y but PULSED (q3-6 wk) and in a non-neoplastic disease; no continuous schedule. [NEW]
- PMID 31490575 (Jones 2019, JVPT 42:588): SC 200 mg/m2 AUC 32,510 ng.h/mL; 4 dogs, no AE, CBC at d7. [NEW]
- PMID 22943060 (Crook 2012): SC half-life 1.15-1.35 h; CRI 25 mg/m2/h gives steady Cmax 2.8 ug/mL (~11.5 uM). [NEW]
- PMID 27240905 [REC] CRI vs SC outcomes. PMID 38700360, 35754543: CA CRI adds nothing in MUO (not a toxicity point; irrelevant to lymphoma kill). 
- PMID 27062671 (Hart 2016, JVECC 26:844) DOG case: fatal drug-induced interstitial lung disease after 4th cytarabine CRI (300 mg/m2) with prednisone, MUO. A rare tail hazard of repeated cytarabine. [NEW]
- PMID 33898281: transdermal/rectal cytarabine: bioavailability BLQ (0.1 ug/mL). Generic cytarabine is not orally bioavailable (deamination) => oral route needs the prodrug. [NEW]

## 2b. !!! KEY DISCREPANCY: the record's "CSF 1.0-3.6 uM" for ocfosfate is not what PMC10658504 (PMID 37670479) measured [NEW; full text read]
- Paper (Results 3.3 + Table 3 + Discussion): "CSF Ara-C concentrations ranged from 9 to 64.9 ng/mL (0.04-0.27 uM) with CSF:serum ratios 0.54 to 1.2"; sampled at 24 h (3 dogs) and day 6 (2 dogs), cisternal, under GA. Matching SERUM at those times 16-54 ng/mL (0.07-0.22 uM). Serum Cmax 1.88-2.98 uM (456-724 ng/mL) is the PEAK after dose 1; discussion: "minimum Ara-C concentrations were <50 ng/mL (0.2 uM)". CO itself NOT detected in CSF.
- The record's 1.0-3.6 uM = (CSF:serum 0.54-1.2) x (serum Cmax 1.88-2.98 uM): a product of a TROUGH-sampled ratio and a PEAK serum value. It was written as "CSF 1.0-3.6 uM ... measured" in LYMPHOMA_UNIVERSE F/G, core/lymphoma_universe.py l.320-328 (setpoint csf_setpoint_nM=1000, "low end of the measured 1.0-3.6 uM range") and SWEEP_exists. Measured CSF is 0.04-0.27 uM = 4-25x BELOW the 1.0 uM setpoint. Grade should be DERIVED-from-product (not MEASURED) and the derivation is not supported by sampling times.
- Fair bracket (my arithmetic, unverified): single-dose AUC mean 3243 ng.h/mL (Table 2; whether AUC0-last or 0-inf is not stated) / 24 h = ~135 ng/mL = ~0.55 uM time-average serum; accumulation index 2.1 on repeat dosing -> steady-state average ~0.5-1.2 uM serum; x CSF:serum 0.54-1.2 -> time-average CSF ~0.3-1.4 uM. So 1.0 uM is the TOP of a plausible time-average band, not the low end; and the only direct CSF measurements (troughs) are 0.04-0.27 uM. Paper itself: below ~1 uM Ara-C uptake is transporter-limited; dog leukaemia-cell IC50 range 0.2-4.1 uM; 1 ug/mL (4.1 uM) is the in-vitro 90%-kill level.
- Same paper, tolerability at the PK dose: after only 7 daily doses of 200 mg/m2, 3/3 dogs had grade 1 neutropenia, 3/3 thrombocytopenia (1 grade 2, 99k), 1/3 mild anaemia; neutropenia resolved by d13, platelets by d13-20. Protocol said "7 doses or until grade 2 leukopenia". i.e. marrow suppression appears within one week; continuous dosing without recovery weeks is untested.
- Drug source: Starasid, Nippon Kayaku (Tokyo); reference standard from Nippon Kayaku. Paper says ocfosfate was reported in 9 cats and 1 dog with MDS. No import/compounding route stated. Starasid launched Japan 1992 (Yamasa/Nippon Kayaku; web), Japan-only; no US/EU veterinary or human licence found. Availability for dogs = research import only (unverified beyond that).
- Accumulation: Ara-C AI 2.1, CO AI 1.46; DNA-incorporated Ara-CMP "not saturated after 7 doses", elimination half-life 45-61 h, >25% of peak at day 13 (so the Ara-CTP depot does bridge ~1-2 weeks of missed dosing; supports 14-d off-periods being partly covered). Authors: "dosing intervals could be extended".

## 3. Long-term safety of the other components (dogs)
### Verdinexor
- [NEW] FDA FULL approval: Laverdia (NADA 141-614, FOI dated 2025-12-18; fda.gov page says full approval 2026-01-16; conditional Jan 2021 after four annual renewals) -- WebFetch of fda.gov page + FOI PDF text extracted (opened, quoted). Label: 1.25 mg/kg PO twice weekly (>=72 h apart), up to 1.5 mg/kg if tolerated at 2 wk; given after eating. Pivotal field study: 134 dogs analysed (106 verdinexor, 28 control), TTP 37 d (CI 29-57) vs 23 d (CI 14-30), p=0.011. The record's "licensed" tier-A label is now true in the US.
- LONG-TERM SAFETY BASIS = 13-week (13-cycle) laboratory study in 32 healthy beagles at up to 1.75 mg/kg THREE times weekly: vomiting, inappetence, weight loss, lymphocyte/eosinophil/monocyte decrease; lower testes, thymus, thyroid/parathyroid weights with histologic lesions in testes, epididymides, thymus. FOI: "an anti-neoplastic drug treating a terminal disease; adverse reactions are expected". No chronic (>13 wk) toxicology in the FOI.
- Longest documented continuous canine exposure: 17 months in one cutaneous T-cell lymphoma dog (PMID 39235783, abstract read, [REC]); FOI dose-expansion partial responses lasting 246 and 354 days (Phase II/pilot; not continuous-safety data). Pivotal dogs were followed to day 56 only (CBC/chem days 7-56). => measured continuous max ~1 y in a few dogs; 4 y = no data; chronic testicular/thymic lesions in a 13-wk study are cumulative-type findings -> GRADE for 4-y: ASSUMED (and the 13-wk lesions argue against assuming).
### Hydroxychloroquine
- [NEW] PMID 20374572 (Mauldin 2010, Vet Dermatol 21:373; doi 10.1111/j.1365-3164.2010.00867.x; PMC3294011): GSP dogs with exfoliative CLE: HCQ 5-10 mg/kg once daily for 8 wk, 7 mo, 9 mo, "no side effects"; two male dogs "maintained on hydroxychloroquine for another year" (so ~1-1.5 y in those two); authors: "first to address the safety of long-term administration of hydroxychloroquine, albeit in a small number of dogs". No ophthalmic examination reported in the abstract (unverified in full text).
- [NEW] PMID 21718370 (Oberkirchner 2011, Vet Dermatol 23:65): one dog, HCQ 5 mg/kg/day for a year, "adverse drug events were not seen".
- So the longest documented canine HCQ is ~1-1.5 y at 5-10 mg/kg/day in n<=3, and it is the DERMATOLOGY dose (5 mg/kg), not the antineoplastic MTD 12.5 mg/kg/day (PMID 24991836 [REC]). Human retinopathy ceiling <=5 mg/kg real weight/day (PMID 26992838 [REC]). Canine retinal toxicity data: NOT FOUND. Grade for 4 y at the dermatology dose: ASSUMED/TRANSFERRED (human 5 mg/kg for years is tolerated with <1% retinopathy at 5 y [REC]); at 12.5 mg/kg: no basis, CONTRADICTED by the human ceiling logic (2.5x).
