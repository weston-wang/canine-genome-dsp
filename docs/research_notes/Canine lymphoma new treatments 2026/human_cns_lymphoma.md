# New human treatments for CNS lymphoma (PCNSL, SCNSL, CNS prophylaxis), Jan 2025 to 31 Oct 2026, with path-to-dog assessment

Scope and method. Searched 31 Oct 2026: PubMed (entry date 1 Sep to 31 Oct 2026 for "central nervous system lymphoma", 119 hits, the first 40 titles screened; plus targeted 2025-2026 queries on BTK inhibitors, IMiDs/CELMoDs, CSF concentrations, bispecifics, CAR-T, intrathecal pemetrexed, focused ultrasound), web search (FDA/sponsor releases, ASH 2025 abstracts, ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTIS). Record check: case-insensitive grep of docs/LYMPHOMA_UNIVERSE.md, LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md, LYMPHOMA_DURABLE_RESPONSE.md and docs/universe/*.md for each drug name, PMID and NCT number. "Post-sweep" = dated on or after 1 Oct 2026 (the project's last sweeps ran 1-6 Oct 2026). Evidence strength is shown in square brackets.

Limits: the ASH abstract site (ashpublications.org) returned HTTP 403, so the ASH 2025 abstracts were read only through secondary sources. ASH 2026 abstracts were not found online as of 31 Oct 2026. EMA CHMP minutes and the PMDA list were not checked directly.

## Q1. New regulatory approvals (FDA / EMA / PMDA) for PCNSL or CNS-relapsed lymphoma, 2025-2026

### Takeaway
No new drug was approved for PCNSL by the FDA between Jan 2025 and 31 Oct 2026. The one FDA action was a label change: in Feb 2026 the FDA removed the PCNSL "Limitation of Use" from axi-cel (Yescarta). That change does not help a dog, because axi-cel is made from the patient's own human T cells. Tirabrutinib, approved in Japan since 2020, is under FDA review with a PDUFA date of **18 Dec 2026**. If it is approved, a US vet could lawfully use it extra-label from that date. Tirabrutinib has no canine data.

### Cited Findings
- **Axi-cel (Yescarta), FDA label update, 6 Feb 2026 [regulatory; supporting data phase 1]**. The FDA removed the earlier "Limitations of Use" for relapsed/refractory PCNSL. The supporting study was a Dana-Farber investigator-sponsored phase 1 trial with 18 patients (13 PCNSL, 5 SCNSL; primary endpoint safety). In the 13 PCNSL patients, neurologic toxicity occurred in 85% (11/13) and was grade 3 in 31% (4/13). Grade 3-4 events were hypotension 23%, encephalopathy 15% and seizure 15%. Median onset of neurologic toxicity was day 3 (range 1-9), median duration 59 days (52-87), and toxicity was still ongoing in 45% (5/11) at study withdrawal, death or data cutoff — [Gilead/Kite release, 6 Feb 2026](https://www.gilead.com/news/news-details/2026/fda-approves-label-update-for-kites-yescarta-for-relapsedrefractory-primary-central-nervous-system-lymphoma); [Dana-Farber](https://www.dana-farber.org/newsroom/news-releases/2026/dana-farber-research-helps-drive-fda-label-update-for-primary-cns-lymphoma); [TargetedOnc, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/fda-approves-label-update-for-axi-cel-for-r-r-primary-cns-lymphoma). **NOT IN RECORD** (grep for "Yescarta", "limitation of use", NCT04608487 and "Nayak" returned nothing; axi-cel itself is in SWEEP_cart_human.md).
  - Efficacy of the same trial (NCT04608487), reported at ASCO 2024 (before the window but not in the record): ORR 94%, CR 67%, median PFS 14.3 months, median OS 26.4 months, 1-year PFS 55%. CAR-T expansion was greater in responders and in patients with higher-grade ICANS, and was "comparable" to ZUMA-1. CAR-T cells in CSF expressed more PD-1, TIM-3 and BLIMP-1 than CAR-T cells in blood — [TargetedOnc](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/early-data-shows-axi-cel-s-potential-in-treating-r-r-primary-and-secondary-cns-lymphomas); [OncLive](https://onclive.com/view/axi-cel-shows-safety-clinical-activity-in-r-r-primary-and-secondary-cns-lymphomas). Strength: phase 1, single centre, n=18, response is a secondary endpoint.
- **Tirabrutinib (ONO-4059, Velexbru), US NDA [phase 2 pivotal; under FDA review]**. The FDA accepted the NDA for R/R PCNSL, announced 16-17 Feb 2026, with a **PDUFA target date of 18 Dec 2026** under the accelerated-approval pathway. The basis is PROSPECT (NCT04947319): n=48, tirabrutinib 480 mg orally once daily, median follow-up 11.5 months, ORR 67% (95% CI 52-80), CR/CRu 44% (29-59), median DOR 9.3 months (4.6-14.6), median PFS 6.0 months (5.3-11.1), median OS not reached, median time to response 1 month. The confirmatory phase 3, **NCT07104032**, compares tirabrutinib with rituximab + temozolomide and is recruiting — [OncLive, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-accepts-nda-for-tirabrutinib-in-relapsed-refractory-pcnsl); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04947319](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04947319); [ONO ASCO 2025 release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250528535854/en). Grade >=3 TEAE 56.3% (search-tool summary of the PROSPECT publication; primary not opened) — [ONO press PDF](https://prd.ono-pharma.com/sites/default/files/en/news/press/enews_ONO%20PHARMA%20Presents%20Positive%20Results%20from%20Pivotal%20Trial%20in%20U.S.%20Patients%20with%20Relapsed%20or%20Refractory%20PCNSL%20at%202025%20ASCO%20Annual%20Meeting%202025058.pdf). The Japanese approval dates from 25 Mar 2020, before the window — [Drugs.com history](https://www.drugs.com/history/tirabrutinib.html). **Record status: tirabrutinib itself is ALREADY IN RECORD** (universe/SWEEP_cnsregimens.md:82, the Japanese phase 1/2 data, PMID 38690230, with the dog columns marked "NOT FOUND"). **The US NDA, PROSPECT, the PDUFA date and NCT07104032 are NOT IN RECORD.**
- No FDA approval of any BTK inhibitor, IMiD/CELMoD or bispecific specifically for PCNSL or SCNSL was found in the window. A search for October 2026 CNS-lymphoma regulatory news found no item dated October 2026 — [search hub: Hematology Advisor](https://www.hematologyadvisor.com/news/fda-accepts-application-for-novel-r-r-primary-cns-lymphoma-treatment/).

### Inferences
- Tirabrutinib is the only near-term event that would change what a US vet can lawfully prescribe for the CNS. If the FDA approves it on or around 18 Dec 2026, extra-label use under 21 CFR 530 becomes lawful (the drug would be FDA-approved). It is not lawful before then.
- On the human data, tirabrutinib alone behaves like the other BTK inhibitors: responses last months, not years (median PFS 6 months). It would at best be an additional CNS-active maintenance or bridging agent, not a closer. This matches the record's verdict that BTK inhibitors are "EVALUATED, EXCLUDED as closers" (LYMPHOMA_UNIVERSE.md:145).
- The axi-cel label change confirms that IV CD19 CAR-T is now an FDA-sanctioned human CNS treatment. For the dog model it only strengthens the case for a canine CAR-T; it creates no lawful product for dogs.

### Gaps
- Tirabrutinib in dogs: no canine PK, toxicology or canine-BTK potency found. Searches were limited; ONO's nonclinical package, which may include dog toxicology, was not reachable.
- Tirabrutinib CSF:plasma ratio in humans: none found in the window. The PROSPECT reports carry no CSF data.
- Did not directly check EMA CHMP highlights 2025-2026 or the PMDA approval list. I found no evidence of any EU or Japanese PCNSL approval in the window, but that is a search gap, not a confirmed negative.
- Whether FDA action on tirabrutinib comes before 18 Dec 2026 is unknown.

## Q2. BTK inhibitors, IMiDs and CELMoDs in CNS lymphoma: new clinical readouts with numbers

### Takeaway
2025-2026 brought many phase 2 and retrospective studies, mostly from China, adding a BTK inhibitor (orelabrutinib, zanubrutinib, ibrutinib) or an IMiD (lenalidomide, pomalidomide) to HD-MTX-based induction. They report high CR rates (78-90%) and 1-2-year PFS of 72-94%, but follow-up is short. The only randomised phase 2 is French LOC-R01 (ibrutinib vs lenalidomide added to R-MPV). Golcadomide (a CELMoD) has entered CNS-lymphoma trials, with no efficacy data yet. Of these agents only zanubrutinib, ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, lenalidomide and pomalidomide are FDA-approved and therefore lawful extra-label for a US vet. Orelabrutinib and golcadomide are not.

### Cited Findings
- **LOC-R01, randomised phase 2 [phase 2]**. Ibrutinib + R-MPV, n=47: ORR 93%, CR 82%, 18-month PFS 82%, 18-month OS 88%. Lenalidomide + R-MPV, n=45: ORR 90%, CR 86%, 18-month PFS 72%, 18-month OS 87%. Abstract: Blood 2025;146:58 (ASH 2025). Read through a highlights review: Wei R, Yang S, J Hematol Oncol 2026;19:22, PMID 41957790 — [PMC13064001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13064001/), [DOI 10.1186/s13045-026-01786-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13045-026-01786-7). **NOT IN RECORD** (no "LOC-R01" hit).
- **ZANA (zanubrutinib + rituximab + HD-MTX, first line) [prospective single-arm]**. n=29: ORR 89.7%, CR 89.7%, 2-year PFS 82.8%, 2-year OS 95.2% — Blood 2025;146:60, [ASH abstract](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/60/548795/Zanubrutinib-rituximab-and-high-dose-methotrexate) (numbers taken from [PMC13064001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13064001/)). Real-world R-MZ, n=35: ORR 88.6%, median PFS 29 months, median OS 43 months — [Blood 2025;146:3699](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/3699/554802/Real-world-study-of-rituximab-methotrexate-and). Zanubrutinib is **ALREADY IN RECORD** (SWEEP_inhibitors_partial.md:58,125, CSF ratio from PMID 35004280; SWEEP_cnsregimens.md:89 canine/rat toxicology PMID 32484067). These two trials are NOT IN RECORD.
- **MSZ (HD-MTX + selinexor + zanubrutinib) [phase 2]**. n=21: ORR 95.2%, 1-year PFS 94.1%, 1-year OS 100%. The CR rate is ambiguous: the review's table gives "-" and its text gives 95.2%. Dosing: six 21-day cycles of HD-MTX 3.5 g/m2, selinexor 40 mg twice weekly and zanubrutinib 160 mg twice daily, then maintenance with selinexor + zanubrutinib in 13 patients — Li L et al., Blood 2025;146:3550, [ASH abstract page (403 to fetch; snippet via search)](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/3550/554819/From-mechanistic-synergy-to-clinical-breakthrough), [DOI 10.1182/blood-2025-3550](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2025-3550); numbers from [PMC13064001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13064001/). **NOT IN RECORD.** Its CSF result is under Q5.
- **Orelabrutinib combinations (all from China) [phase 2 / retrospective]**:
  - RMOT (orelabrutinib + R + temozolomide + HD-MTX) vs RMT, retrospective with propensity matching, n=101: CR 84.8% vs 63.2% (p=.026), PFS HR 0.357 (95% CI 0.150-0.850); in non-GCB patients PFS HR 0.274. Published 12 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42948599, [DOI 10.1093/noajnl/vdag262](https://doi.org/10.1093/noajnl/vdag262). NOT IN RECORD.
  - RBTL (rituximab + orelabrutinib + temozolomide + pomalidomide) induction, retrospective, n=40 (28 newly diagnosed, 12 R/R): ORR after induction 86.8% (CR/CRu 36.8%), best ORR 94.7% (CR/CRu 63.2%); at a median 18.5 months, 12-month PFS 74.7% and OS 71.3%. One treatment-related death from thrombocytopenia. Published 17 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42910270, [DOI 10.2147/BLCTT.S619996](https://doi.org/10.2147/BLCTT.S619996). NOT IN RECORD.
  - Ore-MIED / Ore-MTD in R/R CNS DLBCL, retrospective, n=37: ORR 89.2%, CR 51.4%, median PFS 7.0 months — PMID 40538656, [DOI 10.3389/fneur.2025.1570224](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2025.1570224). NOT IN RECORD.
  - R-MO (rituximab + MTX + orelabrutinib) first line, phase 2: ORR 97.1%, 1-year PFS 83.6% — [Blood Cancer J 2025, s41408-025-01278-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41408-025-01278-w). NOT IN RECORD (no orelabrutinib hit anywhere in the record).
  - OST (orelabrutinib + sintilimab + temozolomide) in R/R PCNSL, n=19: ORR 84.2%, CR 78.9% — [Blood 2025;146:5461](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/5461/552847/Orelabrutinib-sintilimab-and-temozolomide-OST-in). Orelabrutinib + sintilimab phase 2 (n=28, R/R) — [BMC Cancer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-026-17092-x). OPF (orelabrutinib + anti-PD-1 + fotemustine), n=27 evaluable: ORR 85.2%, CR 66.7%, median PFS 8.9 months, 2-year PFS 32% — Blood 2025;146:5465 via [PMC13064001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13064001/). POR/ROM (pomalidomide + orelabrutinib + R, then R + orelabrutinib + MTX), n=33: ORR 84.3%, 2-year PFS 56.2% (the review gives CR as both 78% and 59.3%) — Blood 2025;146:5471 via the same review. All NOT IN RECORD.
- **Golcadomide (CC-99282, CELMoD) [trials only, no data]**:
  - NCT07608731: phase 1 of golcadomide + pemetrexed + rituximab + dexamethasone in R/R CNS lymphoma, not yet recruiting (first posted about May 2026) — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07608731).
  - EU phase 2 HO176 (CTIS 2024-517051-12-00): golcadomide in R/R primary large B-cell lymphoma of the CNS, recruiting, start Sep 2025 — [CTIS](https://ctis.eu/trial/2024-517051-12-00).
  - No iberdomide or mezigdomide CNS-lymphoma study was found.
  - **NOT IN RECORD** (no golcadomide or iberdomide hits).
- **BTK-inhibitor maintenance** (retrospective, PMID 41770393): **ALREADY IN RECORD** (SWEEP_cnsregimens.md:83).
- **Acalabrutinib pilot in R/R PCNSL/SCNSL (NCT04548648, UNC)**: a web-search result said results were posted in June 2026, but the results tab returned no data when fetched — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04548648). Acalabrutinib is **ALREADY IN RECORD** (LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md:49,96, in the model). NCT04548648 is NOT IN RECORD.

### Inferences
- The consistent signal is that BTK inhibitors (or IMiDs) added to HD-MTX-based induction raise CR rates by about 10-20 points. That is induction intensification, not new evidence of durable (5-10-year) CNS cure. Follow-up is mostly 1-2 years. The record's existing judgement (BTKi / lenalidomide as non-closers) is not overturned.
- For the dog: zanubrutinib (FDA-approved; human corrected CSF/free-plasma ratio about 43%; canine toxicology already in the record) is the lawful BTK inhibitor with the best CNS-penetration data. The MSZ regimen is a direct human template for the project's XPO1 + BTK idea (verdinexor + acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib). Orelabrutinib, despite its CSF data, is not FDA-approved and is therefore not lawful extra-label for a US vet.
- BTK is targeted in dogs already: the record lists acalabrutinib with canine in-vivo measurements (LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md:49), so BTK-inhibitor activity on canine BTK is not the blocker. Penetration into the CNS of the specific agent is.

### Gaps
- Primary ASH 2025 abstracts (LOC-R01, ZANA, MSZ, POR/ROM, OPF) could not be opened (HTTP 403). The numbers come from one secondary review (PMID 41957790), which has internal inconsistencies, noted above.
- Golcadomide and iberdomide have no CNS or CSF data, no canine data, and are not FDA-approved.
- NCT04548648 (acalabrutinib) result values, including any acalabrutinib CSF:plasma ratio, were not retrievable.

## Q3. CAR-T in CNS lymphoma: CSF expansion, durable CNS remission, intrathecal/ICV CAR-T

### Takeaway
New in the window: an FDA label change (axi-cel, Q1), a 547-patient meta-analysis (pooled 12-month PFS 51%), a reassuring 12-month cognitive follow-up, and a rat study of minibeam radiotherapy + CAR-T. No human intrathecal or ICV CAR-T results in lymphoma were found. Registered trials exist (an ICV CD19 CAR-T trial in PCNSL, and a trial using an Ommaya reservoir). The record's CAR-T parameters (CR about 50%, 24-month PFS about 28%) remain consistent with the new data.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta-analysis, CD19 CAR-T in PCNSL/SCNSL** (23 studies, n=547, searched to 31 Aug 2025): pooled 12-month OS 64.5% (95% CI 49.0-76.0), 12-month PFS 51.0% (35.0-68.0); CR 52% in PCNSL and 40% in SCNSL; CRS 76% / 77%; ICANS 50% / 46%. Published 5 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42840206, [DOI 10.1002/jha2.70407](https://doi.org/10.1002/jha2.70407). NOT IN RECORD. (Another 2026 meta-analysis, PMID 41948499, is ALREADY IN RECORD at SWEEP_cart_human.md:49.)
- **Medium-term cognition after CAR-T for isolated CNS lymphoma** (Pitié-Salpêtrière, n=30, 21 with 12-month follow-up): acute neurotoxicity 63% (grade >=3 20%). MoCA improved from baseline 22.1 to 25.4 at 12 months (p<0.001). Published 2 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42826379, [DOI 10.1212/WNL.0000000000218561](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218561). NOT IN RECORD.
- **Minibeam radiotherapy (MBRT) + CD19 CAR-T, orthotopic PCNSL in immunodeficient rats [preclinical]**: MBRT 30 Gy followed by CAR-T five days later gave the longest median survival of the radiotherapy-containing arms and beat conventional-RT combinations, but did **not** significantly beat CAR-T alone. Published 8 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42898331, [DOI 10.1016/j.isci.2026.117827](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.117827). NOT IN RECORD. Relevant to the project's 23.4 Gy whole-brain + cell-therapy sequencing: conventional RT was not synergistic with CAR-T in this model.
- **CSF CAR-T phenotype** (axi-cel phase 1): CAR-T cells in CSF expressed more PD-1, TIM-3 and BLIMP-1 than blood CAR-T cells — [TargetedOnc](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/early-data-shows-axi-cel-s-potential-in-treating-r-r-primary-and-secondary-cns-lymphomas). NOT IN RECORD.
- **Intrathecal / ICV CAR-T in lymphoma**: no 2025-2026 human results found. Registered: an ICV CD19 CAR-T phase 1 in PCNSL (NCT05625594, target n=20, listed completion May 2026) — [HealthTree listing](https://healthtree.org/lymphoma/community/clinical-trials/list/NCT05625594). An IV CAR-T phase 1 with an Ommaya reservoir (NCT04532203, about 72 planned) — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04532203). Preclinical: ICV, but not IV, CAR-T eradicated CNS and systemic lymphoma in mice — [cancer.fr summary](https://en-www.cancer.fr/professionnels-de-sante/veille/nota-bene-cancer/bulletin-n-471/the-cerebroventricular-environment-modifies-car-t-cells-for-potent-activity-against-both-central-nervous-system-and-systemic-lymphoma). The record already states "no lymphoma IT/ICV CAR-T human series found" (SWEEP_near_future_cell.md:91; SWEEP_cart_human.md:214). **Status unchanged.** NCT05625594 and NCT04532203 are NOT IN RECORD.

### Inferences
- The 12-month PFS of about 51% pooled is higher than the record's EBMT 24-month PFS of 28% (PMID 40400509). The two are consistent with a continuing decline between 12 and 24 months, so the record's durable-fraction parameter (about 0.28 at 2 years) should stand.
- Human CAR-T products have no lawful or practical path to dogs. The only relevance is as evidence that a canine CD19/CD20 CAR-T could clear CNS disease, with the caveat that CAR-T cells in CSF look more exhausted. That favours combining with PD-1 blockade, or using CSF-delivered dosing.

### Gaps
- No CSF CAR-T cell count or kinetics data newer than what the record holds (CSF peak 0.19-0.50 cells/uL, SWEEP_cart_human.md:21).
- NCT05625594 results (completion listed May 2026) not found.

## Q4. Bispecific antibodies (glofitamab, epcoritamab, mosunetuzumab, odronextamab): CNS activity and CSF data

### Takeaway
The main new item is post-sweep: glofitamab + polatuzumab vedotin in 9 R/R CNS-lymphoma patients gave ORR 100% and CR 89%, with flow cytometry showing lymphoma cells cleared from CSF within days. Several case reports add epcoritamab and more glofitamab responses. No new CSF concentration data beyond the 0.44% already in the record were found. No data were found for odronextamab or mosunetuzumab in CNS lymphoma. Whatever the efficacy, the record's species cross-reactivity blocker (human CD3/CD20 arms do not engage dog cells, SWEEP_bispecific.md:17,147-151) means there is no path to dogs.

### Cited Findings
- **Glofitamab + polatuzumab vedotin, R/R CNS lymphoma (retrospective, n=9)**: ORR 100%, CR 89%. Responses occurred in both parenchymal and leptomeningeal disease, with "rapid clearance of lymphoma cells from the cerebrospinal fluid within days" by flow cytometry. Safety matched the systemic profile. Cases included chemorefractory Burkitt and TP53-mutant mantle cell lymphoma. Published 21 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42906182, [DOI 10.1182/bloodadvances.2026022200](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2026022200). NOT IN RECORD.
- **Glofitamab + lenalidomide, R/R CNS lymphoma (n=4)**: ORR 100% after 2 cycles; 3 CR/CRu after 4 cycles; no CRS, no ICANS, no grade >=3 haematological toxicity — PMID 41256314, [DOI 10.3389/fonc.2025.1685271](https://doi.org/10.3389/fonc.2025.1685271). NOT IN RECORD.
- **Case reports (n=1-2 each), all NOT IN RECORD**:
  - Epcoritamab: radiological CR of suspected CNS relapse of LBCL — PMID 42278654, [DOI 10.3390/ijms27115132](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27115132).
  - Glofitamab in HIV-related SCNSL — PMID 41783646, [DOI 10.3389/fonc.2025.1699475](https://doi.org/10.3389/fonc.2025.1699475).
  - Glofitamab + BTK inhibitor in CNS Burkitt lymphoma — PMID 41783442, [DOI 10.3389/fonc.2026.1724213](https://doi.org/10.3389/fonc.2026.1724213).
  - Glofitamab in isolated-CNS Richter transformation — PMID 40755773, [DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1635589](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1635589).
- **Already in record**: glofitamab CSF/plasma up to 0.44%, ORR 75% in 16 PCNSL patients (PMID 42035265, SWEEP_bispecific.md:57,174); the 84-patient glofitamab series (PMID 42579821, SWEEP_bispecific.md:58); the CUBIC series (PMID 42173834, SWEEP_bispecific.md:59); the epcoritamab case PMID 41867647 (SWEEP_regional.md:315); glofitamab cases PMIDs 42319957 and 40174220 (SWEEP_near_future_cell.md:113).

### Inferences
- Bispecifics clear CNS disease at very low CSF exposure (about 0.44% of plasma), which implies the T cells they recruit act in the brain and CSF. For the dog this supports a canine-specific CD3 x CD20/CD19 engager or a canine CAR-T. It does not support the human molecules.
- Glofitamab (FDA accelerated approval 2023) and polatuzumab vedotin are FDA-approved, so extra-label use is lawful. But cross-reactivity makes them inactive in dogs: per the record, human CD3 epsilon is 43% identical to canine, and rituximab-family anti-CD20 does not bind endogenous canine CD20.

### Gaps
- Polatuzumab (anti-human CD79b) cross-reactivity with canine CD79b: not found and not searched in depth.
- No odronextamab or mosunetuzumab CNS-lymphoma data found in the window.

## Q5. Measured CSF or brain-tissue concentrations of lymphoma drugs (including in non-enhancing tumour)

### Takeaway
The most consequential new finding for the project's brain program is the **first human selinexor CSF measurement**, reported from the MSZ phase 2 trial (ASH 2025): **CSF-to-plasma ratio of about 0.6%**, as quoted in a 2026 review. The project's verdinexor backup route rests on a preclinical selinexor brain:plasma ratio of 0.60-0.72 (PMID 27323910). If the human CSF number is right and comparable, human CSF exposure is about 100-fold lower than that nominal ratio. The finding is **NOT IN RECORD**: SWEEP_xpo1_cns.md:52 lists "selinexor CSF concentrations" as not found. No measured concentration of any lymphoma drug inside non-enhancing (intact-barrier) tumour was found in the window.

### Cited Findings
- **Selinexor, human CSF [phase 2 correlative; secondary source]**: "This study was the first to quantify selinexor concentrations in cerebrospinal fluid ... especially given the relatively lower CSF quantification (CSF-to-plasma ratio ~ 0.6%)", "compared to BTK inhibitors (~ 50%)". Source study: Li L et al., MSZ trial, Blood 2025;146:3550 ([DOI 10.1182/blood-2025-3550](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2025-3550)). Quoted from Wei R, Yang S, J Hematol Oncol 2026;19:22, PMID 41957790 — [PMC13064001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13064001/). **NOT IN RECORD** (SWEEP_xpo1_cns.md:52 records it as a gap). Dated Dec 2025 / Apr 2026, so pre-sweep but missed.
  - Caveats: (1) the primary abstract could not be opened (HTTP 403), so n, timing, absolute concentrations, and whether the ratio uses total or free plasma are unknown; (2) "~0.6%" in a secondary review could be a transcription of 0.6 (60%). The phrase "relatively lower ... compared to BTK inhibitors (~50%)" supports a genuinely low value.
  - Clinical context: the regimen still produced ORR 95.2% and 1-year PFS 94.1%, but alongside HD-MTX and zanubrutinib, so selinexor's own contribution in the CNS cannot be separated out.
- **Selinexor/XPO1 CNS trials registered with CSF endpoints**:
  - SELINA (NCT07191431): orelabrutinib + selinexor + MTX, thiotepa or temozolomide in R/R PCNSL/SCNSL; selinexor blood and CSF levels at 4 timepoints are an exploratory endpoint — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07191431).
  - NCT07002099: selinexor + HD-MTX + rituximab + RT, phase 2 — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07002099).
  - Both NOT IN RECORD.
- **Orelabrutinib, human CSF [phase 2 correlative; pre-window, not in record]**: median CSF concentration 28.7 ng/mL (range 11.8-52.7); median CSF/free-plasma ratio 59.8% (range 46.09-86.67%); 5 paired samples from 4 patients (orelabrutinib + sintilimab trial in R/R PCNSL) — [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/orelabrutinib-sintilimab-combo-induces-rapid-responses-in-peripheral-cns-lymphoma). NOT IN RECORD.
- **Venetoclax detected in CSF** in a Bing-Neel syndrome patient on venetoclax + zanubrutinib, who cleared abnormal CSF B cells. The abstract gives no concentration. Published 23 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42937731, [DOI 10.1097/MD.0000000000051158](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000051158). Venetoclax CSF numbers are ALREADY IN RECORD (CSF/total plasma about 0.0026-0.0074; SWEEP_pk.md:255, SWEEP_outside_model.md:96). The case report itself is NOT IN RECORD.
- **Glofitamab**: CSF/plasma up to 0.44% — ALREADY IN RECORD (PMID 42035265).
- **Zanubrutinib**: corrected CSF/plasma 42.7% +- 27.7% — ALREADY IN RECORD (PMID 35004280).
- **Lenalidomide** (>=20% CSF/plasma, PMID 29986852) and **pomalidomide** (rat about 39%, PMID 23940785) — ALREADY IN RECORD (SWEEP_cnsregimens.md:85-86).

### Inferences
- Implication for verdinexor. Selinexor is highly protein-bound (about 95%, per the XPOVIO label — value from memory, not re-fetched this session). If the 0.6% is CSF relative to total plasma, CSF relative to free plasma would be about 12%. That is still well below the free-drug equilibrium implied by a brain:plasma ratio of 0.6-0.72. The likely reason is efflux at the barrier: selinexor is a reported P-gp/BCRP substrate in some datasets, though that was not verified here. The project should treat the "verdinexor brain access" backup as **weakened**: the human CSF signal is low, it is unmeasured in dogs, and it is especially doubtful for a P-gp-high cell behind an intact barrier. Verdinexor is in any case a different molecule from selinexor, with no CNS distribution data (record gap SWEEP_xpo1_cns.md:52).
- CSF is not brain interstitial fluid. A low CSF ratio does not prove low brain-tissue exposure, which is what the mouse brain:plasma number measured. The two numbers measure different compartments in different species. Both should be carried, and the human CSF value should not simply replace the mouse value.

### Gaps
- Primary MSZ abstract data: n, sampling times, absolute ng/mL values, total vs free plasma.
- No measured drug concentration in **non-enhancing** (intact-barrier) human lymphoma tissue was found for any agent in 2025-2026.
- No acalabrutinib or tirabrutinib human CSF numbers found in the window.
- No canine CSF or brain data for any of these agents found (verdinexor, zanubrutinib, lenalidomide).

## Q7. Killing quiescent or dormant lymphoma cells in the CNS; focused-ultrasound BBB opening with lymphoma drugs in patients

### Takeaway
Nothing found. No 2025-2026 human study used focused-ultrasound BBB opening with any lymphoma drug, and no clinical or preclinical CNS-lymphoma study specifically targeted quiescent, drug-efflux-high progenitors. The project's hardest escape (a quiescent P-gp-high progenitor behind an intact BBB) has no new human countermeasure. The nearest relevant evidence is long-term follow-up showing that very late relapses still occur after WBRT-containing therapy.

### Cited Findings
- PubMed "focused ultrasound blood-brain barrier lymphoma", 2025-2026: 2 hits, both reviews (PMID 42793416, [DOI 10.3390/brainsci16090991](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci16090991); PMID 40113789, [DOI 10.1038/s41420-025-02358-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41420-025-02358-3)). No patient data with lymphoma drugs. Focused ultrasound is ALREADY IN RECORD (SWEEP_brain_access.md, SWEEP_modalities.md).
- C5R long-term follow-up (n=43, median 35 years): 3 patients disease-free at 27, 28 and 44 years; late relapses, neurocognitive decline (projected 60% at 20 years) and second cancers (28%) were common — PMID 42685570, [DOI 10.1016/j.ejca.2026.117010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2026.117010). **ALREADY IN RECORD** (SWEEP_cnsregimens.md:59).
- Lower-intensity chemotherapy backbones (relevant because these drugs are dog-lawful), both NOT IN RECORD:
  - Dose-modified HD-MTX 3.5 g/m2 every 21 days + rituximab + temozolomide, n=40: CR 77%, 2-year PFS 73%, OS 78% (median follow-up 33.9 months). Published 22 Sep 2026 — PMID 42959631, [DOI 10.1016/j.bneo.2026.100315](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bneo.2026.100315).
  - Real-world RTOG-0227 MTR, n=36: ORR 89%, CR 25%, median PFS 6.0 years; 5-year PFS 63% in responders. Published 7 Oct 2026, **POST-SWEEP** — PMID 42951740, [DOI 10.1016/j.clml.2026.10.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clml.2026.10.005).

### Inferences
- With no clinical anti-dormancy or FUS data in CNS lymphoma, the project should keep treating this escape as covered only by radiation (which is cell-cycle-independent to a degree) plus repeated IT methotrexate. Neither is designed for a P-gp-high quiescent cell, although IT dosing bypasses the BBB. Temozolomide (FDA-approved, lipophilic, given to dogs) appears in both new backbone series and is the most plausible lawful systemic addition. Its activity against non-dividing cells is limited, and MGMT status matters.

### Gaps
- No study measured drug penetration into non-enhancing PCNSL in the window.
- No quiescence or dormancy biology study specific to PCNSL found (consistent with the existing record note at SWEEP_cnsregimens.md:204).
