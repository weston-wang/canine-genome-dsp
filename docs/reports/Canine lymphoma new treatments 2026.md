# One spinal-fluid number reshapes the brain plan

**Bottom line.** No new lymphoma drug, antibody, vaccine or cell therapy for dogs was approved or licensed anywhere between 1 and 31 October 2026. The only lymphoma approval in 2025-2026 is Laverdia's move to full approval, and the record already has it. The one finding that changes the project's conclusion is an older human measurement the record missed. A November 2025 conference abstract reports the first measurement of selinexor in human spinal fluid (selinexor is the human sister drug of verdinexor, the dog drug). When we put that measurement into the model, it shows the brain schedule the project reported for its lawful programs depended on an optimistic guess at how much verdinexor reaches the brain. That reported schedule was one spinal-fluid injection of methotrexate every 3 weeks: 20 injections for B-cell disease and 11-12 for T-cell disease. At the measured level, that schedule never clears either brain. Both brains still close in the model if methotrexate is given more often, and the four cases now stand as follows. **B-cell body: closed (unchanged). T-cell body: closed (unchanged). T-cell brain: closed, with 17-24 injections every 10-14 days, which is inside what dogs have actually received (20-28). B-cell brain: closes in the model, but needs 29-45 injections, against 20-28 in canine series. Whether a dog tolerates that many is now the project audit's single failing input (graded ASSUMED: no canine series and no written human transfer yet).** The brain closure is also no longer "two independent routes, either of which suffices". It now rests on the intrathecal methotrexate (the drug injected into the spinal fluid). Every other item in this sweep either has no lawful path to a US dog yet, or adds options without changing any of the four verdicts. The dog-side news worth knowing: the canine CD20 CAR-T trial (LEAH Labs with the Universities of Minnesota and Missouri) is now enrolling, which moves it from "on hold" to "in a dog trial". Four other dog studies are missing from the record: the ORBIT virus-plus-checkpoint trial, a Zoetis under-the-skin anti-CD20 antibody, the Tufts/NCI antibody-chemotherapy trial, and an 82-dog Bellingham adoptive T-cell series.

## A human spinal-fluid measurement cuts verdinexor's assumed brain reach about threefold

Some background first. The project's lawful brain programs for both lineages combine four things: the body program, one course of whole-brain/spinal radiation (23.4 Gy), repeated injections of methotrexate into the spinal fluid (intrathecal methotrexate), and verdinexor (Laverdia) tablets. In the model, verdinexor's contribution in the brain depends on one number. That number is the fraction of the drug's free, active level in blood that also turns up as free drug in the brain. The record set it at **0.30**. It chose that as a value "below" a measured **total brain-to-plasma ratio of 0.60-0.72** for selinexor in mice, rats and monkeys ([PMID 27323910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27323910/)) ([LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md K.4](../LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md)). That animal ratio counts drug stuck to brain tissue as well as drug free to act. So it is not the same quantity as free drug, which is what the model compares with a kill threshold. The record also listed "selinexor CSF concentrations" as **not found** (`docs/universe/SWEEP_xpo1_cns.md:52`).

A measurement now exists. It comes from the MSZ phase 2 trial in Dalian, China: high-dose methotrexate + selinexor + zanubrutinib in 21 people with CNS lymphoma. The trial was published as an ASH 2025 abstract (Li L et al., *Blood* 2025;146(Suppl 1):3550, dated 3 Nov 2025). The publisher's page blocked access, so the full text was read from the publisher's own deposit in Crossref ([Crossref record, DOI 10.1182/blood-2025-3550](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1182/blood-2025-3550)). The abstract reports a mean peak plasma selinexor of **204.67 ng/mL** 4 hours after a 40 mg dose, a CSF (spinal fluid) level of **0.919 ng/mL** at the same time, and a "stable CSF-to-plasma ratio (~0.6%)" from 4 to 25 hours. The FDA label says selinexor is **95% bound to plasma proteins** ([openFDA Xpovio label](https://api.fda.gov/drug/label.json?search=openfda.generic_name:selinexor&limit=1)). So free plasma drug is about 10.2 ng/mL, and spinal fluid divided by free plasma comes to **about 0.09**. That is roughly a third of the 0.30 the model used, and seven to eight times below the animal ratio it was inferred from. A 2026 review citing this abstract describes the CSF exposure the same way: low compared with BTK inhibitors ([Wei & Yang, J Hematol Oncol 2026, PMC13064001](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13064001/fullTextXML)). The record's two animal figures (total brain 0.60-0.72; human contrast-enhancing tumour 0.09, [PMID 34728525](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34728525/)) do not contradict the new number. They measure different things. A heavily protein-bound, fat-soluble drug can pile up bound in brain tissue while its free level stays low.

The project's rule 14 says to search the measurable stand-in for a quantity, and to prefer a measurement in the compartment at issue over a more flattering inference. Spinal fluid is the standard measurable stand-in for free drug in the fluid around brain cells, so the 0.09 figure outranks the 0.30. The caveats need to be stated just as plainly:

- The drug measured is **selinexor, not verdinexor**. They are sister compounds of the same chemical series, so this is a cross-drug transfer, graded TRANSFERRED.
- It is **spinal fluid, not brain tissue**. No brain-tissue value exists in humans.
- The source is a **conference abstract**. It does not say how many patients were sampled, which cycle was sampled, or whether plasma was total or free.
- The abstract's own numbers give **0.45%**, not its stated 0.6%. Using the stated 0.6% instead gives a free ratio of about 0.12, which changes none of the conclusions below.
- No verdinexor spinal-fluid or brain measurement exists in any species. That is a reason to transfer from the sister compound, not an open gap ([verification note](../research_notes/Canine%20lymphoma%20new%20treatments%202026/verification_selinexor_csf_and_leah.md)).

A second human measurement is planned in the SELINA trial (spinal-fluid selinexor at four time points), with no results yet ([NCT07191431](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07191431)).

## Both brains still close, but the B-cell brain now needs more injections than any dog has had

The model was re-run with the lawful programs unchanged except for verdinexor's brain reach. The intrathecal methotrexate kill rate comes from the model's own derivation, which uses the measured canine spinal-fluid volume and half-life ([model re-run note](../research_notes/Canine%20lymphoma%20new%20treatments%202026/model_rerun_selinexor_csf.md)). For this report, the key rows were reproduced with the same project code (`lymphoma_sustained`, `lymphoma_grounded.evaluate_best_schedule`), and the grid was extended to intermediate intervals. "Never" means the brain compartment is not cleared within 15 years.

The first result is that the reported program does not survive the correction. The reported schedule gives methotrexate every 21 days and assumes the cautious (pessimistic) potency, IC50 118 nM. IC50 is the drug level that kills half the cells. Under those assumptions the schedule clears the B-cell brain in 420 days (20 injections) and the T-cell brain in 241 days (11 injections), but **only at verdinexor brain reach 0.30**. At the measured 0.09 it **never** clears either brain. The same is true at 0.12 and at the central potency (IC50 14 nM). The every-3-weeks schedule needs a verdinexor brain reach of at least about **0.18 for B-cell and 0.25-0.30 for T-cell disease**, which is two to three times what the human measurement supports. The record's claim that "either input suffices" was tested at half-strength methotrexate (a kill rate of 0.06 per day). An injection every 3 weeks at the pessimistic potency delivers only 0.044 per day, so the reported schedule always needed both inputs ([LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md K.4 item 1](../LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md)).

The second result is that more frequent methotrexate restores closure for both lineages at the measured level:

| Methotrexate potency (IC50) | Injection interval | B-cell brain, reach 0.09 | T-cell brain, reach 0.09 | T-cell brain, verdinexor removed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 118 nM (pessimistic) | every 10 days | 451 d = **45 injections** | 241 d = **24 injections** | 241 d = 24 |
| 118 nM | every 14 days | never | never | never |
| 14 nM (central, human T-ALL anchor) | every 14 days | 483 d = **34 injections** | 241 d = **17 injections** | 241 d = 17 |
| 2.5 nM (favourable, secondary citation) | every 14 days | 409 d = 29 injections | 231 d = 16 injections | 241 d = 17 |
| 2.5 nM | every 16-17 days | 451-483 d = **28 injections** | not run | not run |

The canine record for repeated spinal-fluid dosing is a series of dogs carrying under-the-skin CSF ports, dosed every other week for 8-11 months, for **20-28 injections** (already in the record, `LYMPHOMA_UNIVERSE.md` K.6). Against that:

- **T-cell brain.** It closes inside canine precedent at every potency tested: 24 injections every 10 days at the pessimistic potency, 17 every 14 days at the central one. Verdinexor's brain reach no longer matters for it, because removing verdinexor gives the same window.
- **B-cell brain.** It still closes in the model, but at the central potency it needs **34 injections over about 16 months**, and at the pessimistic potency **45**. Only the favourable potency, which rests on a secondary citation, with dosing every 16-17 days brings it down to **28**, the top of the canine range. Verdinexor at 0.09 still contributes here. Without it, the central-potency 14-day schedule never clears.

The two sub-compartments have also changed roles. The record expected methotrexate to cover the spinal fluid and the spaces around blood vessels, and verdinexor to cover the brain tissue itself. With verdinexor's reach cut to 0.09, brain-tissue coverage now leans mostly on the single radiation course.

The model code now carries the measured value as `lymphoma_sustained.VERDINEXOR_CNS_ACCESS_MEASURED = 0.09`, and `lawful_brain_window()` uses it as the default for verdinexor's brain reach (the catalogue's generic 0.05 is left untouched). A test in `tests/test_lymphoma_universe.py` (`test_measured_selinexor_csf_removes_the_brain_redundancy_...`) asserts the result above. The project's standard audit was run before stating any verdict, as rule 11 requires.

- **`lymphoma_standard_audit.failing()` returns exactly one item (run 31 Oct 2026):** "B: tolerability of 29-45 intrathecal methotrexate doses over 409-451 d", graded **ASSUMED**. The audit's stated reason is that canine precedent is 20-28 doses (CSF ports, 8-11 months), and there is "no canine series and no written human transfer yet for this many". The T-cell brain schedule (17-24 doses over about 241 days) passes because it sits inside canine precedent. The audit's 29-45 range covers the 10-14-day schedules. The extended grid above finds 28 injections at the favourable potency with dosing every 16-17 days, which is the top edge of precedent and rests on a secondary-citation potency. So it does not remove the failing item.
- **`routes_not_counted()`** now records the old schedule ("brain closure on 20 (B) / 11-12 (T) intrathecal doses every 21 days") as **CONTRADICTED**. Its line "T-cell brain from existing agents: OPEN" was stale-looking and has been relabelled: it lists only the set-aside section-I route (sustained oral cytarabine or CD7/CD5 CAR-T, without intrathecal methotrexate); the T-cell brain itself closes by radiation + intrathecal methotrexate.
- **`wrongly_reported_as_gaps()`** lists five items: no canine CAR-T efficacy measured, no drug level in gadolinium-non-enhancing lymphoma, no canine engager, MHC loss under a matched graft, and unattributed multidrug resistance. Under the project's standard none of these may be presented as open, and none is presented as open here.

Here is the verdict as a decidable list per case, not as odds:

| Case | Record as of 6 Oct 2026 | Verdict 31 Oct 2026 | What carries the closure | Condition with no written basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B-cell body | Closed (lawful program, verdinexor on-label ~168 d) | **Closed, unchanged** | unchanged | none new |
| T-cell body | Closed | **Closed, unchanged** | unchanged | none new |
| T-cell brain | Closed, 11-12 injections every 21 d, "redundant" | **Closed**, 17-24 injections every 10-14 d, inside canine precedent; not redundant | intrathecal methotrexate + one 23.4 Gy course + T-cell program; verdinexor not needed | none new |
| B-cell brain | Closed, 20 injections every 21 d, "redundant" | **Closes in the model; open at the project's bar on one input** (the only item `failing()` returns) | intrathecal methotrexate + radiation + verdinexor at 0.09 | that a dog tolerates 29-45 intrathecal injections over about 13-16 months (ASSUMED): canine precedent is 20-28, and human long-course precedent (TT16's 13-27 per child; long-course reservoir dosing in leptomeningeal disease) has not yet been searched for this question |

One more consideration, not a new gap: more frequent injections increase exposure to repeated intrathecal methotrexate. The record already named that as "the agent to watch" for late white-matter injury, and it already named procedural risk (port infection, catheter blockage, anaesthetic deaths) as the largest unmodelled risk. Both now weigh more heavily on the B-cell brain schedule ([LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md K.4 items 3 and 7](../LYMPHOMA_STATUS.md)).

## (a) Genuinely new since 1 October 2026: a dog CAR-T trial reopened, little else reaches dogs

**Strength labels used in this report:** approved / in a dog trial / human trial / abstract or press only / preclinical.

**The canine CD20 CAR-T trial is now enrolling [in a dog trial].** On 4 October the record read both institutional pages as ON HOLD. On 31 October the University of Minnesota page says "Current status: Open and enrolling" and names LEAH Labs as sponsor ([UMN CAR-T page](https://vetmed.umn.edu/departments/centers-and-programs/clinical-investigation-center/current-clinical-trials/car-t-cell)). Missouri's page says it "is enrolling patients" ([Missouri trials](https://vhc.missouri.edu/small-animal-hospital/oncology/clinical-trials/current-clinical-trials/)). LEAH's homepage says "Trial open — actively enrolling" at Minnesota, Ohio State and Missouri ([leahlabs.com](https://leahlabs.com/)). Ohio State rests only on the company's word.

The regimen is new to the record. It starts with two drugs that clear out the dog's own lymphocytes to make room for the CAR-T cells: a pentostatin bolus, then cyclophosphamide on day -3. The CAR-T cells are then given both by vein and by injection into lymph nodes. Eligible dogs have newly diagnosed or relapsed B-cell disease, substage A, weigh at least 10 kg, and are not boxers. No cell dose is published. The only outcome evidence is press-level. Two dogs with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma reached remission ([dvm360, Sep 2025](https://www.dvm360.com/view/new-cancer-therapy-for-dogs-shows-promise)), and a Newsweek feature (undated) paraphrases the investigator as saying both relapsed "months later" ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/dog-cancer-almost-back-normal-after-pioneering-new-treatment-2023820)). Under rule 13 this agent moves from "on hold" to **NEAR-FUTURE, in dog trials (enrolling)**. It is lawfully reachable only by enrolment, it does not reach the spinal fluid by design, and nothing published bears on how long the cells persist. So it does not restore redundancy to the brain closure. One of the research notes still describes these sites as on hold; that note was written before the verification and is superseded.

**Adoptive T cells after CHOP, 82 dogs [published, retrospective].** A JAVMA series from Bellingham (5 Oct 2026) reports dogs with B-cell lymphoma given their own expanded T cells after CHOP chemotherapy. Median survival from diagnosis was **660 days**, time to next treatment after the T cells **317 days**, and adverse events occurred in 5% ([PubMed 42833272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42833272/)). There was no control arm and the dogs were selected (all had finished CHOP), so this is a consolidation option for the B-cell body, not evidence of a durable cure. It does not displace low-dose-rate half-body radiation (progression-free 532 vs 296 days, already in the record) as the best-supported consolidation.

**Human items after 1 October, none with a path to a US dog that changes a verdict:**

- **Glofitamab + polatuzumab [human, retrospective, n=9].** In relapsed CNS lymphoma, 100% responded and 89% had complete responses, with lymphoma cells cleared from spinal fluid "within days" ([PubMed 42906182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42906182/)). Human CD3/CD20 engagers do not bind dog cells, so this supports building a canine engager but is not usable in a dog.
- **CAR-T for CNS lymphoma, meta-analysis [human, 547 patients].** Pooled 12-month progression-free survival was 51% ([PubMed 42840206](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42840206/)). That is consistent with the record's 28% at 24 months.
- **Rat minibeam radiation + CAR-T [preclinical].** Conventional radiation was not synergistic with CAR-T in this model ([PubMed 42898331](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42898331/)). This is relevant to any future radiation + cell-therapy sequencing.
- **Chinese BTK-inhibitor regimens [human, retrospective].** Orelabrutinib combinations (RMOT, RBTL) raised complete-response rates ([PubMed 42948599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42948599/); [PubMed 42910270](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42910270/)). Orelabrutinib is not FDA-approved, so a US vet cannot use it lawfully.
- **Cochrane review of donor transplant in mature T-cell lymphoma [human, randomised trials, low certainty].** First-line donor vs own-cell transplant showed no survival difference and much higher treatment-related death ([PubMed 42905121](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42905121/)). This is the strongest-design human evidence touching the T-cell program's matched-sibling transplant. It compares two transplants, not transplant vs none, so it does not contradict the record's graft-versus-lymphoma transfer.
- **CD7 CAR-T and CD28 CAR [human and preclinical].** A five-year survival report for donor CD7 CAR-T has no abstract released ([PubMed 42845047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42845047/)). A CD28-directed CAR spares CD8 and NK cells ([PubMed 42844286](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42844286/)). Neither has a canine reagent.
- **EMA's October veterinary committee [regulatory].** It gave no oncology opinions ([EMA CVMP Oct 2026](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/meeting-highlights-committee-veterinary-medicinal-products-cvmp-6-8-october-2026)).

Smaller post-sweep dog items:

- A re-publication of a 178-dog doxorubicin-vs-epirubicin stomach-and-gut side-effect study ([PubMed 42947482](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947482/)).
- An n=1 vulvar relapse after CHOP ([PubMed 42944196](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42944196/)).
- Eight canine CD30-positive T/NK lymphomas characterised by immunohistochemistry ([PubMed 42935134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42935134/)). This confirms a CD30-defined subset exists, but there is still no canine CD30 therapeutic.

## (b) Older items the record had missed: the selinexor number, new dog trials, and one lawful T-cell drug

The selinexor spinal-fluid abstract above is itself in this category: it was published in November 2025, before the October sweeps, and the record listed it as not found. The other missed items that matter fall into four groups.

**Dog trials and products the record did not have:**

- **ORBIT at Minnesota [in a dog trial].** Oncolytic VSV, a virus that infects and kills cancer cells, followed by an anti-PD-1 checkpoint antibody, in dogs with aggressive B- *or* T-cell lymphoma, newly diagnosed or relapsed. Phase 2 is "Open and enrolling", and the antibody and virus construct are not named ([UMN ORBIT page](https://vetmed.umn.edu/departments/centers-and-programs/clinical-investigation-center/current-clinical-trials/orbit-vsv)). A single-dog VSV-then-CHOP study with serial single-cell sequencing preceded it ([PubMed 42779884](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42779884/)). It matters because it accepts T-cell disease, and the record found anti-PD-1 alone gave 0 responses in 15 lymphoma dogs.
- **Zoetis subcutaneous, fully canine anti-CD20 antibody [abstract only, snippet strength].** Sixteen dogs with B-cell lymphoma received escalating doses up to 10 mg/kg, with no dose-limiting toxicity and B-cell depletion in all dogs at 5-10 mg/kg (VCS 2026 supplement, [DOI 10.1111/vco.70098](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/vco.70098)). The abstract itself was blocked; only search snippets were read. This is the first primary-source sighting of a Zoetis lymphoma antibody, which the record treated as an unverified market claim. A fully canine antibody should provoke fewer anti-drug antibodies over long dosing. It would matter for the B-cell body if licensed, and the record excluded anti-CD20 from the lawful programs precisely because none is licensed. It does not reach the brain.
- **Tufts/NCI anti-CD20 + chemotherapy + immunomodulator trial [press only].** More than 70 dogs with DLBCL received doxorubicin, an anti-CD20 antibody, and one of KPT-9274, RV1001 or TAK-981. A news release reports median survival **455 vs 180 days** against chemotherapy alone, about 20% long-term survivors, and one German Shepherd (TAK-981 arm) in remission five years after diagnosis ([Tufts news, Feb 2026](https://vet.tufts.edu/news-events/news/german-shepherd-clinical-trial-still-remission-five-years-after-lymphoma-diagnosis)). The comparator is unclear. It is the only report found of a lymphoma dog in remission at five years, at n=1.
- **An unnamed oral drug at UGA [in a dog trial].** Daily oral dosing for 84 days in newly diagnosed large B-cell lymphoma ([UGA CVM](https://vet.uga.edu/clinical-trial/dogs-diagnosed-with-b-cell-lymphoma/)). It is probably, but not verifiably, the BTK inhibitor BI 705564 already in the record.
- **Laverdia went on sale in the US on 23 Sep 2026 [approved].** Dechra's launch, at 1.25 mg/kg twice weekly rising to 1.5 mg/kg ([dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/laverdia-now-available-for-purchase-for-dogs-with-lymphoma)), turns the programs' core body agent from "approved" into "orderable". The record holds three different dates for the full approval. The FDA's own announcement of 16 Jan 2026 is the authoritative public date ([FDA CVM](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/cvm-updates/fda-grants-full-approval-first-oral-treatment-lymphoma-dogs)).

**Dog chemotherapy and supportive-care papers [published].** None of these touches the dormant, drug-pumping progenitor cell (escape E5) or the brain:

- Mechlorethamine was tolerated at **10.7 mg/m²**, 3.6 times the usual dose ([PubMed 41198583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41198583/)).
- A temozolomide-for-cyclophosphamide CHOP variant (THOP) produced complete responses in 14 of 14 dogs, with median survival of 433 days ([PubMed 40266942](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40266942/)). Temozolomide crosses the human blood-brain barrier, but there are no canine spinal-fluid data.
- A 19-week asparaginase protocol (LHOP) beat a 15-week version ([PubMed 40003004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40003004/)).
- Upfront steroids did not worsen outcome in multicentric DLBCL ([PubMed 40624957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40624957/)). This slightly weakens the idea that steroid pre-treatment induces multidrug resistance (escape E1).
- A single T-cell case with lymphoma infiltrating nerve roots ("neurolymphomatosis") did not respond to chemotherapy ([PubMed 40702997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40702997/)). At n=1, it is a reminder that T-cell nervous-system disease can sit in nerve roots, outside a whole-brain radiation field.

**Human drugs a US vet could lawfully reach, or soon could:**

- **Denileukin diftitox-cxdl (Lymphir) [approved, human, Aug 2024].** This is the one lawful T-cell agent the record never evaluated. Canine work shows it binds the dog IL-2 receptor and depletes canine regulatory T cells at 9-18 µg/kg IV ([PubMed 22173275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22173275/); [PubMed 16920555](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16920555/)). Human response rates are low: 16% in a 104-patient Japanese cohort, with capillary leak in 22% ([PubMed 42605024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42605024/)). It is a candidate adjunct, not a closer.
- **Tirabrutinib [human trial; FDA decision due 18 Dec 2026].** Its new-drug application for relapsed primary CNS lymphoma has a target date of **18 Dec 2026** ([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-accepts-nda-for-tirabrutinib-in-relapsed-refractory-pcnsl)). Approval would make extra-label use lawful. Its median progression-free survival of 6 months marks it as months-of-control, consistent with the record's verdict that BTK inhibitors are not closers.
- **Zanubrutinib regimens [human trial].** Zanubrutinib is the FDA-approved BTK inhibitor with the best measured spinal-fluid reach. ZANA and MSZ report complete-response rates near 90% ([PMC13064001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13064001/)), and MSZ is a direct human template for the project's XPO1 + BTK idea.
- **Other items, none of which changes a verdict:** the LOC-R01 randomised trial adding ibrutinib or lenalidomide; dose-modified methotrexate + temozolomide backbones ([PubMed 42959631](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42959631/)); and intrathecal pemetrexed, which has human data only in solid tumours.

**Biology and platform items:**

- **Canine TRBC1 and TRBC2 are identical on the cell surface [published].** A second canine study across 14 breeds found the exposed parts of TRBC1 and TRBC2 identical ([PubMed 41236719](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41236719/)). This blocks, in dogs, both the human TRBC1 CAR and the new anti-TRBC2 antibody-drug conjugate.
- **Allogeneic CAR-T cleared residual disease in humans [human trial, interim, company and press].** In ALPHA3, off-the-shelf allogeneic CAR-T (cema-cel) given as first-remission consolidation cleared minimal residual disease in 7 of 12 patients vs 2 of 12 under observation, with no CRS, neurotoxicity or graft-versus-host disease ([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/cema-cel-improves-mrd-clearance-as-first-line-consolidation-in-large-b-cell-lymphoma)).
- **First in-vivo LNP CAR-T in B-cell lymphoma [abstract only].** Lipid nanoparticles that generate CAR-T cells inside the body were tested in 4-7 lymphoma patients, with numbers not retrievable ([JCO 2026;44(16_suppl):7014](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.16_suppl.7014)).

The last two items make two record lines out of date:

- The record's "in-vivo LNP CAR = autoimmunity only" line no longer holds.
- A peer-reviewed table says HN2301 CAR-T levels return to baseline in **2-3 days**, against the record's press-sourced 7-10 days ([PMC13587011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13587011/)). The same table shows the record's "Legend LB2501" numbers are the trial LIVTO-TaVec100, NCT07002112.

## (c) Already in the record, and what this sweep did not check

The sweep re-found, and does not present as news, the following items already in the record:

- Laverdia full approval; L-CHOP + half-body radiation (progression-free 532 vs 296 days) and the Lai and Goto half-body series.
- The afucosylated anti-CD20 + CHOP study (13 of 13 complete responses); canine IL-15; gilvetmab's 0 of 15 responses.
- The 101-dog TKI meta-analysis; the mediastinal LOPP-vs-CHOP study; the autologous-vaccine systematic review.
- In-vitro verdinexor combinations; canine EZH2 and copy-number work; the 7,545-tap CSF-collection safety series.
- Glofitamab's 0.44% spinal-fluid ratio; zanubrutinib's ~43% corrected ratio; tirabrutinib's Japanese data; the C5R very-long-term follow-up.
- The CD7 CAR-T escape papers; Penn's tandem CD19/CD20 canine CAR; the BI 705564 trial; Umoja's beagle vector study.

Locations are given in each research note.

This sweep has these limits:

- **Conference supplements.** The VCS 2026, VCS 2025 and ESVONC 2026 abstract supplements could not be opened (publisher returned HTTP 403). Every number from them, including the Zoetis antibody, is search-snippet strength. These supplements are the likeliest place for unreported dog treatment data.
- **ASH.** ASH 2025 primary pages were blocked; the MSZ text came via Crossref, but the poster, the number of patients sampled and per-patient values were not found. ASH 2026 abstracts were not yet public.
- **Dog trials and outcomes.** The Bellingham paper's full text was not read. The trial registries (veterinarycancertrials.org, AVMA, NCI COTC, Morris) and the CSU, NC State, UC Davis, Cornell, Ohio State and Wisconsin pages were not fetched individually. - **The dose-tolerability question.** Human precedent for long courses of intrathecal or intraventricular chemotherapy was not searched for the 29-45-injection question. That search is what would decide the audit's single failing input, and with it whether the B-cell brain is closed or open at the project's bar.

## Conclusion

The most important correction in this sweep came from a measurement that was eleven months old, not from a new drug. It fits the project's recurring failure exactly: a number inferred from animals (0.30, below a total-tissue ratio) stood in for a quantity that had been measured in the right compartment (free drug in human spinal fluid, 0.09). Once the measured number is used, the brain closure stops being redundant. It rests on one lawful, well-established agent, intrathecal methotrexate, given more often than reported. T-cell disease, previously the hardest case, now closes inside canine precedent and no longer depends on verdinexor at all. B-cell disease, previously the easier case, now runs into a different limit: not drug reach or potency, but how many spinal-fluid injections a dog can safely receive. The project's audit now names that as the one input that fails its bar.

Two cheap actions would settle what remains, plus one tidy-up. First, search human long-course intrathecal and reservoir data to give the 29-45-injection tolerability, the audit's single failing input, a written transfer, or show that it has none. That search decides whether the B-cell brain is closed. Second, measure free verdinexor in spinal fluid from any dog already on the licensed drug. That would replace the cross-drug transfer, which the model now encodes, with the dog's own number, whichever way it falls. The tidy-up is to remove the stale "T-cell brain: OPEN" line from `routes_not_counted()`.
